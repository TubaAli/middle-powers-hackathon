# Before Geneva: demo-video draft

Draft for team review | 1 November 2026

Suggested running time: about three minutes. This is a proposed presentation length, not an organizer limit. Record the live interface with legible zoom and a calm narration. Avoid showing private tabs, chats or account details.

## Opening: the question

Show the policy explorer at https://tubaali.github.io/middle-powers-hackathon/routes/#basis=model.

Narration: “What can countries outside the US and China do to make advanced AI safer? We built Before Geneva to turn broad ideas about influence into specific questions about who can act, what evidence they need and what happens when a test fails.”

## The map: compare proposals and inspect assumptions

Select a proposal and show its factor breakdown. Keep the axis labels visible.

Narration: “The map contains 32 policy proposals. Feasibility and potential impact are explicit team judgments, informed by sources. They are not success probabilities. Missing factors are marked, and the sensitivity view lets you see how assumptions change the comparison.”

Open https://tubaali.github.io/middle-powers-hackathon/routes/stress-tests.html?route=indiaai-subsidy-evaluation-gate.

Narration: “For example, an Indian public-compute condition initially sounds like a way to stop an unsafe workload. Our evidence review found a provider's commercial-continuation path. That does not show evasion. It tells us that stopping funding and stopping service are separate claims, requiring separate tests.”

## The game: make the trade-off concrete

Open https://tubaali.github.io/middle-powers-hackathon/game/?route=indiaai-subsidy-evaluation-gate in a fresh demonstration browser profile. Begin negotiations, choose a council priority, and enter the India conversation. Show the objection and open the dossier briefly.

Narration: “The same research becomes a fictional diplomatic conversation. You have eight months, limited effort and consequential choices. You can prepare evidence, commission a bounded test, pursue a shared protocol or record why the proposal is not ready. The dossier stays one click away.”

Commission a pilot, return to the lounge, close the month, select the next council priority and revisit the route. Show the fictional complication and the repair/retest choice. Do not present a cut as uninterrupted play.

Narration: “Commissioning a pilot is not completion. You must return, inspect what went wrong and decide whether to repair and retest it. Shared protocols need a tested partner route. The ending follows completed fictional work, not assumed government consent.”

## Close: what the project establishes

Return to the stress-test view and show Japan alongside the competing Brazil and LUMI directions, or show the summary and next-test fields one at a time.

Narration: “Our current research suggests a conditional next test of Japan's purchasing and release authority, while retaining Brazil and shared LUMI as competing priorities. The next real step is a willing institutional partner, an authorised instrument and a costed controlled test. We have built a working research tool and game; we have not measured real-world safety impact.”

Show an end card with the live game, explorer and repository links. Include: “AI-assisted research, coding and dialogue. Gemini-generated scene art. Fictional game outcomes. Research snapshot: 31 October 2026.”
