# Cross-route measurement foundation

Observation and evidence cutoff: **31 October 2026**. Target checkpoint: **21–22 June 2027**. Scope: methodology and shared retrieval infrastructure for the 32 routes in `data/routes-quantitative.json`; no coordinates assigned. Companion machine-readable ledger: `measurement.json` (all identifiers prefixed `method-`).

## 1. What could the same x and y actually mean across all routes?

### Takeaway

**A common measurement protocol is feasible; a fully observed common coordinate pair is not established by the retrieved evidence.** Define x as the probability of an explicitly specified safeguard implementation event before the checkpoint, conditional on the route's current state. Define y either as outcome-specific incremental harm reduction conditional on implementation, or explicitly as reach within one fixed population of systems/exposures. Do not combine these two meanings of y.

### Cited Findings

- The current repository defines an agreement index as support share divided by one plus unresolved external handoffs, explicitly describing its penalty as a modelling choice rather than a fitted law. The dataset also mixes different conditional resource pools. This is a different estimand from on-time implementation probability or harm reduction. Source: local `DESIGN.md`, lines 5–18; `data/routes-quantitative.json`, `method` and route `quantitative` objects.
- The OECD's 200-government-AI-case analysis explicitly says its results are **not generalisable to the broader universe** of government AI efforts. It is case discovery, not an adoption denominator. [OECD report, chapter 2, p.60](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_398fa287/795de142-en.pdf).
- The 2025 OECD DGI is already reported in the 2026 outlook: 36 OECD countries plus eight accession candidates, mainly covering 2023–24; Indonesia/Thailand observations cover 2022–23. Thus an older 2023 index should not be called current. DGI is institutional maturity, not measured safeguard completion probability. [OECD, chapter 1, figure 1.2 and annex tables](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/digital-government-outlook_0496b2bc-en/full-report/digital-governments-at-a-turning-point_0491aad4.html).
- AISI distinguishes safeguard requirements, plans, evidence, reassessment and a sufficiency decision. It recommends explicit threats and assumptions; its framework is not a validated conversion from policy documents to safety effect. [AISI principles](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/principles-for-safeguard-evaluation).

### Inferences

**Measurement classes to retain in every record** (our proposed protocol):

| Class | Example | Permitted interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Observed input | Actual signature, approval date, invoice payment, accelerator inventory, logged retest | A dated observation, with scope and source |
| Derived physical/accounting measure | Elapsed days; verified covered GPU-hours / same-pool GPU-hours | Arithmetic on matched observations |
| Calibrated prediction | Out-of-time validated completion probability from comparable historical cases | A prediction with uncertainty and documented transport assumptions |
| Proxy | DGI, procurement spend, disclosed model count, number of approvals | A correlate or structural descriptor, not efficacy/probability |
| Scenario/normative choice | Threat weighting, damage valuation, frontier threshold, equal system weights | User-selected assumptions, never relabelled observations |
| Arbitrary score | LLM 1–5; min–max rescaling of unlike quantities; unfit handoff penalty | Exclude from empirical coordinate layer |

**x event contract.** Before modelling, choose whether success means (a) legal adoption, (b) enforceable operational safeguard, or (c) operational safeguard plus witnessed failure→hold→remedy→retest. Store all three timestamps; do not compare a law's adoption with another route's tested implementation. Recommended x is (b), with (c) shown separately because a real failure may not occur; a controlled rehearsal can test remedy execution. Fix whether the deadline is the start of 21 June or end of 22 June, and its timezone.

For a route already in state s for a known duration, estimate remaining-time completion conditional on that state and age, not the unconditional duration of all procurement. With cancellations and rejection as terminal competing events, use a cumulative-incidence/multistate model, not `1 − ordinary Kaplan–Meier survival` treating cancellation as harmless censoring. Match reference cases by instrument, authority, amendment/new-contract path, procedure, size, current stage and novelty. Calibrate on held-out later cohorts and, where possible, held-out jurisdictions. Record uncertainty, sample support and calibration error. Do not train such a model on the 32 uncompleted proposals and pretend they supply outcomes.

**y choices.** A genuinely common outcome estimand could be `E[harm without route − harm with route | route implemented]` over a stated period and population. This needs exposure, counterfactual incident rate, conditional severity and causal treatment effect. Different harms (privacy infringement, infrastructure disruption, model theft, catastrophic misuse) cannot be added without a normative conversion. Harm can increase; do not clip negative effects to zero merely to fit a 0–1 plot.

A narrower shared **reach** estimand can use a prespecified deduplicated population U of model-version/deployment/service units and exposures `w_i`: `sum(w_i × I[verified safeguard applies to i]) / sum(w_i)`. The same U, period, exposure unit and safeguard condition must apply to every displayed route. Equal weights mean share of systems, not share of risk. A model-wide control can map to multiple deployments only with verified propagation; finance/compute access cannot automatically inherit a developer's entire deployed reach. Use set union for coalition coverage and publish overlap. Partial but fixed inventories yield **inventory coverage**, not world coverage.

**No generic transformation fixes unlike denominators.** Percentiles preserve rank within a reference distribution, not cardinal safety meaning; logs preserve ordering, not commensurability. Monetary conversion harmonises currencies, not safety consequences. H100-equivalent conversion can harmonise an explicit hardware-performance basis, not utilisation, inference exposure or prevented harm. Shares of procurement expenditure, loan commitments and compute ownership are not interchangeable because all happen to lie in [0,1].

**Handoff penalty audit.** The exact-form search `"handoff penalty" "1/(1"` and a broader interorganisational implementation-duration search did not identify a fitted policy implementation relationship. This is a bounded search result, not proof no such literature exists. More importantly, the repository itself identifies the formula as a choice. `1/(1+N)` imposes 1, 1/2, 1/3… regardless of calendar, vetoes, parallel processing, institutional repetition, or previous agreement. A graph with k institutions has at least k−1 connecting edges only under the assumed connected-graph representation; edges are not necessarily sequential approvals. Keep named dependencies and observed timestamps. Fit a handoff effect only after obtaining event-level outcome data; otherwise show the dependency graph with x unknown. Independence-based multiplication of assent probabilities would also be an unverified model.

### Gaps

- No route-transferable calibrated relationship between these administrative/financial inputs and June 2027 safeguard implementation was identified in this retrieval. Relevant historical event logs may still be obtainable; no claim of universal data absence.
- No retrieved dataset provides a census joining all 32 routes' legally covered systems, common exposure, verified safeguard status, and counterfactual harms. This is the missing join, not a licence to invent a score.
- Exact-safeguard approval logs and operator usage logs remain route-owner retrieval tasks. The denominator, target event, harm weights, horizon and deployment boundary require explicit decisions even if all source data become available.

Actual procurement events and downloadable inventories exist. The practical first pass is to build **event histories and linked system inventories**, retaining the difference between observed transactions, plans, survey indices and discovered cases. The following sources span independent primary-source families: EU procurement/JRC, World Bank, OECD, UK government, research producers and SEC.

### Inferences

**Practical retrieval order, shared across regional researchers:**

1. Build an instrument/event table keyed by route, legal entity, procedure/award/contract IDs, instrument version, actual decision timestamp, publication timestamp and source. Obtain complete histories including non-awards/cancellations, not only winners. Distinguish actual milestone completion from planned due dates.
2. Pilot TED and one OCDS publisher to establish field semantics, duration completeness and linkage accuracy. Retrieve similar amendments and new contracts separately. Return to national sources for missing documents and implementation logs; this shared task supplies the extractor, regional owners supply actual route cases.
3. Fetch B-READY/ES/DGI/GovTech raw items and edition country lists. Report route-country matches, missing items, survey dates and sample denominators. Do not average the coalition's country indices or multiply them into a probability without validation. If microdata login requires the user, retain public aggregates and clearly mark the gap.
4. Join PSTW/ATRS to responsible agency, supplier and model-version. Add authoritative inventories from regional owners. Verify completeness against a declared administrative scope before computing adoption shares. Do not use public case-study counts as population totals.
5. Join model/cluster IDs to **actual binding rights** and scope: contract exhibits, allocation awards, specific covered services, enforceable flow-down, and verified installed/utilised resource. Inspect Epoch uncertainty notes and cited original sources. Compute HHI or top-k only within a stated, fixed resource market; neither HHI nor top-k is a safety coordinate.
6. Ask regional owners for the same minimum evaluation packet: baseline/post-change version, threat/task distribution, test sample, failures, held/released decisions, remedy/retest results, exposure denominator, follow-up period, actual harmed outcomes, and measurement independence. This is a retrieval request specification, not a contact action.

### Gaps

The B-READY ZIP was blocked locally; DGI workbook contents, GovTech asset/country rows, ES microdata and actual TED result payloads were not inspected. The Nigeria example establishes that transaction/milestone retrieval exists, not that every route-country publishes comparable data. Coverage of national procurement systems outside these demonstrations remains a regional retrieval task, not an unavailable-data conclusion.

## 3. What empirical evidence can justify an impact transformation?

### Takeaway

**There is empirical evidence for particular technical safeguards under particular evaluations. There is no retrieved basis for assigning those effect sizes to “has an evaluation clause”, “requires a DPIA”, or “funds compute”.** Keep intervention efficacy, exposure reached and policy-induced adoption separate.

### Cited Findings

- Anthropic's controlled automated evaluation used 10,000 synthetic jailbreak prompts: reported attack success fell from 86% without its classifiers to 4.4% with them. Its later public challenge nevertheless found a universal jailbreak. This is evidence of a specific technical intervention on a defined test distribution, with adversarial generalisation limitations—not of downstream harm reduction or procurement-clause efficacy. [Constitutional Classifiers, automated evaluations and 13 February update](https://www.anthropic.com/research/constitutional-classifiers).
- OECD AIM uses news-event detection and LLM classification/enrichment; OECD acknowledges it observes a subset of incidents/hazards. Its metadata is not automatically verified ground truth. [AIM methodology](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents-methodology).
- AISI recommends regular reassessment because safeguard effectiveness can change as new attacks develop. [AISI principles](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/principles-for-safeguard-evaluation).

### Inferences

For the classifier experiment alone, the absolute attack-success difference is `0.86 − 0.044 = 0.816` (81.6 percentage points); relative reduction is `1 − 0.044/0.86 ≈ 94.9%`. These are legitimate transformations **of that experiment**, not candidate route coordinates. Preserve numerator/denominator, clustering and shared prompts; confidence intervals require the actual experimental design, not invented independent-trial counts.

For a policy route, an effect chain needs evidence at each link: binding condition → actual uptake → faithfully implemented safeguard → changed failure frequency under relevant exposure → harm outcome. A clause can increase testing without inducing fixes. Incident reporting can raise observed incidents while true risk falls. A remedied incident count needs the eligible incident population, time at risk and comparable detection. Prefer an ethically acceptable phased rollout or credible quasi-experiment with concurrent controls; raw before/after changes are confounded by model upgrades, exposure and discovery.

**Concrete metric conclusion:** retain a common x target but withhold numeric x until a supported historical reference class/calibration is assembled. For y, first pursue common **verified deployment/exposure reach**, explicitly labelled reach and only for a fixed audited population. Report outcome-specific technical efficacy separately. If stakeholders require all 32 routes on one consequential-safety scale, they must specify a scenario/outcome valuation and accept model-based estimates; the retrieved primary data do not supply a normatively neutral empirical normalisation. Unknowns should stay unknown rather than be numerically downgraded.

### Gaps

This pass did not identify a causal study transporting the effect of these proposed procurement, funding, privacy or operator-governance routes to a single advanced-AI-harm outcome. It did identify useful technical evaluation evidence and discovery datasets. Next discriminating question: **can at least two different instrument families be linked to the same named deployment population and the same logged failure/remedy outcome, with a credible comparison group?** That would test a shared denominator and a policy-to-outcome relationship together.
