# Latin American route coordinate evidence

Observation and cutoff: **31 October 2026**. Geneva checkpoint: **21–22 June 2027**. Companion: `latin-america.json`, containing **60 facts (56 numeric), 24 source records, 22 grouped retrieval records and 16 explicit gaps** for all four input route IDs. These are inputs, not coordinate estimates. Existing route definitions in `data/route-research/latin-america.json` were not changed.

## What numerical implementation evidence exists?

### Takeaway

Brazil has an identifiable supervised AI cohort and actual historical enforcement results. Colombia has relevant-unit workload statistics and signed support-contract records. Chile has unit-level startup costing and a public-sector AI survey, but those do not establish operational regulator staffing. Mexico's statutory process is clear; its operational data remain the weakest part of this pass.

**Mexico — `mexico-public-sector-impact-assessment`**

- The 2025 law provides **30 business days' advance submission** and a **30-business-day recommendation window**, counted from the day after submission. These are statutory windows, not measured processing times. Recommendations are **nonbinding**; the deploying body's own release authority must adopt a hold. Article 73 exemptions can remove the assessment requirement. The retrieved consolidation lists its latest amendment as **14 November 2025**. [Arts.3,68–73, especially p.17][mxlaw]
- An indexed excerpt from **INAI's 2024 annual report**, section **8.2.1.4**, reports **four impact-assessment opinions**. This is retained as **partial-access historical evidence**: the archive download timed out, an official mirror failed DNS, and the full case table/reporting-period boundaries were not recovered. It is not four AI deployments or current SABG output. [Chamber archive][mxinai]; [SIL mirror][mxsil]
- A current organization-manual lead was found, but repeated direct retrieval failed. **Do not treat the earlier progress-message reference to a named directorate as a verified signing-chain result.** Exact receiving/signing unit, staff and actual expenditure remain unresolved. [DOF/SIDOF manual lead, partial][mxmanual]

The contract register identifies assigned units and supervisors. It does not establish exclusive AI assignment, payments, continuing performance in October or approval of this pilot. ### Inferences

- Use historical throughput and contract expiry as implementation constraints, not calibrated feasibility probabilities.
- Preserve accounting distinctions: Chile's fiscal estimates, Brazil's commitments/payments, and Colombia's signed contract values cannot be pooled as comparable pilot budgets.
- Regulator authority is not consent to an experiment; a regulated controller's cooperation is not necessarily a legal veto over lawful inspection. Mexico's voluntary internal release hold is an additional institutional decision.

### Gaps

No route yet has a fully verified combination of **dedicated technical capacity, booked deployment/inspection, exact approval chain, signed protocol and actual response-time distribution**. Mexico's historical count is partial-access. The JSON ties each unresolved critical input to law/terms, annual-execution, and native-language case/portal retrieval records; it does not label them universally unavailable.

## What coverage or safety-impact inputs exist?

### Takeaway

There are concrete inventories worth following. They can narrow the next research step without inventing affected GDP, institutional assent or national AI-system coverage.

### Cited Findings

**Brazil: a downloadable October 2026 enforcement register.** The source portal explicitly offers the workbook behind its pilot dashboard. Recalculation gives **75 distinct PF/PP processes**, including **48 active**, **31 tagged under the old AI/facial-recognition theme**, and **3 tagged under the new AI/emerging-technology theme**. There is a particularly useful cohort of **23 football-club facial-recognition cases**, worksheet rows **46–68**, all initiated **29 January 2025** and marked active in the October snapshot. These are actual process records with names and SEI references. [Workbook, `PF e PP`][brworkbook]; [portal explanation][brportal]

The sanction worksheet has **37 distinct process IDs**: compliance is **5 fulfilled**, **2 unfulfilled**, **27 pending**, **1 no sanction**, and **2 blank**. A pending or blank field is not a failed remedy. None of these general privacy statuses is a reproducible AI retest. The **27 activities** in the annual report and **31 flagged processes** in the workbook have different definitions and observation periods; they should neither be added nor silently reconciled. [Workbook, `PAS`][brworkbook]; [annual report, p.27][brannual]

For scale context, the July 2024 Meta intervention cited approximately **102 million active Facebook users in Brazil** and prescribed a conditional **BRL 50,000/day** noncompliance fine. Neither number measures training records removed or risk reduction. This is a historical preventive intervention, not a claim that its original suspension still operates. [ANPD, 2 July 2024][brmeta]

**Chile: official public-sector AI questionnaire data, not just an English-language overview.** The source is the Secretaría de Gobierno Digital questionnaire issued through **Ord.245/2025**. Its full corrected CSV has **121 response rows**, **109 columns**, and **117 distinct institutional codes**. The catalogue's August 2026 harvesting date is not the fieldwork date. [Official CSV][clsurvey]; [catalogue and original-resource links][clcatalogue]

Recomputed respondent-level inputs:

| Measure | Affirmative/count | Matching answered-item denominator |
|---|---:|---:|
| Initial or advanced AI use | **45** = 38 initial + 7 advanced | **121**; other categories: 33 evaluating, 43 non-use |
| Contracted AI development services | **14** | **45**, with 31 no and 76 skipped |
| Ethical-use decision guidelines | **14** | **45**, with 31 no |
| Assigned AI monitoring/accountability owner | **9** | **45**, with 36 no |
| Bias-mitigation mechanisms | **20** | **45**, with 25 no |
| Team members with AI skills | **36** | **45**, with 9 no |
| Ethical-AI training | **10** | **45**, with 35 no |
| Use of AI/data-science procurement directive | **5** | **16**, with 11 no and 105 skipped |
| Uploaded algorithms to public platform | **3** | **19**, with 16 no and 102 skipped |

Each row is a recount of the corresponding Spanish-named column in the [official CSV][clsurvey]; full column locators are in the JSON. These are **responses, not unique institutions**, because repeated codes were retained. They are self-reports, not legal compliance audits. Initial AI use includes proofs of concept; not every system processes personal data or meets the high-risk trigger. Conditional questionnaire blanks must not be turned into “no”.

**Parsing check:** Python's standard `csv.reader`, with doubled-quote handling, yields 121 rows each matching the 109-column header. Automatic dialect sniffing incorrectly selected quote handling and produced three shifted rows; those preliminary governance counts were rejected. Final counts above use the valid standard parse.

**Colombia: an actual deployed AI tool and a separate prototype.** SIC reports that its **RNBD policy-review AI** was integrated in September 2025, entered production in October, and reviewed approximately **400 privacy policies by early November 2025**. The stated **7,000 policies/year** is projected capacity, not completed work or a matching denominator. The same page describes **one complaint-classification prototype**, which is not a second production deployment. [Annual report, p.26][coannual]

SIC's own AI operations could be a concrete follow-up subject, but are not automatically the external-supervision route's covered population. The RNBD registers databases, not all AI deployments. [Report, pp.22,26][coannual]

### Inferences

- The most useful Brazilian next step is to select a named case from the 23-club cohort and recover its requests, responses and technical evidence.
- Chile's response-level fractions are legitimate descriptive statistics of the answering cohort. They must not become map coordinates for national feasibility or safety impact.
- Colombia's 400/7,000 is **not** an implementation or coverage fraction: the numerator and denominator represent different periods and concepts.

### Gaps

All four routes still lack a **matched eligible-system denominator and a verified intervention numerator** such as reproducible privacy failures corrected and successfully retested. There are no causal safety-effect estimates here. No national GDP is substituted for affected activity, and missing evidence is never encoded as zero.

## What could change before Geneva, and what should be retrieved next?

### Takeaway

**Chile's deferral is still a bill in the current official record, not an enacted delay.** Its next legislative and staffing events are decisive. For the other routes, the key uncertainty is an actual authorized case and measurable remedy, rather than another strategy document.

### Cited Findings

- **Chile, controlling latest retrieved status:** the Chamber record for **18623-07 / internal ID 19307** shows **Segundo trámite constitucional**, with Senate transmittal **29 October 2026**, following the **28 October** urgency entry. This is affirmative process evidence, stronger than absence of a gazette search hit. The linked transmittal PDF returned **403**, so this pass does not establish final Senate wording or its replacement date. [Official chronology][clchamber]; [unread transmittal][cloficio]
- The original fiscal costing models **1 December 2026** commencement. The **31 August 2026** fiscal analysis models a proposed move to **1 December 2027**, with staffing and operating costs shifted one year. These are distinct dated scenarios. Neither should be silently promoted to a final amended legal commencement date. [DIPRES 246, p.2][clfiscal246]; [DIPRES 179, pp.1–2][clfiscal179]
- **Brazil:** the first-semester deadline ends **30 June 2027**, later than the **21–22 June** summit. Its official programme therefore cannot by itself promise a completed pre-summit case. [MTP Annex][brmap]
- **Mexico:** 30-business-day submission and review windows require advance selection of a qualifying deployment and an explicit exemption determination. Nonbinding advice does not create a release gate. [Arts.68–73][mxlaw]
- **Colombia:** the identified support contracts end in **December 2026**, before the proposed 2027 pilot period. Renewals and actual assigned work must be checked. Circular 002 of **21 August 2024** supplies an AI-specific privacy-study duty, but not the proposed automatic benchmark sanction. [Contracts, p.1][cocontracts]; [Circular, instructions II–IV and VIII][cocircular]

### Inferences

Keep two Chile scenarios visible: an operational statutory assessment route if commencement and staffing permit, and a voluntary controller-readiness exercise if they do not. Neither means the agency/controller has consented. For every country, retain the proposed experiment as proposed until there is a named decision owner, scope, signature and execution record.

### Gaps

| Route | Three materially different approaches used for unresolved inputs | Next discriminating retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| Brazil | Adopted resolution/procedure; annual execution and accounts; downloadable register and actual Meta case | SEI requests/response dates and technical annexes for one selected case; assigned staff-hours and actual retest results |
| Mexico | Consolidated statute; INAI annual/performance reports; current organization manual and native-language assessment-case searches | Full four-opinion case table, current receiving/signing-unit confirmation and one actual federal AI deployment's assessment/exemption file |
| Chile | Statute/Chamber/gazette checks; original and deferral fiscal reports plus execution search; official AI questionnaire dataset | Read the Senate transmittal and subsequent law status; appointment/payroll documents; one deduplicated high-risk deployment with assessment and authorized pilot |
| Colombia | Original AI circular; annual workload/execution reports; native-language case searches and signed-contract register | AI-specific administrative decision with complete receipt/order/compliance dates, renewal/work orders and controller test/retest evidence |

Search details and failed access attempts are recorded as **22 grouped retrieval records** in the JSON, rather than pretending they were only 22 individual tool calls. Sixteen gaps use bounded statuses. No `not_publicly_disclosed` claim is made merely because retrieval failed. Stopping point: strong new operational data found, but additional broad searches had diminishing returns; the remaining work is identifiable case-file, appointment and technical-annex retrieval.
