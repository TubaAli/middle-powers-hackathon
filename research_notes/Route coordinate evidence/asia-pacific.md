# Asia-Pacific route coordinate evidence

Observation and evidence cutoff: **31 October 2026**. Target checkpoint: **21–22 June 2027**. Companion: [structured evidence](asia-pacific.json).

## Result

The seven routes have **33 fact records, 25 explicitly classified gaps, 29 source records and 23 retrieval records**. Repeated evidence across routes is intentional, not independent additional evidence. No coordinates, expert integer scores, implementation probabilities or causal safety-effect estimates are assigned.

The strongest additions are actual Japanese Government AI contracts, Taiwan's **implemented** compute allocation cohorts and annual uptake, Singapore's documented government test–remediate–retest workflows, and an actual Singapore AI-safety procurement award. These substantially improve historical implementation evidence. They do **not** establish consent to the proposed safeguards, complete approval denominators, matched production coverage or avoided harm.

Here **x** means evidence bearing on implementation by Geneva, not a calibrated probability. **y** means evidence bearing on consequential safety reach, not a surrogate such as money, model count or benchmark score. A coverage ratio needs the same unit, population and period in numerator and denominator. A complete approval denominator requires the actual decision path, including vetoes. Neither can be reconstructed from institution counts alone.

## 2. Japan–Korea–Singapore: common procurement profile

Japan's executed awards establish a national implementation precedent. Singapore's procurement framework assigns policy to MOF and purchasing to agencies; its **four stages** are sourcing, evaluation, award approval and contract management. Evaluator and approver are distinct. Four stages do not mean four institutions or required signatures. [MOF policy][sg-policy]; [process, updated 9 September 2026][sg-process].

A fresh official award-data retrieval found **IMD000ETT25000023**: IMDA's Singapore AI Safety Red Teaming Challenge 2026, awarded **14 November 2025** to **Terra Systems Pte. Ltd. for S$175,000**. Locator: API record **`_id=6242`**, with supplier, date and `awarded_amt`. This is an executed safety-related purchase, not a common-profile contract, deployment gate, annual laboratory budget or marginal test price. [Official award dataset][sg-awards].

SG/KR's actual bilateral experiment is stronger evidence of technical cooperation than a memorandum alone, but does not establish Japan's participation or three-country procurement recognition. [Original report][eval]. The missing pieces are the national acceptance owners, a mutual-recognition instrument, comparable eligible purchasing/deployment inventories and deduplication of shared models. Japanese contracts, Singapore policy/award data and Korean procurement retrieval are materially different paths; Korea's PPS maintenance blocked the last path. A coalition coordinate remains unjustified.

## 3. Singapore: agent data-leakage deployment gate

### Existing evaluation workload and outcomes

The original SG/KR study uses **12 scenarios × three agents × ten repetitions × two institutes = 720 attempted runs**, **360/institute**. These are attempted runs, not 720 valid scored observations: NA and termination rules matter. The protocols use non-equivalent caps of **60 steps** and **150 messages**. No elapsed throughput, annual capacity or marginal cost follows from the run count. [Scenario design pp4–10; Table II p11; Tables III–IV run totals][eval].

On the Singapore suite, the fully **safe-and-correct** counts are:

| Agent | Singapore pipeline | Korean pipeline |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 10/60 | 9/60 |
| B | 35/60 | 21/60 |
| C | 14/60 | 16/60 |

Source: [Table III p11 and Table IV p12][eval]. This joint endpoint is not leakage alone. Pipeline differences cannot be labelled treatment effects. These results identify failure and reproducibility issues; they do not estimate the risk reduction from imposing a production release gate.

### Actual government implementation precedents

GovTech's **2 October 2026** chat.gov.sg account describes **21 attack skills**, **ten agents/policy**, initial attack success **15%**, and **11.3%** after audit. Following guardrail changes, none of the previously successful attacks still succeeded. This is a concrete test–remediate–retest loop. The source does not provide the attack-count denominator, independent held-out validation, elapsed remediation time or cost. Zero remaining successes in the known-attack retest is not zero general attack risk. [GovTech case][sg-chat].

The **23 October** PSD/GovTech HR concierge case uses **four binary quality metrics**, synthetic conversations up to **ten turns**, agency-restricted tools, Litmus/Sentinel testing and owner remediation. Its phased rollout remains subject to readiness/approvals. This is operational integration evidence, not a nationwide mandatory gate. [HR case][sg-hr].

The official experiment, agency implementation accounts, and procurement-policy/award-data paths still do not identify the **binding release owner**, exact leakage threshold, actual eligible-agent register or gate-specific costs. The earlier **90,490 Pair monthly-user lead is excluded**: product-page retrieval did not recover verifiable metrics. It must not become a denominator by repetition.

**Next discriminating retrieval:** one agency's approved release checklist and delegated signatory, joined to an actual failed-test hold, corrective retest, active-system inventory and exposure counts.

## 4. Singapore–Korea–Japan: shared evaluation evidence protocol

The bilateral report supplies an executed **two-institute** precedent with **three agents**, **12 scenarios** and **720 attempts**. These are the same observations as section 3, not new additional capacity. Agent B's **35/60 versus 21/60** safe-and-correct outcomes on the same suite demonstrate why execution settings and scoring rules need explicit treatment before reciprocal acceptance. [Report design and Tables III–IV][eval].

Japan's proposed **300-question/15-hour** evaluation is a concrete recipient-side process to investigate, but it evaluates a different population and purpose. It is not evidence of Japanese acceptance of this agent-leakage protocol. [Japan plan p2][jp-plan].

Three paths—original cross-lab results, Japan's evaluation plan and national legal/procurement materials—leave the recognition instrument and complete recipient approval chain unresolved. Run counts, maximum evaluation time and a Singapore safety-event contract are incompatible cost/capacity measures; they cannot be combined into annual throughput. No source establishes the population of consequential production deployments that would be governed by mutually recognised evidence.

**Next discriminating retrieval:** a signed recognition/acceptance rule, versioned replication logs with staff/compute time, and recipient deployment IDs. Historical bilateral collaboration should increase confidence in the existence of technical capability, not be recoded as future three-country consent.

## 5. Taiwan: public-compute safety conditions

### Current instrument and alternative resource units

The **14 August 2026 FY2026 terms** award **1,000 / 2,000 / 6,000 points**. Training converts at **0.5 GPU-hour/point**, giving **500 / 1,000 / 3,000 GPU-hours**. Inference alternatives are **100 / 200 / 600 million input OR output tokens**. Awards last **three months** from notification and queues can apply. These are entitlements, not actual consumption; tokens and GPU-hours cannot be added. [Terms p3][tw-terms].

The document assigns eligibility/professional review and provisioning to **Taiwan AI Cloud**, while **ADI** convenes training review and approves; inference does not use the extra training meeting. It includes suspension/termination provisions, a **three-day inactivity cure**, **three-year** log/performance retention, and final report/demo within **30 calendar days** after expiry. [Terms pp5–6, 8–9, 13–14][tw-terms]. This supplies a real conditional-allocation mechanism, not proof that the proposed new safety condition is already authorised.

### Historical implementation that fills a previous gap

The FY2025 performance report records, through December 2025:

- **242 firm-participations**, covering **186 distinct firms**;
- **at least 266 models** and **80 firms achieving AI-service productisation** (not a measured sales total);
- a platform with **40 NVIDIA/AMD GPUs** built since 2024; expansion to **140** is planned, not completed.

These are programme uptake/output measures, not frontier-risk or FY2026 safety-conditioned coverage. The distinct-firm and participation figures must not be interchanged. [Performance report pp26,43][tw-performance].

The **9 July 2025** executive minutes give more direct evidence of implementation: first-batch **54 firms actually used compute 9 April–21 May**; a **23 June** committee approved **64** second-batch firms and borrowed **80 H100 GPUs**, with July–August access planned. First-batch actual use, second-batch approval and planned second-batch use are different events. Do not add the cohorts to the annual totals or automatically add the borrowed GPUs to the platform fleet. [Minutes p11, item10(4)][tw-minutes].

An older February 2026 assessment retains **97 applicants / 64 approvals / 88 models** and describes FY2025 **planned 163,000 GPU-hours**, **150 firm-participations** and maximum equivalent **NT$1.5m/participation**. These planned quantities are not actual utilisation or cash disbursements; the newer annual report is better for actual uptake. The 64-approval cohort is not independently reconciled to the minutes. [Assessment p17][tw-tax].

### Timing and remaining scope

The platform's final application deadline was **30 October 2026, 23:59:59**, already passed at cutoff. It lists **three training** and **six inference** review rounds, with the final inference review **planned 2 November**. This makes pending review or the next cycle a more precise intervention target than claiming open applications. The news page rendered an empty table; it does not prove no awards were published. [Platform schedule][tw-platform]; [news retrieval][tw-news].

Terms, implementation/annual reports and live application/award pages are three distinct retrieval paths. Together they materially strengthen x, but leave the FY2026 award/consumption ledger, power to amend pending awards, next-cycle terms, incremental testing cost and frontier-risk workload mix unresolved.

**Next discriminating retrieval:** approved safety conditions for a named next-cycle award, plus executor usage accounting joined to project risk and hardware-equivalent compute. Headquarters or the existence of NCHC does not establish control over all Taiwanese compute.

## 6. Singapore: purchaser-linked sandbox release gate

The ongoing AI Verify assurance sandbox reports **30 applications**, **12 geographies** and **14 industries**. These are not 30 Singapore government deployments or completed gated releases. Eligible applications can be in production or intended for production. The programme requires technical testing and a case study within **three months**, permits commercial tester selection and mentions funding without an amount. This is a target requirement, not an observed completion-time distribution or admission guarantee. [Current sandbox and FAQ][sg-sandbox].

The **February–May 2025** pilot showcases **17 deployer/tester pairs**, a separate historical cohort. It should not be added to the current 30 or counted as 17 independent approving purchasers. [Pilot][sg-pilot].

Current terms, historical pilot evidence, and agency procurement/remediation records demonstrate relevant capability. They do not supply a sandbox participant's purchaser contract with an enforceable failed-test hold. An evaluator can produce evidence without controlling launch or payment. Costs, admission time, actual completion distributions and production exposure remain partial or unknown.

**Next discriminating retrieval:** one participating purchaser's acceptance schedule with an authorised hold/retest decision, joined to dated test completion, fees and production usage.

## Retrieval discipline, limitations and handoff

- The JSON records each fact's population, period, evidence type, units, precise source locator, permissible coordinate use and limits. Publication dates are separate from event periods and observation date. `null` publication dates mean not recovered; English source titles are descriptive where needed.
- `found_partial` means useful evidence exists but the target field is incomplete. `not_found_after_search` is bounded to the listed paths, never proof of absence. Future consent/outcomes are `future_unobserved`, not zero. Access failures remain explicit in source/search records and in the reason for each affected gap.
- Critical unresolved empirical fields were pursued across at least three materially different paths as listed per gap: terms/plans, execution/experiments, and procurement/administrative/operational records. Some paths failed or were partial; this is **not** three successful independent confirmations. Future consent is not empirically discoverable merely by multiplying searches.
- Search-call status messages without result text are recorded as such and excluded as evidence. Two earlier shell-only leads—Pair metrics and an IMDA Google-agent factsheet—lack preserved exact URLs; their limits are disclosed, and neither supports a fact. Do not treat them as strong reproducible negative findings.
- Validation checked the exact seven route IDs, required fact fields, unique `ap-` evidence IDs, allowed gap statuses, all source/search references, every source URL's presence in this Markdown, and Markdown reference-link definitions. Primary-source spot checks confirmed Japan's award column/dates, the SG/KR results tables and Taiwan's annual-report/cohort figures. This is structural and selected factual validation, not proof that every unresolved retrieval path is exhausted.
- No installation, contact, spending, subagent or git operation was used for this handoff. Only the two assigned outputs were edited. The administrative status check returned HTTP403; no guide change was adopted.

**Highest-value follow-up:** obtain one actual enforceable instrument and matched operational ledger: Japan's November acceptance terms, Taiwan's next-cycle safety-conditioned award, or a Singapore purchaser's failed-test hold. For Korea, verifying the final notice is the prerequisite. More application counts or partnership announcements will not resolve those questions.
