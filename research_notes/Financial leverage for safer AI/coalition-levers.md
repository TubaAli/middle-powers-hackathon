# Middle-power financial leverage for a safer AI transition

Research cut-off and observation date: **31 October 2026**. Prepared for the Leverage Map + Coalition Builder. These are ranked hypotheses, not evidence that a coalition has consented or that a financier already possesses the proposed rights. Currency is as stated by each source; Canadian government amounts below are CAD. No aggregate “middle-power share of finance” is estimated.

## 1. What do all three Slack-linked pages establish, and what survives source checking?

### Takeaway

Together the pages suggest three separate tests: identify a scarce input; turn bargaining opportunities into durable, exercisable rights; and inspect the actual legal instrument rather than assuming that shared values or national ownership imply control. The most promising financial application is **prospective conditional capital and procurement**, with an identified signatory, decision window, verification mechanism and remedy.

Substantive source chains followed:

- **B1 → IEA Energy and AI executive summary**: IEA distinguishes local grid constraints from global energy demand and estimates that grid-related constraints could delay roughly one-fifth of planned data-centre projects absent mitigation. This is a model-based risk estimate, not an observed refusal rate. It supports examining the financed site, connection and construction schedule rather than only the investor's passport. Publication: 2025; underlying demand observation: 2024; projections: through 2035. [IEA][S1]
- **B2 → EuroHPC call**: the live call is procurement of AI compute access time from selected consortia. Published/opened **30 July 2026**. It now distinguishes **Lot 2: 3 December 2026, 17:00 Europe/Luxembourg**, and **Lot 1: 4 February 2027, 17:00 Europe/Luxembourg**. The page explains Lot 1's extension by national budget procedures and financial commitments. The announcement of the extension is undated, so archive its timing before representing it as a historically dated amendment. Do not repeat the blog's single deadline for both lots. [EuroHPC][S2]
- **B2 → Singapore MDDI speech**: on **7 October 2026**, the minister said IMDA would develop codes with industry, expected commencement in 2027, and explained that EDB negotiates/monitors economic commitments which can become licence conditions. Crucially, paragraphs 128–130 say assured access goes beyond the Bill's scope; the speech does not establish a right to foreign model weights. This is official legislative explanation, not a substitute for reviewing final statutory text and licences. [MDDI][S3]
- **B2 → Japan ministerial transcript; B3 → official AI Act translation**: the **6 October 2026** press conference announced review discussions from late October without committing to a legislative deadline. The Act's Article 10 provides for necessary financial and legislative measures; Article 12 addresses infrastructure; Article 13 guidelines; Article 16 investigation/advice. The official English Article 7 says operators “must cooperate,” so B3's paraphrase “only asked” is too loose. None of these passages establishes a state veto over SoftBank investments or a general pre-release evaluation mandate. [Transcript, updated 7 October][S4]; [Act No. 53 of 4 June 2025][S5]
- **B2 → CPTPP Chapter 14 and UK procurement guidance**: Chapter 14 excludes government procurement under Article 14.2(3), restricts compelled computing localisation under 14.13, and permits source-code provisions in commercially negotiated contracts under 14.17(3)(a). This does not exempt procurement from every other treaty obligation or resolve whether model weights are source code. The UK AI Playbook asks buyers to address supplier approach, data risks and lock-in. [Treaty text][S6]; [UK Playbook, 10 February 2025][S7]
- **B2 → US export rule**: the published **14 July 2026** rule says G42/Core42 authorisations expire **6 April 2027 absent subsequent BIS notice**. That is a US-controlled permission, not UAE control of foreign suppliers. Treat it as a dependency to diligence before financing UAE capacity, and check for superseding notices before acting. [Federal Register, supplement note 2][S8]

### Inferences

- **From all three pages:** store `holder`, `decision-maker`, `legal instrument`, `covered borrower/operator/model`, `verification`, `expiry`, `substitute` and `remedy` separately. A country total should never substitute for these fields. [B1][B1] [B2][B2] [B3][B3]
- **From B3's disaggregated governance method:** countries with light-touch general AI law may still bargain through purchases or discretionary finance; countries with strong AI law may still have independent investors and narrow public mandates. A shared regional identity is not evidence of aligned safety preferences. [B3][B3] [S5][S5] [S6][S6]
- **Proposal inspired by B3's federated-custody discussion:** pooled financing should purchase reciprocal evaluation access and geographically distributed continuity capabilities, with review and appeal, rather than make one investor a universal gatekeeper. Federation's ability to survive a legal cutoff must be tested; the page's diagram is illustrative. [B3][B3]

### Gaps

- The three pages were read, but their entire bibliographies were not independently audited. B1's materials prices, growth estimates and roughly 40% weighted share are not verified measurements in this note. B3's entire nine-country legal checklist and cultural scores were not revalidated. B2's Australian, Korean, Geneva and EU liability dates are not promoted here to verified finance deadlines.
- No source retrieved gives a general legal right for middle-power governments to redirect all domestic pension funds, insurers, banks or venture investors. No actual financing agreement or confidential investment side letter was available for the proposed covenants below.

## 2. Which countries and instruments rank highest by hold, last and use?

### Takeaway

Prioritise a small number of transactions where public authorities actually allocate money or access: **Canada's staged Cohere investment, EuroHPC procurement, Singapore capacity-linked commitments, and Japan-led infrastructure finance**. UAE/Saudi investor-operators are important counterparties, but their foreign portfolio stakes cannot be treated as automatic control of models.

### Cited Findings

Recent primary-source facts anchoring the candidates:

- **Canada:** on **29 October 2026**, government announced an **up-to-CAD1.6bn equity commitment** to Cohere, to be invested in stages with progress reporting. Announced commitments include Canadian incorporation/headquarters, additional domestic compute investment and continuing work with the Canadian AI Safety Institute. This is a planned staged investment, not proof of complete disbursement, board control or an evaluation veto. [Prime Minister's announcement][S9]
- **Canada infrastructure:** SCIP's infrastructure layer lists approximately **CAD890m over seven fiscal years from 2026–27**; applications closed **1 June 2026**. Funding is expressly notional, subject to negotiation, appropriations and executed agreements. Its geographic/governance requirements are explicit. Do not call it a currently open application window. [ISED, modified 1 June 2026][S10]
- **Japan infrastructure finance:** JBIC signed an NTT India loan **28 March 2025**, announced 31 March, up to **INR13bn JBIC / INR21.7bn total co-financing** with MUFG/Mizuho, under the Japan–US–Korea DiGi framework. A separate US data-centre loan signed **18 September 2024** was **USD250m JBIC / USD500m total** through NTT Finance Americas. These establish a real policy-finance route, not model-safety terms or current undrawn balances. [India loan][S11]; [US loan][S12]
- **Export credit/insurance precedent:** a **12 March 2025** JBIC buyer's-credit agreement for Japanese port cranes combined roughly USD30m JBIC and USD50m total lending, with NEXI insuring the private-bank loan. This is an instrument precedent outside AI, not an AI-safety case. UKEF's **2024–29** strategy separately describes transaction-level environmental/social standards and monitoring over the support period. [JBIC, 13 March][S13]; [UKEF strategy][S14]
- **Singapore investors:** the Finance Ministry stated on **4 August 2026** that Temasek makes individual commercial investment decisions independently; government holds its board accountable for overall long-term performance and governance. State ownership must not be encoded as ministerial authority over a particular AI deal. [MOF parliamentary reply][S15]
- **UAE:** AIATC announced MGX in **March 2024**, with Mubadala/G42 as founding partners and senior Abu Dhabi leadership on its board. This documents a state-linked strategic vehicle, but does not reveal portfolio voting rights. MGX's **21 July 2026** announcement reports a consortium's completed acquisition of all Aligned equity at about **USD40bn enterprise value**, plus a **USD5bn growth-capital commitment**; it describes AIP's USD30bn equity/USD100bn including debt as targets. Enterprise value, growth commitment and fundraising ambition must not be summed as fresh UAE money. [Mubadala][S16]; [MGX closing announcement][S17]
- **Saudi Arabia:** PIF's **28 October 2025** statement described a **non-binding** term sheet for Aramco to acquire a significant HUMAIN minority, with PIF retaining majority ownership, conditional on definitive agreements and approvals. It establishes the proposed control structure, not proof of closing or the current share split. [PIF][S18]
- **UK/Canada insurance:** Lloyd's identifies Canada-headquartered Armilla as an AI-liability MGA/coverholder and describes independent model evaluation, audits, warranties and liability products. This is evidence of a commercial underwriting route, not proof that catastrophic frontier-model risk is covered or that government directs underwriting. [Lloyd's][S19]
- **UK competition:** on **16 October 2026**, CMA proposed Microsoft business-software SMS designation; final decision was still pending with a **February 2027** statutory deadline. Its separate cloud work remains engagement/monitoring, with another update expected spring 2027. Do not label AWS/Microsoft cloud SMS designations as already imposed by this announcement. [CMA][S20]
- **Japan private finance:** SoftBank's **9 September 2026** disclosure announced repayment on **15 September** of its remaining USD25.9bn drawn bridge balance. The source is notice of intended repayment, not a post-payment cash confirmation. It nevertheless invalidates treating the original bridge maturity as an assured future pressure point. [SoftBank][S21]

### Gaps

- Obtain **one actual contract** before assigning an implemented-leverage label: sovereign subscription/side letter; EuroHPC tender contract; credit agreement; guarantee/insurance policy; operator lease; model licence. Needed fields include consent thresholds, assignability, termination, governing law, dispute forum, audit access and enforcement beneficiary.
- State-linked sponsor governance is not identical to a public agency. PIF/HUMAIN's current cap table, MGX/AIP consortium voting, and the independent investment duties of participating funds remain unresolved.
- No denominator establishes how difficult replacing a financier would be. No model-lab funding runway, competing term sheets, unused lender commitment or uninsured exposure is verified here. Ranking can change sharply once these are known.

## 3. How should finance decision windows translate into enforceable safety conditions?

### Takeaway

Make the first deliverable a **transaction-specific covenant schedule**, not a pledge to stop all AI finance. Choose conditions the financed entity can perform, tie them to a real discretionary payment or access decision, and ensure rights survive the initial financial bargaining moment where legally possible.

### Cited Findings

- The six requested outcomes are independent pre-release testing, shared serious-incident reporting, protected weights, essential-user continuity, chip-location verification and a lawful local stop capability. They are the blog's proposals; scope and remedies must be separately drafted. [B2][B2]
- The strongest observed openings have different legal forms: staged public equity in Canada; purchase of compute access by EuroHPC; capacity-linked licensing commitments in Singapore; policy-bank co-financing in Japan. Combining their headline amounts would obscure rather than quantify leverage. [S9][S9] [S2][S2] [S3][S3] [S11][S11]
- For a US registered offering, SEC guidance requires business, financial, risk and management disclosure plus audited statements; additional information must prevent misleading disclosure. Nonpublic draft review is also available under SEC procedures. Thus “IPO preparation,” confidential submission, public registration and completed sale must remain separate statuses. Absence of a public filing does not disprove a nonpublic draft. [Registration statement guidance][S23]; [Nonpublic review procedures][S24]

### Inferences

The following is a **proposed term-sheet checklist**, not legal advice or existing terms. Feasibility rests on the candidate's actual powers, the named counterparty and the financing instrument. The six outcomes originate in B2; the payment/acceptance logic is an inference from the observed transactions. [B2][B2] [S2][S2] [S9][S9] [S11][S11]

| Condition | Obligor and drafting point | Verification deliverable | Decision window and proportionate remedy to negotiate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-release testing | Model developer must sign or tenant must procure binding developer cooperation; specify thresholds, models/versions, evaluator confidentiality and access method. An infrastructure borrower alone cannot promise access it lacks. | Evaluator report delivered before first covered training/deployment milestone; specified treatment of unresolved critical findings. | Before subscription or tenant admission; later discretionary tranche/access conditional on completion. | Protected weights | Developer and host security annex with responsibility boundaries, credential/key custody, subcontractor obligations and version-change control. | Independent security assurance and remedial testing; audit access without unnecessarily exposing weights. | Before drawdown/commissioning, renewed at expansion or major model change; stop deployment of deficient covered workload rather than defaulting unrelated assets automatically. |
| Essential-user continuity | Named users, model licence, compute reservation, executable backup, staff/key access and update obligations; price the service. | Real failover drill, with permitted scenarios and recovery targets. | Procurement or equity/JV negotiation; service credits, substitution, escrow/step-in only where licensed and lawful. A lender's acceleration right is not an operational off-switch. |

**Hold → last → use discipline (proposal):**

1. **Hold:** ask what would actually fail or be delayed if the offer vanished. Compare replacement capital, host capacity, permit timelines and technical substitutes; do not use AUM as the answer. B1's scarce-input logic and IEA's site constraints motivate this test. [B1][B1] [S1][S1]
2. **Last:** do not rely only on covenants extinguished by repayment. Where counterparties agree and law permits, couple finance to a separately priced operating agreement, model licence, surviving audit/reporting obligation, or continuing access contract. Record sale, refinancing, insolvency and change-of-control treatment. This is proposed drafting, not a claim that existing loan rights survive repayment. [S2][S2] [S21][S21]
3. **Use:** name who can withhold an uncommitted tranche, reject an acceptance test, issue a lawful direction, enforce the agreement or authorise a waiver. Require a tested operational pathway and fund evaluator capacity. Government speeches and assurance plans are not proof of execution. [S3][S3] [S9][S9]

**Coalition design hypothesis:** start with one Canadian equity or compute agreement, one EuroHPC access contract and a Japan-financed infrastructure project. Offer a shared evaluator protocol with reciprocal recognition, incident taxonomy and transparent exceptions. Singapore can develop compatible host assurance; Gulf operators could join through defined project contracts. Avoid demanding that commercially independent investors adopt a government-wide investment boycott. Make benefits concrete: lower duplicate diligence, credible continuity, predictable acceptance and appropriate risk pricing. These benefits are hypotheses to test with counterparties, not documented institutional commitments. [S2][S2] [S3][S3] [S9][S9] [S11][S11] [S15][S15]

### Gaps

**Next discriminating question:** *For the next unpaid Canadian Cohere stage, which signed document lets which official require independent evaluation, and can Cohere replace that stage without material delay?* This simultaneously tests authority, remaining discretion and substitutability. If that window has closed, obtain the EuroHPC tender pack and identify which assurance provisions remain lawfully adjustable before award.
