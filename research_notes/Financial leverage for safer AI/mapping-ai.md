# Mapping AI: financial leverage for a safer AI transition

## 1. What does the funding dataset actually measure?

### Takeaway

Mapping AI is useful as a sourced relationship-discovery tool, but its funding export is neither a comprehensive commercial-capital database nor solely a policy-philanthropy dataset. Crucially, its displayed funding concentration measures **counts of recorded funding relationships, not dollars, annual revenue, or budget dependence**. Do not transfer its percentages into the Coalition Builder as capital shares.

### Cited Findings

All web observations below were retrieved **2026-10-31**. Dates embedded in data are distinguished from retrieval dates. Research used direct HTTPS retrieval of the live site, its deployed JavaScript, public JSON and upstream source code; it did not rely on a rendered screenshot. The observations describe the retrieved snapshots, not an independently reconciled October 2026 financial census.

- **Scope:** the site describes itself as mapping the US AI policy landscape. Its funding section explicitly calls the data a sample, and describes funders as VCs, philanthropies, government agencies and technology companies. The live funding export has **1,176 records**, **570 with amounts**, **749 with dates**, and **1,176 with citations**, with `generated_at: 2026-05-03T23:49:24.482Z`. Thus 606 records lack amounts and 427 lack dates. The presence of a citation does not mean that the financial interpretation has been independently verified. [Live funding export](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json); [Insights](https://mapping-ai.org/insights#funding).
- **Mixed population:** recomputation from the export's recipient categories gives Academic 312 records, AI Safety/Alignment 179, Frontier Lab 120, Unknown 113, Deployers & Platforms 98, Think Tank/Policy Org 90, VC/Capital/Philanthropy 52, and Infrastructure & Compute 36, alongside smaller categories. These are record counts, not numbers of unique organizations or money shares. It is incorrect to describe the entire export as policy philanthropy. [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json).
- **Sankey unit:** the deployed interface specifies that ribbon width is the number of investments. Exporter `flows` groups by funder and recipient category, increments `count` once per record and excludes flows with fewer than two records. Although `total_usd` is also exported, it is not the displayed width's stated unit. [Deployed insights implementation](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/insights-DZVjZHgf.js); [Exporter](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/scripts/export-funding-data.js).
- **Concentration formula, verified in deployed code:** function `mt` groups records by recipient, then funder, and computes each funder's `share = count / total recipient records`. No amount enters the calculation. It filters recipients to **Think Tank/Policy Org and Ethics/Bias/Rights**, excluding AI Safety/Alignment from this particular plot, and requires at least two distinct funders. Red means **strictly greater than 0.6**, yellow greater than 0.4 through 0.6, green at most 0.4. It selects at most one high-concentration, four moderate and four low examples. The prose's suggestion that a displayed percentage measures how much funding comes from a source is therefore stronger than the implemented measure warrants. [Deployed code, `function mt`](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/insights-DZVjZHgf.js); [Readable component](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/src/insights/funding/FundingConcentration.tsx).
- **Incomplete financial typing:** public detailed records contain funder, recipient, amount_usd, year, citation, source_url, source_title and categories. They omit financial instrument, payment/completion status, start/end dates, confidence, amount context, transaction ID and legal rights. The exporter reads start/end dates but publishes only the start year in detailed edges; it sums all non-null amounts without a common financial-period filter. Its SQL selects promoted `edge_discovery` records and does not join the separate `edge_evidence` table. [Exporter](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/scripts/export-funding-data.js).
- **Discovery is bounded web extraction:** the published discovery script searches approved people/organizations, requests five results with up to 2,000 characters of text each, and uses Claude extraction. Its definition includes commercial AI labs, tech companies, semiconductor manufacturing, AI-policy organizations, university research and government programs. It requests amount context and confidence but these are absent from the public financial edges. Existing production relationships are enriched into `edge_evidence`; new relationships enter `edge_discovery`. This matters because the cited exporter reads only the latter. These are inspected implementation facts, not proof that every live row was generated by this exact current version. [Discovery script](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/scripts/edge-enrichment/discover-funding.js); [Exporter](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/scripts/export-funding-data.js).
- **Duplicates and amount attribution need review:** the live export has two Sequoia → Ineffable Intelligence records, one amountless and one assigning $1.1 billion to the edge even though the quoted Bloomberg text describes a round led jointly by Sequoia and Lightspeed. This establishes an internal attribution problem to investigate, not Sequoia's individual contribution. The discovery script consolidates by names and amount, not a canonical financing-round identifier, so different amounts or null-versus-known amounts can survive as separate records. [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json); [Discovery script](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/scripts/edge-enrichment/discover-funding.js).
- **Commitments and procurement are mixed in:** the dataset includes Meta → CoreWeave, $21 billion, year 2027, with a quotation describing dedicated cloud capacity over 2027–2032; SoftBank → OpenAI, $40 billion, undated, with a quotation describing early talks for a round; and Alphabet → Anthropic, $40 billion, year 2026, with a quotation mentioning performance milestones. These examples are **claims recorded by Mapping AI**, not independently verified deal facts in this note. Their own citations make clear why summing them as paid-in equity would be unsound. 2027 is future relative to the retrieval date. [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json).

### Inferences

- Treat the export as a **lead list with evidence snippets**. Count-based concentration is primarily concentration in observed records. Multiple citations or grants from a transparent donor can increase that donor's apparent importance relative to an opaque donor with a larger contribution.
- A one-funder organization can be highly financially dependent but is excluded from the concentration chart. The chart is neither an exhaustive fragility ranking nor a loss-of-funding simulation.
- Proposed correction to the design interpretation: retain the Sankey and counterfactual interaction pattern, but calculate supply dependence from independently sourced physical shares or properly reconciled financial exposures. Do not label relationship-count ratios “funding share.”

### Gaps

- No complete budget denominators, common accounting periods, contract terms, disbursement series, maturity schedule or normalized deal identifiers were found in the inspected public export. No aggregate financial amount or national capital share is defensible from this inspection.
- Underlying news stories quoted in the commercial examples were not individually opened and verified here. Their status is explicitly dataset-recorded evidence, not corroborated transaction evidence.
- Neither the May financial snapshot nor the June map snapshot establishes completeness as of October 31.

## 2. Who are the kingmakers, and can middle powers use these insights?

### Takeaway

“Kingmaker” means reach across a selected policy-organization sample. Coefficient Giving leads that measure, followed by Survival and Flourishing Fund, Future of Life Institute and Jaan Tallinn. This is useful for identifying potential research-funding partners and diversification needs, but does not establish control over frontier-lab capital or a country's coercive leverage.

### Cited Findings

- **Exact kingmaker definition:** deployed function `_t` filters recipient categories to Think Tank/Policy Org, AI Safety/Alignment and Ethics/Bias/Rights, counts unique recipient names per funder, requires two or more recipient organizations, sorts descending and shows the first 15. No dollar amount or graph-centrality algorithm is used. [Deployed implementation](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/insights-DZVjZHgf.js); [Readable component](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/src/insights/funding/PolicyKingmakers.tsx).
- **Recomputed ranking, May snapshot retrieved October 31:** the selected universe contains 114 unique recipient names. Leading funders below are measured by unique selected recipients, followed by number of records in parentheses: Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy) **26 (33)**; Survival and Flourishing Fund **13 (15)**; Future of Life Institute **10 (11)**; Jaan Tallinn **10 (11)**; AI Safety Tactical Opportunities Fund **8 (8)**; Good Ventures **7 (7)**; William and Flora Hewlett Foundation **5 (5)**; Manifund **5 (5)**; Ford Foundation **4 (5)**; Long-Term Future Fund **4 (4)**; Future Fund **4 (4)**; ARIA **4 (4)**. Ties retain input order. These are reproducible calculations from the export using the deployed category filter. [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json); [Calculation definition](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/insights-DZVjZHgf.js).
- **Avoid double-counting reach:** the kingmaker component calculates a top-five coverage-like percentage by summing each of the five funders' recipient counts and dividing by the recipient universe. Recipients may overlap. It is not a deduplicated union share. Likewise the first funder summary in the export shows Coefficient Giving's 60 unique recipients across **all** categories, which must not be confused with its 26 policy recipients. [Deployed implementation, `function _t`](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/insights-DZVjZHgf.js); [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json).
- **Funder categories are coarse:** the export puts foundations, venture investors and sovereign investors in the shared VC/Capital/Philanthropy category. This cannot by itself distinguish private philanthropy, return-seeking equity and state investment authority. [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json).
- **Primary-source spot-check and counterexample to simple control stories:** the export records a Coefficient Giving → Epoch AI $8.5 million grant dated April 2025 and links Epoch's funding page. The live page now redirects to Transparency. It confirms Coefficient Giving among donors, distinguishes donations from paid collaborations, says only donations of $70,000 or more are listed, and says engagements under $30,000 may be omitted. It lists government and commercial collaborators separately and states that Epoch does not lobby or advocate specific AI policy positions. The retrieved page text did not expose the historical $8.5 million entry, so that exact amount remains uncorroborated here. [Mapping record](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json); [Epoch primary disclosure](https://epoch.ai/about/transparency).
- **Correlation is not causation:** the insights prose itself acknowledges that funders may select aligned organizations rather than cause their positions. Its funding-model/stance chart uses organizations with available stance scores and groups having at least five observations; the live methodology describes ordinal, partly inferred belief scoring. A cross-sectional association does not establish funder instructions, institutional consent or willingness to join a coalition. [Insights implementation](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/insights-DZVjZHgf.js); [Methodology](https://mapping-ai.org/methodology).

### Inferences

**Useful applications for the Coalition Builder (proposals, not observed powers):**

1. **Independent safety-capacity financing:** use policy-funder reach to identify donors and public agencies worth investigating for co-financed evaluation infrastructure, incident research and technical expertise. Use recipient accounts to test actual dependence before proposing replacement finance.
2. **Bargaining-power discovery:** connect commercial funders to primary transaction documents, then ask who can actually amend a future commitment, renew a facility, vote on a financing or impose procurement conditions. The relevant holder might be a ministry, fund board, lender or corporate investment committee. Its location alone does not identify the decision authority.
3. **Coalition coordination:** identify complementary types of support—public grant funding, evaluation facilities, procurement demand and investor conditions—rather than adding heterogeneous exposures into one percentage.
4. **Specific safety asks:** possible negotiated conditions include independent pre-release evaluation access, incident reporting, weight-security obligations and continuity provisions. These are proposals requiring a relevant instrument, negotiation window and enforceability evidence; Mapping AI does not establish those prerequisites.

**Circular-financing use:** a directed graph can suggest loops worth investigating, such as a supplier investing in a lab while the lab purchases infrastructure. But a financial loop requires separately verified edges for equity, debt, cloud commitments and actual cash payments, with dates and counterparties. Equity entering a lab and subsequent procurement spending are different transactions, not two independent injections of external capital. A loop does not prove fictitious revenue or fraud. The public export's undifferentiated “funder” edges cannot establish net external financing, circularity magnitude, refinancing fragility or the consequences of withdrawing one country's capital.

For each proposed leverage path require: **holder → decision authority → instrument/right → decision date → substitutability → coalition incentive → enforceable safety demand**. These are recommended research fields, not fields supplied by Mapping AI.

### Gaps

- No evidence in the inspected material establishes that any named donor, firm, public fund or country would consent to a common safety demand.
- The graph does not supply beneficial ownership, voting rights, undrawn commitments, refinancing dates, collateral, jurisdiction or investment mandates. These are essential for distinguishing a prominent node from an actionable lever.
- **Next discriminating question:** for one proposed middle-power-linked financing or procurement commitment, which named institution has a still-open decision right, what contractual condition could it attach, and how readily could the recipient replace it? Establish one such path from primary documents before scaling the map.

## 3. What can be reused, and what are the coverage and evidence limits?

### Takeaway

The public JSON and source code make the approach inspectable and reproducible. Its evidence-first interaction design is reusable as an idea; bulk code/data reuse permissions should not be assumed from the site's “open source” language because the inspected README gives a copyright notice rather than an explicit permissive license.

### Cited Findings

- **Current retrieved map size:** `data/map-data.json`, generated **2026-06-22T22:44:00.285Z**, contains 824 people, 976 organizations and 168 resources (**1,968 entities**), plus 2,946 relationships and a separate 21-row person_organizations array. This is distinct from the funding export's May date and 1,176 records. The design document's approximate 1,800 entities should not be presented as a current exact count. [Live map data](https://mapping-ai.org/data/map-data.json); [Map](https://mapping-ai.org/map).
- **US focus, not US exclusivity:** the stated research purpose is US governance, but the export includes ARIA, Canadian public agencies, European Commission and German funding entities, among others. Therefore “US-centered and not designed to establish middle-power supply-chain leverage” is accurate; “no non-US entities” would be false. [About](https://mapping-ai.org/about); [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json).
- **Methodology publication:** live methodology labels itself **June 2026**. It describes curated seeds, public submissions and LLM-assisted extraction; explicit versus inferred positions; source-linked claims; manual approval; automated adversarial verification; and community corrections. Seeds include AI Safety Map and TIME 100 AI 2025. These procedures explain the intended provenance workflow but are not an independent audit of every record. [Methodology](https://mapping-ai.org/methodology); [Deployed methodology text](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/methodology-CWS-szoN.js).
- **Claim confidence is not universal financial evidence:** the methodology's high/medium/low confidence descriptions concern extracted claims, including policy beliefs. The public financial edge schema has no confidence field, despite its discovery script requesting one. Thus the design's statement that every relationship has a quote, URL and confidence should be qualified when applied specifically to this export. [Methodology](https://mapping-ai.org/methodology); [Exporter](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/scripts/export-funding-data.js).
- **Reuse access, verified:** public endpoints expose the financial export and map skeleton; the insights client also requests `/data/map-detail.json`. Public GitHub includes exporter, discovery pipeline and individual funding components. README instructs developers without database credentials to download production snapshots. This proves technical availability, not completeness or blanket redistribution permission. [Dataset](https://mapping-ai.org/funding-data.json); [Client implementation](https://mapping-ai.org/assets/insights-DZVjZHgf.js); [README](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/README.md).
- **Licensing gap:** the inspected README License section says the project is maintained by the working group and content is © 2026 the respective contributors. A recursive repository-tree inspection found no path matching a license filename. Meanwhile the site calls its codebase open source. Record this discrepancy and clarify applicable code/data terms before substantial copying or republication; this note makes no legal determination. [README](https://github.com/MappingAI/mapping-ai/blob/main/README.md); [Repository tree](https://api.github.com/repos/MappingAI/mapping-ai/git/trees/main?recursive=1); [About](https://mapping-ai.org/about).
- **API maturity:** June methodology lists Open API/MCP as future work and temporal-edge data as planned. Public static files exist, but this inspection did not establish a supported versioned public API or historical revision guarantee. [Methodology](https://mapping-ai.org/methodology).

### Inferences

- Good design elements to adopt independently: click-through evidence, explicit uncertainty, source and observation dates, relationship-type filters, top-counterparty dependence, and inspectable counterfactual assumptions.
- For financial use add instrument typing; investor-specific amount versus total round size; original currency and conversion date; announcement, signature and disbursement dates; conditionality; annual versus cumulative values; deal IDs; source IDs; revision dates; confidence; and completeness flags. Represent unknown amounts as unknown, not zero exposure.
- Separate **policy funding**, **commercial financing**, **commercial procurement**, and **state-controllable rights** in both data and interface. Otherwise a visually compelling combined graph can falsely imply shared measurement units and government control.

### Gaps

- No statistical sampling frame or population denominator was found. Missing nodes cannot be interpreted as absent funding relationships, and degree/rank cannot be converted into market share.
- Dollar entries and relationship classifications require a larger primary-source audit before they support financial concentration claims. The Epoch check demonstrates a useful disclosure source and a limitation, not validation of the full export.
- GitHub `main` and deployed hashed assets may represent different revisions. Calculations above rely on the actual live JSON and deployed implementation; source scripts explain architecture but should not be assumed byte-identical to the process that produced May's snapshot.
- No external messages were sent and no reuse permission was requested. Only this note was created; no git operation was performed.
