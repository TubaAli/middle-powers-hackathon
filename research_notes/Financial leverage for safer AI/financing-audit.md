## 1. What do the filed governance instruments actually establish about ownership and control?

### Takeaway

Anthropic's S-1 exhibits verify a dual-lock control structure in which large investors hold economic exposure but not operational control. Pre-IPO, the Long-Term Benefit Trust (LTBT) elects a majority of the board (4 of 7 since February 11, 2026). Post-IPO, the LTBT continues to elect "the smallest number of directors that constitutes a majority" of the board, and a single Class F share held by Anthropic Founders, LLC carries ~50.1% of all stockholder votes. Amazon's and Broadcom's/Google's conversion and purchase instruments settle into non-voting N-Class stock. Safety control is institutionally separated from investor economics.

**Post-IPO charter (EX-3.3, form to be effective at the offering).** [Exhibit 3.3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit33-sx1.htm)

- Five classes (Art. IV): Participating Common (300bn Class A + 100bn Class N authorized), **Class E Common (30bn authorized)**, **Class F Common (1 share)**, **Class T Common (1 share)**, Preferred (30bn authorized).
- **Class F formula (Art. V §5.6(d))**: Applicable Class F Common Stock Vote = (0.501 × A) ÷ (0.499 × B), where A = all other votes entitled to vote generally and B = Class F shares outstanding (always 1). **The single Founder LLC share therefore always holds ~50.1% of total voting power.** Class F sunsets at the earlier of its redemption/absence or **450 days after a "Membership Triggering Event"** (Founder LLC below three Qualifying Members for 90 consecutive days) (§5.1(k), 5.1(x)).
- Class E is employee stock: no dividends; auto-redeemed for no consideration on death/incapacity/termination for cause/going to a "Direct Competitor"; half forfeits on each anniversary of leaving; redeemable in whole by 80% of the Whole Board + Class F holder + Class T holder (§5.3(b)). Founder-LLC members and Trust trustees cannot vote their Class E shares (§5.6(a)(iii)).
- Class T (the Trust): redeemed only by 80% Whole Board + Class T holder or the "Trust Amendment Threshold"; protected against charter changes adverse to it **and against any amendment or agreement that would materially alter the board's DGCL powers, including "amending the Corporation's specified public benefit or modifying, amending or eliminating the Corporation's status as a public benefit corporation"** (§5.5(d)(ii)).
- **Board (Art. VII)**: prior to the Class T Sunset, the Class T holder "shall be entitled to elect the smallest number of directors that constitutes a majority of the total number of authorized directors" (§7.1(b)); the balance are "At-Large Directors" elected by the Electing Holders (§7.1(c)). One Class T Director is the "Specified Class T Director", whose nomination/removal is subject to a three-person **Nominating Committee (one Class T Director + two At-Large Directors)** and the "Requisite Class E Vote" until the Class F Sunset (§7.1(d)). Board size is fixed by 80% of the Whole Board (§7.1(a)); quorum requires at least one At-Large Director (§8.7); special meetings are restricted and written consent is curtailed (§8.4, §8.5); no cumulative voting (§8.6); DGCL §203 opted out until both sunsets (Art. X); core articles amendable by majority (pre-F-sunset) or 66⅔% (post) of voting power (Art. XI) — with the Class F share always a blocking position until its sunset.
- Class N has no votes and automatically converts to Class A on a "Market Transfer" or "Private Transfer" (≤9.99% beneficial ownership) (§5.2(b)) — investor economics arrive as non-voting shares until sold.
- Class E/F/T liquidation receipts are capped at par value (§5.7); the control instruments are economically near-worthless by design.

**Trust governance (EX-99.1, Amended and Restated Anthropic Long-Term Benefit Trust Agreement; original June 29, 2023).** [Exhibit 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit991-sx1.htm)

- The company "irrevocably issued and conveyed to the Trustees all of the Company's authorized shares of Class T Common Stock"; the Trust is a Delaware **noncharitable purpose trust** (12 Del. C. §3556) with **no beneficiaries** (§1.1, §1.3).
- Purpose (§1.3): to ensure "that the Company is managed in a manner that responsibly balances the pecuniary interests of the Company's stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by the Company's conduct, and the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity."
- **Five authorized Voting Trustees** (natural persons), self-perpetuating (majority appoints; reappointment by majority of the others; if none in office, 80% of the Whole Board appoints), with Nominating Committee notice/wait periods (§2.2–2.4). Signature pages name **Ben S. Bernanke, Richard Fontaine, and Neil "Buddy" Shah** as Voting Trustees (three shown; five authorized) and **The Bryn Mawr Trust Company of Delaware** as Administrative Trustee with no voting or enforcement powers (§2.9).
- Powers are exercised by majority of Voting Trustees, but **"Specified Matters" require 80% of Voting Trustees** or a "Voting Request" under the Stockholders' Agreement (§2.1(f)). Specified Matters map to the Class T/Class E/Class F protective provisions of the charter (§1.4(s)).
- Conflict rules (§2.5): a Voting Trustee may not hold material pecuniary interests in the company, a material competitor, or a **material contractual counterparty** of the company without disclosure, Nominating Committee approval, and majority approval of unconflicted trustees — an anti-capture rule directly relevant to any investor coalition strategy. Compensation is fixed cash, not profit-contingent (§2.6).
- Enforcement is exclusive to: a Voting Trustee, the Company, the Class F holder, or a person/group **beneficially owning ≥10% of the voting power of the Non-Trust Voting Shares continuously for ≥1 year** ("Enforcer"); court removal of a trustee requires serious misconduct (§2.7(b), §5.1). Amendment/termination requires 80% of Voting Trustees + 80% of the Whole Board, or staged stockholder votes, or 85% of Class A after the Class F sunset (§4.3); the Trust terminates if it ceases to hold Class T shares (§4.4). The company guarantees the Trust's indemnification obligations (§5.4(i)).

### Inferences

- **Operational safety control vs economic exposure is now instrument-verified, not inferred.** The LTBT holds the board-majority instrument (all Class T shares; 4 of 7 seats since Feb 11, 2026 pre-IPO; smallest board majority post-IPO) and veto rights over changes to the public benefit/PBC status and board powers; the Founder LLC holds 50.1% of stockholder votes through a single Class F share. Amazon's Series G-2 and its $20bn facility (settling into N-Class/Public Stock), and Broadcom/Google note conversions (N-Class) are economic instruments. No investor-held instrument read in this audit grants a board seat, veto, or release-condition right over Anthropic's safety conduct.
- The voting architecture (non-voting series for strategics; N-Class conversions; Class E sunset/redemption; Daffy shares voted by CEO proxy) is constructed to keep mission/founder control independent of capital raised. Middle-power-linked investors (e.g., GIC via Greenview Investment Pte. Ltd. under the IRA) hold contractual investor rights, not control.
- The charter's fixed date "from and after February 11, 2026" for the seven-member board implies the Final Phase-In Date already occurred by then; since Final Phase-In is defined as the earlier of May 24, 2027 or eight months after a board determination of $6bn "Total Funds Raised", the drafters appear to have fixed February 11, 2026 after such a determination — inference, not a filed statement of when the determination was made.

## 2. What are the cloud-purchase and vendor-financing obligations, at contract level?

### Takeaway

The S-1 exhibits do not contain the headline AWS "$100bn/10-year" or Azure "$30bn" cloud purchase agreements, but they do contain the TPU-programme financing stack: Amazon's $20bn stock/convertible-note facility (EX-4.3), Broadcom's secured note purchase agreement with maturities tiered at $24bn/$32bn thresholds (EX-4.4), Google's quarterly-draw note purchase agreement (EX-4.5), Broadcom's master equipment lease to Anthropic's wholly-owned AI Infrastructure OpCo II, LLC (EX-10.11), and a Morgan Stanley-led syndicated revolving credit agreement dated September 4, 2026 (EX-10.10). Vendor financing is the concrete, decision-by-decision channel through Anthropic's compute buildout.

### Cited Findings

**Amazon (EX-4.3) — "2026 Stock Purchase and Convertible Note Facility Agreement", April 20, 2026, Anthropic PBC × Amazon Web Services, Inc.** [Exhibit 4.3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit43-sx1.htm)

- AWS purchases a redacted number of **Series G-2 Preferred Stock** shares "in connection with, and as a part of, the Company's Series G Financing" (recitals) — verified attribution of AWS to Series G-2, a **Non-Voting Preferred** series under the charter (EX-3.1 Art. IV(B)5).
- Beyond the equity, AWS funds an on-demand facility: on each "Draw Request" by the company, AWS purchases **convertible promissory notes** (convertible into capital stock) and/or **"Public Shares" — shares of N-Class Common Stock ("Public Stock")** at the "Public Stock Price". **"the aggregate dollar amount to be funded by Purchaser … shall not exceed $20,000,000,000 (the 'Aggregate Facility Amount')"**.
- Notes carry a "Conversion Price" equal to the "Public Stock Price"; there is a "Quarterly Conversion" mechanism reducing the outstanding "Balance Amount", a "Maturity Date Election" to convert, and an "Additional Maturity Conversion" at/after maturity. A "Financing Option" ties certain treatment to future equity financings (expressly excluding the Series G financing).

- Maturity tiers: notes up to $24,000,000,000 outstanding mature five years from the "Draw Period Effective Time"; amounts in excess of $24,000,000,000 but not exceeding $32,000,000,000 mature at sixty-three months; amounts in excess of $32,000,000,000 mature at fifty-seven months. - Security: a "Guarantee and Collateral Agreement" grants a security interest in "Collateral" that is "junior in priority only to security interests securing the obligations of the Guarantors" in respect of certain permitted facilities — connecting this obligation to the OpCo financing structure (see EX-10.10/EX-10.11).
- **Broadcom Registration Rights Agreement, June 8, 2026 (EX-4.6)** grants shelf/demand/company registration rights over shares Broadcom may acquire. [Exhibit 4.6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit46-sx1.htm)

### Inferences

- The "circular financing" pattern is verified at instrument level for the TPU programme: Google supplies TPU8i equipment; Broadcom leases it to an Anthropic-owned OpCo and also lends to Anthropic on a secured, 9%, tiered-maturity basis; Google lends on a quarterly-draw basis; a 17-institution bank syndicate provides liquidity. Strategic vendors are simultaneously creditors, lessors and (via conversion into N-Class) future non-voting shareholders.
- Each quarterly Google closing, each Amazon Draw Request, and each Broadcom tranche/lease order form is a discrete re-commitment decision — the realistic "window" for conditions, in contrast to the executed multi-year agreements.
- The Redacted amounts notwithstanding, the Broadcom maturity tiers imply the parties contemplated note balances **above $32bn**; the tiering (shorter maturity at higher balances) suggests lenders shortening tenor as exposure grows. This is design evidence, not a funded amount.

### Gaps

- **The AWS "more than $100bn over 10 years" and Azure "$30bn" purchase agreements were not found among the exhibits read**; they are not EX-4.x (which are Amazon/Broadcom/Google investment-and-notes agreements) and not among the EX-10s identified. They may be described in the unread prospectus body or not filed. Contract-level verification remains open.
- Facility sizes and the Google note interest rate/cap, the Broadcom cap, and the revolver commitments are redacted in the filed versions. Draw conditions precedent for Google's Quarterly Closing Dates were not extracted.
- The "Atlas Credit Agreement" lender identity, OpCo I structure, and the relationship between the OpCo security package and the Broadcom NPA collateral were not fully traced.
- Whether any cloud/notes agreement contains safety-linked conditions or covenants: none were observed in the excerpts read; absence in excerpts is not proof of absence in unread portions.

## 5. What becomes a finance edge in the Leverage Map + Coalition Builder?

### Takeaway

The leverage-relevant objects are now specific contracts with named counterparties and known thresholds: the syndicated revolver (Required Lenders >50% of commitments — named middle-power banks included), Google's quarterly closings, Amazon's draw requests, Broadcom's tranche order forms and secured notes, and the OpCo-level lease/credit structure. The LTBT and Founder LLC are not investor-targetable: they are the mission/founder lock, and the trust's conflict rules and self-perpetuating trustees make capture by any investor coalition structurally difficult and reputationally self-defeating.

### Cited Findings

- **Revolver amendment path**: amendments/consents run through "Required Lenders" holding more than 50% of Incremental Term Loans and Revolving Facility Commitments (EX-10.10), with a Financial Covenant keyed to "Liquidity" (Section 6.12) and an affiliate-transactions covenant (Section 6.10). ### Inferences

- **Highest-value coalition targets**: the middle-power banks in the revolver syndicate (Mizuho, MUFG, SMBC, BMO, TD, RBC, BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole, UBS, Deutsche Bank) for covenant/reporting conditions on future amendments, refinancings or incremental commitments — a proposal channel that already exists contractually, unlike investor board pressure, which the structure forecloses.
- **Highest-value future-labor targets**: the conditions precedent to Google's Quarterly Closing Dates, the Amazon Draw Request terms, and the Atlas Credit Agreement — all currently redacted/unlocated; each is a discrete, dated re-commitment where safety-linked reporting or conditions could in principle be negotiated on new money.
- **What NOT to model**: any edge implying investor control of safety decisions at Anthropic. The instruments show the opposite design: strategics get non-voting economics; the LTBT (with 80%-trustee thresholds for Specified Matters and anti-conflict rules) and the Founder LLC (50.1% Class F) hold control. A coalition asking the LTBT to adopt enforceable safety-demand language is asking the already-mission-locked body; asking syndicate banks and vendors on new tranches is asking parties with actual money-at-risk decisions.
- **Timing**: the auto-conversion of the Preferred on this IPO (EX-3.1 Art. IV(B)4(b)) dissolves the "Preferred Majority" protective provisions — investor-side contractual leverage (veto over liquidation, dividends, charter changes, senior stock) is at its maximum now and sunsets at pricing; post-IPO it collapses into non-voting N-Class. Any map with timestamps should model this as a one-way door closing at the offering.
