## Critical distinction (mandated check)

**1. No empirical autonomous-write time series. Confirmed, and the note maintains it correctly.**
None of the reopened sources measures autonomous agent *write* behaviour. S12 measures crawler
(read) request ratios; S1 measures AI-labelled token shares in crawls without any attribution of
text to autonomous agents versus human-directed tools. The note states the gap itself (Q2 takeaway,
md lines 31, 44–45; Q7 gaps line 180; causal-limits #5; json `causalLimits` line 257 and prohibited
claim "autonomous agents are flooding the web"), and nothing in the verified text licenses reading
crawl ratios or token shares as agent write volume. No correction needed.

**2. No calibrated extrapolation to June 2027. Maintained as the note's own discipline — but the
stated justification is factually wrong and must be re-grounded.** The note's refusal is real and
consistent (seeds labelled "scenario, not forecast"; `alpha/beta/lambda` "unfitted"; prohibited
outputs include any prevalence number for "the Internet"), and it nowhere imports S1's 42.3%/50.7%
forecast. However, the *reasons* it gives are rows 9 and 10 above: "two points cannot identify a
trend" and "the detector's error rate is unpublished" — both false about the retrieved source, and
the existence of S1's own drift forecast (row 12) is not disclosed. The distinction survives; the
argument does not. Corrected posture: "S1 publishes a 62-month vendor-measured series and its own
drift forecast (42.3% end-2027, 50.7% end-2028). We carry scenario bands rather than adopting that
forecast because the detector is vendor-operated and unreplicated, the population is
English-only and filter-distorted, and prevalence is endogenous to crawling policy — and because
nothing in it is a calibrated extrapolation of *autonomous agent writes*, which remain unmeasured."

## Does the note overgeneralize model collapse? No.

- The Q4 takeaway conditions collapse on protocol (replacement degrades; accumulation bounds
  error; fixed budget degrades partially), which matches the verified S4 (and the correction's
  β=γ=0 confirmation that the mathematical setting is pure replacement), and the note explicitly
  refuses the "inevitable" and "solved" readings (md line 75).
- The Gaussian divergence result is correctly scoped as "the Gaussian case is proved" (md line 72),
  not as a general law; Theorem 3.1 verbatim supports exactly that scoping.
- Scale honesty holds: the note flags that all collapse experiments are small-model (OPT-125m to 8B,
  few generations, lab corpora — md lines 82–83, 174) and that no frontier-scale or field
  degradation evidence exists. S1's pretraining models are 19.9M–973M parameters (App. B), which the
  note never overstates.
- The Nature abstract's own strongest line ("indiscriminate use... causes irreversible defects") is
  *not* adopted by the note; the note is more careful than its source. The "Do not claim" phrasing
  rule (md line 210) is exactly right.

## Minor notes (no correction row needed)

- md line 82's "(OPT-125m, 124M–8B scale)": OPT-125m is 125M parameters; "124M" appears to be
  GPT-2's size. Write "125M–8B" (the 8B end is S15's Llama-3.1-8B, not re-verified this session).
- WildAI (S1's corpus) is deliberately AI-enriched — "AI text is 42% of WildAI's tokens, far more
  than on the web. WildAI is a pool for building training mixtures, not a natural sample of the
  web" (App. A). The note's Q6 use of it (labels exist, provenance-by-construction) is fine, but no
  one should later quote the corpus's internal 42% share as a prevalence figure.
- md line 16's "in the retrieved abstract" qualifier is the tell that Q1/Q7 were written from
  abstracts; rows 9–10 are the consequences. Any future pass should read full text for core sources.
- json duplications of the incorrect claims (lines 37–38, 214, 263) matter more than the md ones:
  a machine-readable consumer will propagate "no reported false-positive rate" and
  "two monthly snapshots only" as fact.
