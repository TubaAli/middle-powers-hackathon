## 1. Where the game opens: a dated truce with two cliff edges

The game starts in **November 2026**, nine days before both governments' trade-truce
suspensions are scheduled to lapse. This is the single most useful dated fact for
Game B, because it makes US and Chinese reactions *time-contingent* instead of constant.

Verified state at the cutoff (all observations 2026-11-01):

1. **30 October 2025, Busan:** Trump and Xi met for ~100 minutes; the US readout
   framed it as a "trade and economic deal" ([S8](#source-ledger), published 1 Nov 2025).
   Xi's own readout listed **artificial intelligence** among the areas with "good
   potential" for the two countries "to work together on" ([S11](#source-ledger),
   30 Oct 2025).
2. **US side:** BIS published the **Affiliates Rule** (any entity ≥50% owned by
   Entity List parties automatically inherits Entity List restrictions, to address
   "diversion concerns") on 30 September 2025 [S6], then **stayed** it for one year
   on 10 November 2025. The stay text says the rule's changes are "stayed until
   November 9, 2026" and that "effective November 10, 2026 and extending
   indefinitely, the changes … will be added back into the EAR" [S7]. So the US rule
   **automatically snaps back on 10 November 2026 unless extended**.
3. **China side:** MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 70 of 2025 (7 November 2025) suspends
   the 9 October 2025 export-control package (Announcements 55–58, 61, 62 — rare
   earths, superhard materials, the 0.1% foreign-made-product rule) **until 10
   November 2026** [S12] (original Chinese text read; translation mine). The US fact
   sheet says China also committed to issue **general licences** for rare earths,
   gallium, germanium, antimony and graphite "for the benefit of U.S. end users and
   their suppliers around the world" [S8].
4. **May 2026:** a Beijing summit chartered US-China Boards of Trade and Investment
   [S9]. **25 September 2026:** a reciprocal state visit in Washington "operationalized"
   them, agreed the "super intelligence" terminology, established a **US–China
   Super Intelligence (SI) Dialogue** with "the next exchange … by November 2026", and
   — critically for this game — **"a bilateral communication channel for SI incidents"**
   [S9]. The same sheet records that rare-earth shipments were still a live concern
   being worked ("ensuring shipment levels return to appropriate levels").

### The 10 November 2026 question (unresolved at the cutoff)

- No extension of the US Affiliates Rule suspension appears in the Federal Register
  between 15 September and 1 November 2026 (agency query for BIS documents returned
  nine unrelated items) [S10].
- On the Chinese side, reporting conflicts: one trade source said **no extension had
  been confirmed as of 11 October 2026** [S16, search-snippet only]; Materials
  Dispatch (updated 27 October 2026) reports that **MOFCOM said on 28 September 2026
  that the Kuala Lumpur arrangement behind the pause is extended to 10 January 2027,
  but Announcement No. 70 itself had not been amended as of 27 October 2026** [S13].
  I did not locate the primary 28 September MOFCOM statement.

**Game use:** the opening month should carry a visible, dated uncertainty ("truce
suspensions lapse around 10 November; extension reported, not published"), not a
preset answer. This is honest and mechanically rich: a player who licenses their
chokepoint in November 2026 is acting inside a nine-day window.

## 2. Documented US positions and instruments (2025–2026)

Facts first; each is dated. Interpretation follows the bullet.

- **23 July 2025 — "Winning the Race: America's AI Action Plan"** released: >90
  federal actions; "Exporting American AI" (Commerce and State to "deliver secure,
  full-stack AI export packages … to America's friends and allies"); removing
  "onerous Federal regulations" [S1].
- **23 July 2025 — EO "Promoting the Export of the American AI Technology Stack"**:
  creates the **American AI Exports Program** (hardware, models, software,
  applications "must comply with export controls"), with Economic Diplomacy Action
  Group support (loans, guarantees, technical assistance) [S2].
- **30 September 2025 — BIS Affiliates Rule** (interim final rule, 90 FR 47201):
  ≥50%-owned affiliates of listed entities inherit restrictions; stated rationale is
  diversion via non-listed affiliates [S6].
- **20 February 2026 — India AI Impact Summit:** US delegation promoted "real AI
  sovereignty … owning and using best-in-class technology", urged countries to "join
  the U.S. as partners … with components of the American AI stack", said working with
  the US stack lets nations "keep sensitive data within their borders", and called
  for "firmly rejecting attempts at global governance of AI … bureaucracies and
  centralized control". New instruments announced: **National Champions Initiative**
  (partner nations' AI companies inside customised US stacks), **U.S. Tech Prosperity
  Corps** (Peace Corps), World Bank fund, Ex-Im/DFC financing, and the **NIST/CAISI AI
  Agent Standards Initiative** (interoperable, secure agentic-AI standards) [S3].
- **2 June 2026 — EO "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and
  Security"** [S4]: voluntary AI-cybersecurity clearinghouse; classified benchmarking
  of "covered frontier models" for cyber capabilities; a **voluntary framework with
  developers giving the Federal government secure early access** for trusted partners;
  and an express clause: "nothing shall be construed to authorize creation of any
  **mandatory governmental licensing, pre-clearance, or permitting requirement** for
  the development, publication, release or distribution of AI models". The sheet also
  lists a December 2025 EO protecting AI innovation from an "inconsistent and costly
  compliance regime resulting from varying State laws", a March 2026 National Cyber
  Strategy and a March 2026 national legislative framework.
- **Market access as an instrument (2026, from the Federal Register):** Proclamation
  11020 (2 April 2026) gives a **zero pharmaceutical tariff rate to jurisdictions with
  "a current or forthcoming trade and security framework agreement"**; Proclamation
  11052 (6 August 2026) restricts Chinese polysilicon imports, with a BIS rule (24
  September 2026) monitoring importers for stockpiling [S10].
- **25 September 2026 — state visit fact sheet:** SI Dialogue and the bilateral SI
  incident channel (quoted above) [S9].

Interpretation (mine, not the documents'): the US in 2025–26 runs a consistent pair
of positions. **Against:** mandatory, one-size, pre-release approval regimes on
models, and "global governance" mechanisms that could centralize control. **For:**
voluntary frameworks it designs itself, bilateral full-stack export packages with
security conditions attached, US-origin standards, and market-access rewards for
security alignment. Its export-control enforcement targets **diversion through
third countries**, and its chokepoint instrument (the Affiliates Rule) is scheduled
to snap back on 10 November 2026.

## 3. Documented China positions and instruments (2025–2026)

- **9 October 2025 — MOFCOM/GAC announcements (No. 61 of 2025 et al.):** export
  licensing for additional rare earths, plus a **0.1% Chinese-origin-content
  (value) licensing trigger for foreign-made products**, and a separate trigger for
  foreign-made goods produced with **Chinese rare-earth technology** even when the
  material is not Chinese; value provisions were to apply from 1 December 2025 [S13,
  S14 — trade/legal analyses of the primary; CSET translation located but not read].
- **7 November 2025 — Announcement No. 70 of 2025:** suspends Announcements 55–58
  and 61–62 from 7 November 2025 **until 10 November 2026** (original read in
  Chinese) [S12]. Per the US fact sheet, China also issued general licences for US
  end users and removed some US entity listings [S8].
- **26 July 2025 — "Global AI Governance Action Plan"** (WAIC, Shanghai; full official
  English text read) [S5]. Its 13 points include: safety governance via "a risk
  testing and evaluation system", "the sharing of information as well as the
  development of emergency response" for AI safety risks (point 10); capacity building
  "such as … establishing **mutual recognition platforms for safety assessment**"
  (point 12); the **UN as the main channel**, with support for the Independent
  International Scientific Panel on AI and the Global Dialogue on AI Governance
  (point 11); standards via ITU/ISO/IEC (point 8); and explicit support for the
  Global South to "truly access and utilize AI" (point 4).
- **1 July 2024 — UNGA resolution A/RES/78/311**, "Enhancing international cooperation
  on capacity-building of artificial intelligence": proposed by China, adopted by
  consensus, co-sponsored by 140+ states including the United States; it asks the
  Secretary-General to report to the **eightieth session** (i.e., within this game's
  timeframe) on developing-country AI capacity-building challenges [S17 — partial:
  title and operative paragraph read via UN Digital Library record/search snippets].
- **30 October 2025 — Xi's Busan framing:** cooperation potential with the US in
  "combating illegal immigration and telecom fraud, anti-money laundering,
  **artificial intelligence**, and responding to infectious diseases" [S11].
- **24 July 2026 — MOFCOM Announcement No. 30** (per a 25 October 2026 trade-press
  analysis [S14]; primary not retrieved): imposes **counterparty-specific** dual-use
  supply bans on 14 named entities, including firms in **Germany (Rheinmetall AG
  among them), France and the Netherlands**; it "prohibits overseas organisations and
  individuals from transferring or providing Chinese-origin dual-use items to them".
- **25 September 2026 — joint US fact sheet** records continued work on rare-earth
  shipment levels and the new SI incident channel [S9].

Interpretation (mine): China's documented hostility is aimed at **exclusionary
blocs and unilateral export controls aimed at it** ("protectionism, unilateralism,
and bullying" in Xi's APEC-era framing [S15]), and at US extraterritorial expansion
of entity lists. Its instruments are: licensing that is **slow and case-by-case**
rather than instant embargoes; **entity-specific** designations that reach third
countries (Announcement No. 30 names European defence-linked firms); general
licences as a **reward for de-escalation** (the Nov 2025 package; the Nexperia
resumption attributed to Dutch government action [S8/S15]); and an AI-diplomacy
platform built around UN channels, capacity building and — notably for this game —
**mutual-recognition safety assessment** and **incident-information sharing**.

## 5. Action-specific triggers: what actually sets each government off

This replaces "help rival ⇒ anger". Each trigger names the *instrument* a middle
power's specific action would actually touch.

**United States** (all citations in section 2):

1. **Diversion of US-controlled tech via your territory or firms.** Trigger:
   re-export/transshipment of controlled US chips or tools, or hosting/servicing
   arrangements that let listed (or ≥50%-owned) Chinese entities access them.
   Instrument: Entity List / Affiliates Rule snapback on 10 Nov 2026; criminal
   enforcement. Time cost: licensing and compliance review. This is the US's
   best-documented red line [S6, S7, S10].
2. **Mandatory pre-clearance on models.** Trigger: a rule that makes releasing a
   model inside your jurisdiction conditional on government approval. Instrument:
   diplomatic objection, exclusion of your market from AI export packages. A
   **voluntary, reciprocal early-access scheme** is the documented alternative the US
   itself uses [S4].
3. **Anti-US-standards fragmentation.** Trigger: rules that force a third standards
   stack or duplicate compliance. Instrument: NIST/CAISI standards initiative as the
   offer [S3]; market-access terms [S10].
4. **Market access as reward.** A "trade and security framework agreement" earns
   tariff relief and export-package access [S10, S3] — the US pays for alignment
   rather than only punishing deviation.
5. **Data-localization-compatible partnership is a US selling point**, not a
   trigger: "keep sensitive data within their borders" via the US stack [S3].

**China:**

1. **Joining US-aligned export controls aimed at China** (the game's chokepoint-licence
   move). Trigger: licensing your chokepoint stage in a way that replicates US
   restrictions on Chinese end users. Instrument: slow, case-by-case licensing of
   your imports (2025 backlogs hit global supply chains); entity-specific designation
   of your defence-linked firms (Announcement No. 30 named German, French and Dutch
   firms in July 2026) [S13, S14, S15]. **Reversible:** de-escalation earned general
   licences and a Nexperia-related resumption attributed to Dutch government action
   [S8, S15].
2. **Truce lapse ≠ choice.** China's expansive controls are *suspended until 10
   November 2026*, with the extension reported but not published at the cutoff
   [S12, S13, S16]. If they lapse, the 0.1% content rule, the technology-provenance
   trigger and the five added elements bite **manufacturing supply chains**, including
   middle powers' — a stockpile/seed-own-programme event with a real date [S13].
3. **Seeding your own capacity with Chinese technology keeps the hook.** Foreign-made
   goods produced with Chinese rare-earth **technology** are captured even with
   non-Chinese material [S13]. A middle power's "seed your own refining programme"
   move should inherit a *technology-provenance* dependency if built on Chinese kit.
4. **Bloc framing of a safety accord.** Trigger: an accord presented as excluding
   China. Instrument: rhetorical hostility, UN-channel counter-mobilisation
   (A/RES/78/311 shows the inclusive forum China invests in [S17]). **Not a trigger:**
   safety conditions themselves — China's Action Plan proposes mutual-recognition
   safety-assessment platforms, testing frameworks and incident-information sharing
   [S5], and it joined a bilateral incident channel with the US [S9].
5. **Confidential data in licensing.** Chinese licence applications require
   end-use/supply-chain disclosure; European bodies have raised confidentiality
   concerns about information exposed to Chinese authorities [S14, citing an EU
   official text — not read directly]. A middle power sharing incident or
   supply-chain data with a US-linked coalition could complicate its Chinese
   licensing; the same data flows are also a US selling point in the other direction
   [S3].

**Aid unrelated to AI is documented as a separate track.** The 2025–26 truce trades
soybeans, coal, pandas and fentanyl cooperation alongside chips and minerals
[S8, S9]; China's entity designations are end-use specific (defence-linked firms)
[S14]; the US rewards framework partners with tariff lines [S10]. Nothing retrieved
shows either government punishing third-country food, medicine or energy aid as
such. The game's non-AI tokens (food/medicine/energy/minerals/capital/market) can
therefore be offered **without automatically triggering the AI-rival**, which the
current `odds()` function already handles correctly by pricing tokens on need — keep
that.

## 6. Proposed player-visible rules (policy cards)

Six cards. Each names actor, objective, authority/instrument, trigger, conditions,
time cost, plausible response, substitute and evidence limit, per the contract.

### US-1 · Pre-release testing condition (game condition 1)

- **Actor/objective:** US wants its labs' release cycles free of foreign mandatory
  approval; it wants early access for *itself* though.
- **Authority/instrument:** June 2026 EO's express anti-pre-clearance clause and its
  voluntary early-access framework [S4]; AI Action Plan deregulation pillar [S1].
- **Trigger:** the coalition demands safety-institute testing as a *licensing
  condition* for hosting/serving models.
- **Conditions & outcomes:** mandatory → letter declines *for now*, citing its
  no-mandatory-licensing policy, and counters with a **voluntary, reciprocal
  early-access memorandum** (their institutes get voluntary access; yours get access
  to US cyber-defence tooling). Voluntary + security-framed → limited deal possible.
- **Time cost:** one month's review, since the EO's frameworks already exist.
- **Substitute the US offers:** membership of its AI-cybersecurity clearinghouse and
  NIST agentic-AI standards work [S4, S3].
- **Evidence limit:** no documented US response to a *middle-power coalition* demand
  exists; the EO is domestic. The card extrapolates a documented domestic principle,
  not an observed bilateral reaction.

### US-2 · Chip-location reporting condition (game condition 5)

- **Actor/objective:** US wants to know where advanced chips are; its whole 2025–26
  enforcement layer exists for this [S6, S7].
- **Trigger:** the coalition's export licences carry location-reporting conditions.
- **Conditions & outcomes:** compatible reporting (shared formats, compatible end-use
  checks) → US letters support the condition and offer intelligence-sharing; a
  *conflicting* scheme that audits US firms' lawful trade → US asks for
  mutual-recognition of compliance instead.
- **Time cost:** alignment review across two months (there is a real 10 Nov 2026
  snapback date to schedule around [S7]).
- **Plausible response:** support, not anger — this is the one condition where the
  current game's −10 has no support (row B).
- **Evidence limit:** US instruments address diversion *to China*; a middle-power
  scheme aimed at *all* suppliers (including US ones) has no observed precedent.

### US-3 · The competing bilateral package (replaces the "leans on your ally" event)

- **Actor/objective:** US wants partners on the American stack, not in anti-US
  rule-writing bodies [S3].
- **Authority/instrument:** American AI Exports Program, National Champions
  Initiative, Tech Prosperity Corps, Ex-Im/DFC financing [S2, S3]; tariff relief for
  "trade and security framework agreement" partners [S10]; precedent deal: US–Korea
  Technology Prosperity Deal [S18, located not read].
- **Trigger:** a coalition member is close to joining your accord.
- **Conditions & outcomes:** the US letter offers that member a customised
  full-stack package with financing, conditional on standards alignment and
  anti-diversion compliance. The member may take it instead of your accord — a
  *choice*, not an eviction. Match this to the game's existing `needs()` token logic.
- **Time cost:** one month (state-visit cadence [S9]).
- **Evidence limit:** instrument is documented; using it to compete with a
  safety-coalition specifically is extrapolation. No coercion mechanism is documented.

### US-4 · The 10 November 2026 snapback (calendar event)

- **Actor/authority:** BIS, Federal Register [S7].
- **Trigger:** the date arrives inside the game's first month, whichever way the
  player moves.
- **Conditions & outcomes:** if the player's bloc hosts or services Chinese-linked
  AI supply chains, Entity-List-affiliate risk returns; if extended (gap — unknown at
  cutoff), the event passes with a "truce holds" letter. Show the date on the board;
  let the player's stockpile/licence moves interact with it.
- **Evidence limit:** the snapback is law today; the extension question is open
  [S10, S13, S16]. The game must not pre-decide it either way.

### CN-1 · Chokepoint licensing reaction (replaces the flat retaliation roll)

- **Actor/objective:** China opposes US-aligned controls that target it; it wants to
  be seen defending openness [S5, S15].
- **Authority/instrument:** rare-earth/magnet licensing (slow, case-by-case),
  entity-specific designation (Announcement No. 30 precedent) [S12–S15].
- **Trigger:** the player licenses a chokepoint stage with conditions that exclude
  Chinese end users.
- **Conditions & outcomes:** not an instant embargo. First a **letter of objection
  citing bloc behaviour**; then licence processing for the player's strategic
  industries slows (−1 token per month for N months, modelling backlog rather than
  embargo); a defence-linked national champion may be **named** (visible, specific,
  reversible). De-escalation — the player removing China-exclusion language or
  offering reciprocal access — earns restored flows (the Nexperia and general-licence
  precedents [S8, S15]).
- **Time cost:** months, which the game already has (8 turns).
- **Evidence limit:** backlog effects on *middle powers* specifically are inferred
  from global 2025 effects and European designations; no observed case of China
  punishing a country *solely* for an AI-safety coalition was found — because no
  such coalition exists yet.

### CN-2 · Safety conditions are negotiable; framing is the trigger

- **Actor/objective:** China's stated AI-governance goals include testing and
  incident cooperation — multilaterally and reciprocally [S5, S9, S17].
- **Trigger set:** (a) accord text routed through UN mechanisms or open to Chinese
  participation → China may join condition 2 (incident definition) via extending its
  SI-incident-channel practice, and condition 1 as a **mutual-recognition assessment
  platform** (its own proposal, [S5: point 12]); (b) accord framed as an
  exclusionary bloc or bundled with export-control alignment → refusal letter citing
  bloc behaviour, counteroffer of UN-track cooperation.
- **Conditions & outcomes:** limited deals per condition, letters state the framing
  condition explicitly — the player can rewrite the accord's framing next month and
  re-ask.
- **Time cost:** one month per re-ask (game's existing monthly rhythm).
- **Evidence limit:** China's positions are stated policy and one bilateral channel
  with the US; its reaction to *middle-power* safety conditions is unobserved. The
  card should keep China's acceptance hard-won (its own behaviour is slow,
  case-by-case), just not *categorically* anti-safety.

## 7. Lawful cooperation possibilities (both powers have said yes to these)

For the integration test — "can a player understand what the other government wants
and why its answer changed?" — these are the documented doors a middle-power coalition
could walk through in-world, November 2026 → June 2027:

1. **Plug into the UN mechanisms both powers support:** the Global Dialogue on AI
   Governance and the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI [S5: point 11],
   with the Secretary-General's capacity-building report to the General Assembly's
   eightieth session due inside the game window [S17]. The game's June 2027 Geneva
   summit can be framed as the coalition plugging its six conditions into a channel
   both powers endorse, rather than inventing a rival club.
2. **Extend the SI-incident-channel idea.** The US and China agreed to a bilateral SI
   incident channel in September 2026 [S9]. A middle-power coalition proposing to
   connect its shared incident definition (condition 2) to that emerging practice is
   a documented-adjacent ask, for both powers.
3. **Voluntary early access as a standard package.** The US's own framework [S4] and
   China's mutual-recognition proposal [S5] converge on voluntary/reciprocal testing
   — the coalition's condition 1 in its most passable form.
4. **Buy stability with de-escalation.** Both 2025–26 suspensions show each power
  rewarding the other's restraint with general licences and tariff relief [S8, S12].
   A middle power that visibly avoids US-exclusion or China-exclusion language is
   modelling documented behaviour, not naivety.

## 8. Contradictory evidence

1. **What "eliminate" meant.** The White House fact sheet says China will
   "effectively eliminate" its rare-earth export controls; China's own Announcement
   No. 70 suspends them to a fixed date [S8 vs S12]; Brookings flags the gap between
   the two readouts as the truce's core ambiguity [S15]. The game should treat
   suspension-with-expiry as the operative fact.
2. **The extension reports conflict.** "No extension confirmed as of 11 October 2026"
   [S16] vs "MOFCOM said 28 September 2026 the arrangement is extended to 10 January
   2027, but no amending notice published as of 27 October" [S13]. Both are
   trade-press; neither primary notice was located. Unresolved at cutoff.
3. **The 0.1% threshold basis.** Materials Dispatch's April 2026 article described
   the threshold as by weight; its 25 October 2026 article describes it as **by
   value** [S13 vs S14]. The later, legal-briefing-based reading should be used, but
   the primary text (CSET translation) was not read in this pass.
4. **US "anti-testing" vs US early access.** Both are true simultaneously: the US
   rejects *mandatory* pre-clearance for models while running a *voluntary* early-
   access framework for its own government [S4]. Any rule that flattens this into
   "the US hates testing" will be wrong half the time.
5. **China "anti-safety-rules" vs China's own proposals.** The Action Plan proposes
   testing and incident cooperation [S5]; the state visit added a bilateral incident
   channel [S9]. The game's current CN carve-out (condition 2 only) is directionally
   right about incidents but wrong to make everything else uniformly hostile.
