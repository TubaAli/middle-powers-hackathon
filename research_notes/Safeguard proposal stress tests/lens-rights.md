# Rights and accountability: isolated analytical viewpoint

**Evidence cutoff: 31 October 2026. Decision checkpoint: Geneva, 21–22 June 2027.** This is an analytical viewpoint, not an advocate's testimony or an institutional position. The companion JSON records the 14 fixed-packet hashes and claim-level locators. No external retrieval or peer-lens material informs this choice. Source attributions below are checked against the packet; original case files and technical results unavailable there have not been independently verified.

## Recommendation

**Give the next validation test to `brazil-anpd-ai-inspection-evidence`, narrowly instantiated as the Grok correction-evidence case.** Test whether an existing demand can produce independently reproducible correction, usable redress and proportionate restrictions across the covered interfaces. Do not describe this as validation of the football-club inspection cohort or the whole Brazilian programme. [rights-view-1, 2]

The attraction is not that privacy automatically outranks other safety. The packet supplies an unusually concrete falsifiable chain: reported failures despite company assurances → identified inspection file and evidence demand → a correction that can be challenged. On 11 February 2026, ANPD, MPF and Senacon reported inadequate efficacy evidence and persistent failures in preliminary tests. ANPD identified file **00261.000178/2026-27** and demanded verifiable evidence within five business days covering versions, plans and modalities. This is an official account, not our replication; the clock measures a demand, not time to successful remedy. [rights-view-2]

## Strongest factual challenge and reversal conditions

**The strongest challenge to my first choice is the missing outcome and access record.** The packet has neither Brazil's complete technical remedy record nor the current October case disposition. A press release can identify an investigation without giving an evaluator access to reproduce it. Furthermore, the evidence supports Grok-specific inspection, not the precise original cohort represented by the route's model score. Keeping the old cohort denominator would fabricate coverage. [rights-view-2, 7]

Reverse the priority if, by **15 December 2026**, current authority, lawful access to a versioned technical record and a named independent reviewer cannot be established, or if the record already contains a credible completed independent retest, making another test low-value. This date is a proposed research gate, not an official deadline. Promote Colombia/Kenya only if their operative orders and architecture-level evidence are accessible; otherwise promote the Singapore pair only if a national owner can exercise a real hold. If none qualifies, report unresolved feasibility rather than nominate a paper pilot. [rights-view-11]

Reverse on substance if correction merely shifts failures to another interface, harms legitimate expression or access beyond the predeclared tolerance, leaves complaints unusable, or cannot be reproduced independently. A severe unresolved failure does not become acceptable because average performance improved. An inaccessible case is not evidence that the regulator failed. [rights-view-11]

## Values, assumptions and competing risks

- **Value choice:** count prevention of nonconsensual abuse, wrongful biometric matching and denial of effective redress as serious benefits in their own right. The model gives 40% of its impact-axis weight to reach and defines its upper end as broad frontier-model/infrastructure exposure. This is an editorial valuation, not evidence that a particular frontier-labelled route protects more people. I would show direct protection and systemic risk separately rather than silently rescore either. [rights-view-8]
- **Self-challenge:** more assessments, certificates and complaint forms may impose costs without changing a decision. Nigeria's GAID permits a certificate upon filing while separately providing risk-based audit; Kenya's completion account is the litigant's, not independent technical proof. Rights language deserves no automatic effectiveness premium. [rights-view-3, 9]
- **Severe future risks remain relevant:** a delegated common LUMI service condition could protect shared infrastructure and spread containment practice. Its terms provide a common contracting surface, but not recovered delegation for this safety requirement or unrestricted evaluation access. AISI's reported out-of-scope agent actions in privileged testing make containment a concrete concern, without establishing a production incident rate. Evidence of authorised, affordable containment over consequential workloads could displace this rights-first testing priority. [rights-view-10]
- **Substitution matters equally:** India's documented normal commercial continuation after subsidy expiry shows why support withdrawal cannot simply be called a workload stop. Brazil must likewise be checked for interface/version displacement; deletion audits must include derivatives and processors rather than a local certificate alone. Neither example proves the proposed safeguard has already been evaded. [rights-view-3, 12]
- **Assumptions:** independent access can be arranged, at least one useful comparison record survives, and the intervention can change operations before Geneva. None is an observed commitment. There are no comparable staffing costs, representative affected-person preferences or current covered-population estimates sufficient to establish a quantitative winner. [rights-view-7, 11]

## Falsifiable validation test

**Hypothesis:** for one lawfully specified Grok case scope, the evidence demand connects an independently detectable failure to a persistent operational correction and a usable route for an affected person to contest the result. [rights-view-11]

1. **Authority/access gate — proposed 15 December 2026.** The competent ANPD case owner confirms the operative requirement, service/status dates, current scope and escalation path. Obtain protected access to the actual technical note, version/interface inventory, response and disposition. Separate company assertions, regulator findings and independent findings. Appoint a non-developing reviewer with disclosed conflicts and a route for disputed findings. Record costs and permissions; do not infer independence from accreditation.
2. **Protocol — proposed 31 January 2027.** Pre-register matched pre/post or otherwise defensible comparison tests, held-out variants and benign-use controls. Use lawful synthetic, non-abusive proxies; do not expose real victims or reproduce harmful material publicly. If proxies cannot measure the legally relevant failure, mark the endpoint unvalidated. Fix sample size, uncertainty method, critical-failure definition, benign-use tolerance and resource cap before examining results. Protect claimant evidence under applicable preservation rules while minimising new sensitive data.
3. **Correction and contest — proposed 31 March 2027.** Witness the actual case-specific response to a reproduced failure, then test fresh variants on every interface in the declared scope. The regulator, not the evaluator, decides legal consequences. Test a consented or synthetic complaint journey through acknowledgment, human contest, decision and demonstrable correction where applicable. A working form alone is not redress.
4. **Persistence — proposed 1 June 2027.** Repeat after relevant version/configuration changes. Record residual failures, false holds, legitimate-task completion, correction time, staff hours, reviewer queue, controller cost and complaint abandonment. Report excluded interfaces and whether restrictions displaced activity. Count only tested versions and observed activity; do not borrow the football-club cohort or global user numbers.

**Reject validation** for a reproduced unresolved critical failure, unexercised consequence, non-reproducible correction, unusable contest route, or breach of the predeclared access/utility/cost limits. An underpowered or incomparable result is **inconclusive**, not a pass. Report experimental results separately from observed production outcomes; no small synthetic study establishes prevented population harm. For candidate 3, require shared review to preserve critical-failure detection and national decision quality while reducing total review effort; extra coordination without protection or savings loses its claimed advantage. [rights-view-6, 11]

## Two useful follow-up designs

1. **Challengeable remedy receipt.** Extend a versioned technical receipt with the affected person's correction path, evidence of an actual operational change, review conflicts, outstanding exceptions and expiry/retest trigger. Keep personal data and exploit details in restricted evidence; publish only a suitably redacted decision summary if authorised. Compare the receipt with ordinary case handling on completed corrections, time, abandonment and reviewer cost. If it adds paperwork without benefit, drop it. This adds contestability to the packet's technical-evidence designs. [rights-view-13]
2. **Split-knowledge deletion challenge.** In the Colombia case, and separately Kenya if access permits, an independent examiner reconciles source data, iris codes, distributed derivatives, processors and backup exceptions against the actual order. Use authorised synthetic canaries to challenge residual matching and re-ingestion; never infer global erasure from a negative query. Claimants receive an intelligible result and challenge route without exposing biometric linkages. Compare coverage and reproducibility across the two legal processes, retaining their differences. If the architecture cannot be enumerated, the correct output is a bounded, failed verification—not a stronger certificate. [rights-view-3, 14]

## Dissent I would defend

**Do not let potential frontier reach automatically outrank an enforceable remedy for people already exposed to serious harm. Equally, do not treat a rights instrument as effective until it changes what happens to a person.** I would defend spending the next marginal validation effort on Brazil's concrete failed-assurance case, while changing that choice for stronger accessible evidence of persistent correction, better protective coverage per constrained review effort, or an executable containment measure addressing severe future risk. [rights-view-1, 8–11]
