# Implementation and delivery viewpoint

Evidence cutoff: **31 October 2026**. Decision horizon: **Geneva, 21–22 June 2027**. This is an isolated analytical viewpoint, not an official's testimony or institutional consent. Only the fixed packet and its referenced saved passage were used; no new retrieval. Source IDs below resolve in the packet. The companion JSON records hashes of all 14 packet files.

## Recommendation

Give **`jp-procurement-safety-evidence` the next validation test**, specifically an authority-and-cost check followed by one version-change/withdrawal acceptance rehearsal. The decision-maker to approach is **the Digital Agency's responsible Gen-nai councillor (源内班の参事官), or the documented delegate empowered to approve that change**, together with the contracting officer. The packet identifies the office, **not the incumbent's personal identity or delegation for this proposed clause**. Naming an invented official would disguise the first delivery dependency.

The changeable instrument is a **lawfully still-open FY2027 tender/acceptance schedule paired with the platform release record**. Confirm that it is still changeable before treating it as an opportunity. Do not retroactively reinterpret a signed trial contract. This is a choice about the next information-producing test, not a declaration that Japan already has the best safeguard or greatest safety impact.

The advantage is the conjunction of a documented release decision, real procurement delivery, and a prospective March-award/April-use sequence. The next test can directly expose whether those pieces connect. The disadvantage is equally concrete: none establishes a supplier-bound model-safety remedy, available evaluator hours, or control of upstream model retirement.

## Evidence and source-scope checks

- **implementation-1 — Primary normative text, directly checked in the saved extract:** Gen-nai §4-3 requires prior approval from the empowered person, recorded evidence, another developer's review, and coordinated rollback/recovery. §4-5 prohibits release with high-severity vulnerabilities. This is stronger than an unnamed sponsor but narrower than an independent model-safety certificate or a proven operational hold. Source **`asia-jp-standard`**, physical pp36–40; saved `japan-asia-source-extracts/jp-standard.txt`, lines 1099–1132 and 1206–1249. The report's phrase “independent technical review” must be read as another-developer review, not external institutional independence.
- **implementation-2 — Primary procurement records as represented in the fixed evidence table:** KDDI's ¥499,983,000 rollout-support award and NTT Data's ¥390,000,000 verification-environment award have specified delivery periods ending March 2027. They establish purchased delivery work, not spare staff, payment, completed acceptance or the cost of this safeguard. Sources **`ap-s-jp-april`, `ap-s-jp-may`, `ap-s-final-jp-rollout`**, award cells and appendix p23. The planned 300 questions/15 hours are a selection workload, not a complete safety-review staffing estimate (**`ap-s-jp-plan`**, p2). Actual marginal cost and staff availability remain unknown.
- **implementation-3 — Attributed stakeholder statements and official responses:** Graffer and Rakuten object that downstream suppliers cannot disclose upstream information they do not hold; the response allows reasonable negotiated disclosure, but retains important-system obligations despite upstream-change objections. This does not prove all SMEs are excluded or that any supplier accepts a new duty. **`asia-jp-comments`**, physical pp38,46–47,69, as reported in `japan-asia.md`.
- **implementation-4 — Primary guidance as represented in the shard:** DS-920 supports risk-specific procurement provisions and startup market access; independent/red-team testing is among example methods. It is not a uniform external-certification mandate. **`asia-jp-guideline`**, physical pp40–41, §§6.3.2–6.3.3.
- **implementation-5 — Programme evidence and its limits:** Singapore's three-month requirement is a target, not a measured lead-time distribution or price. AIVF explicitly does not warrant tester independence or whole-system safety. **`apac-sg-sandbox`**, journey/eligibility; **`asia-sg-accreditation`**, introductory disclaimer. Neither establishes a buyer's hold.
- **implementation-6 — Primary common terms as represented in the shard:** LUMI identifies CSC as contracting entity, allows additional service terms and applies common resource cut-off policy; content access remains purpose-limited. **`euro-lumi-terms`**, preamble, §§6,11–12. The inference that a common annex could reduce handoffs requires an internal delegation and rights check. Existing cut-off policy is not a safety enforcement result.
- **implementation-7 — Counterweight to convenient pilots:** ANPD's February 2026 account reports persistent Grok failures after claimed safeguards and demands verifiable correction evidence. **`rights-br-grok`**, opening and ANPD sections. That is a more directly consequential failure signal than account counts. It does not establish current case status or successful correction; the existing route's football-club cohort must not inherit Grok outcomes.

## Strongest factual objection, assumptions and values

**Strongest objection:** the packet has a platform vulnerability hold and supplier objections, but no signed version-specific model-safety acceptance/remedy schedule. A working approval form could therefore coexist with an unusable fallback and a supplier unable to provide the demanded evidence. Programme maturity cannot close this gap.

My value choice is to prioritise a reversible, discriminating implementation test over speculative breadth. I also value useful service continuity, contestability and supplier access; refusal of every task is not safety success. These are choices, not inferred stakeholder consensus.

Assumptions to expose: at least one meaningful workflow is inside the approver's authority; a prospective contract remains open; version or configuration identity can be traced; a non-developing reviewer and replacement operator can be assigned; evidence access does not require indiscriminate transfer of secrets or personal data. Each remains conditional.

**Challenge to my own preference:** a well-run internal drafting pilot may govern too little serious activity to justify priority. Require an inventory of actual sensitive-data/tool actions and normal transaction volume, with excluded and bypassed activity recorded. No 180,000-official denominator, budget total or test-question count substitutes for this. If only low-consequence demonstration tasks are available while a rights-enforcement case offers accessible failed-control and correction records, reconsider the priority rather than expanding the pilot's rhetoric.

## One falsifiable near-term validation test

**Proposed test: two-supplier, one-workflow version-change acceptance rehearsal.** All dates and thresholds here are proposals, not commitments or calibrated universal standards.

1. **By 15 December 2026**, identify the actual approver and contracting officer; obtain the delegation, still-changeable instrument, lawful test/data rights, and an acceptance schedule specifying a critical-failure hold, correction and fresh retest. Identify one real workflow, a larger supplier and a capable smaller supplier/integrator within lawful procurement eligibility. A small sample tests feasibility, not population-wide exclusion. Failure to find an eligible smaller participant is reported, not remedied by silently waiving eligibility.
2. Require an **itemised quote and named-person availability**: purchaser/contract review, workflow owner, integration engineer, non-developing retester, privacy reviewer and fallback operations. These are functions, not necessarily six hires. Record hours, loaded rates, queue/elapsed time, cloud charges, supplier preparation, remediation and recurring version-change costs. The buyer must set a maximum funded envelope and available staff-days **before results**; no defensible numeric budget is in the packet. Compare against ordinary acceptance for the same workflow. No work proceeds on the fiction that the programme budget is spare evaluator money.
3. **By 31 March 2027**, use a staging copy with synthetic data and disabled external actions. Randomise clean tasks, held-out leakage/unauthorised-action cases and a seeded materially changed or withdrawn model configuration. Exercise the release pipeline's real permission boundary in staging. The failed build must remain unapproved; the previous safe version or manual limited-function service must remain usable. Record approver action, technical restriction, correction and independently scored **fresh** retest. A tabletop signature alone fails.
4. Predeclare a **zero-tolerance rule for seeded critical disclosure/action failures** and a proposed clean-task floor of **90% of baseline successful completion**, with no loss of the workflow's essential service. Any critical failure blocks that build; meeting the finite test does not prove universal safety. The owner must confirm that this utility floor and the chosen harm class are appropriate before execution. Both supplier paths must be able to meet the same outcome requirement without pretending to possess upstream data they lack.
5. **By 1 June 2027**, report actual staff-days/cost against the funded cap; detected and missed defects; false holds; service completion/delay; supplier evidence gaps/withdrawals; and the joined workflow/version/permission inventory. Any observation of normal production use must be separately authorised and labelled; a staging result is not measured real-world harm reduction.

**Falsification/reversal:** withdraw Japan's priority if authority or amendability is absent on 15 December, the quote cannot be funded/staffed, the seeded failed version escapes the hold, fresh retesting fails, the replacement destroys essential service, or comparable smaller suppliers cannot participate because of unnecessary fixed evidence costs. A Singapore purchaser with an executable agreement, quote and consequential workflow would then deserve the equivalent test; otherwise report no validated ready route. Switch toward LUMI only on affirmative delegation, workload and cost evidence—not merely because Japan failed.

## Two useful follow-up designs

1. **Proportionate acceptance pack with a cost ledger.** The buyer funds a reusable baseline harness where lawful, accepts equivalent scoped evidence, and separately prices application-specific retests. Confidential examination is an option when necessary, not compulsory escrow of all training data. Keep assessor conflicts, supplier hours, abandoned bids and non-evidence rejection reasons visible. Test whether common work actually lowers small-supplier burden without weakening critical outcome thresholds. Do not force SMEs to subsidise the buyer's public-interest assurance.
2. **Common receipt, local decision.** After the local hold works, reuse only matching version/tool/permission fields across the LUMI national/common pair or separately willing purchasers. Each competent owner retains its own acceptance and rights decisions. Measure duplicate review hours and added governed transactions; withdraw portability on unexplained disagreement. No export of a receipt creates overseas model control, and no shared failure should be propagated as mutual assurance.

## Dissent I would defend

**I would reject calling a certificate, completed procurement or successful staging demonstration a delivered safeguard.** The defensible endpoint is an authorised owner actually restricting the failed configuration, correcting it, retesting it and maintaining useful service at a known cost. I would also reject making small suppliers produce inaccessible upstream information while incumbents satisfy the rule with polished dossiers. But I would not accept permanent low-impact pilots as the price of tractability: if the selected workflow has negligible consequential exposure, the next test should follow an evidenced harmful operation instead.
