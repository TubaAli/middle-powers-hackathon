## 1. Which existing routes survive a search for consequential enforcement?

### Takeaway

**Brazil and Colombia have much stronger concrete enforcement targets than another generic pilot invitation.** Brazil reports technical tests showing that X's claimed Grok safeguards remained inadequate, followed by a demand for verifiable correction evidence. Colombia reports a final administrative Worldcoin decision requiring external certification and technical evidence of deletion. Kenya's litigant reports completed court-supervised deletion, but the underlying technical verification is still missing. These findings distinguish an order, a completion claim and independently verified success. **The strongest technical counterexample is Colombia.** SIC reports that the companies argued their distributed SMPC/AMPC iris fragments were no longer personal data. The authority rejected that position because the system retains an individualisation function, and said the companies had not clearly explained deletion in the distributed architecture. This is a regulator's account of a contested company argument, not independent cryptographic validation. A secure architecture does not by itself cure unlawful collection or make deletion verifiable. [“El componente técnico”][co]

**Preserve a scope discrepancy.** The October 2025 SIC release describes closure of all personal-data processing; the July 2026 release describes sensitive-data processing. Retrieve the dispositive orders before stating the exact final prohibition. Administrative finality in the latter release does not establish that no judicial challenge is possible. [October release][co1] · [July release][co]

**Kenya's outcome is reported, not technically independently verified here.** The original Katiba statement expressly welcomes completed deletion. ### Inferences

- Prioritise the named Brazilian technical note and Colombian external certificate over more programme-count searches. Each can discriminate between an effective control and a paper response.
- Mexico's committee provisions reduce **role ambiguity**, not the gap in release authority. Requiring its signature without checking delegation would manufacture an approval gate.
- South Africa supplies a useful **different-procedure analogue**. Transferring a section-95 enforcement order into section-57 prior authorisation would be a legal category error.
- Nigeria's proposed annex needs a clear marginal contribution: independently testing whether existing obligations work. Merely adding inventory, mitigation and stop-choice fields duplicates parts of GAID.

## 2. Do the proposals protect people, and does the model value that fairly?

### Takeaway

**The evidence supports a direct-rights outcome axis, not an automatic “privacy means low AI-safety impact” discount.** It also supports no automatic upgrade: legal authority, self-reported compliance and filing counts are weaker than a verified change in harmful processing. The current model explicitly makes broad frontier-model/infrastructure exposure the top of its *reach* scale, while *relevance* is defined as prevention or containment of a specified serious failure. That creates a valuation choice which should be exposed, not mistaken for an empirical finding. [Local model, factor definitions and eight route entries][model]

### Cited Findings

- **Actual civil-society position:** Katiba Institute says “no technology, however novel, operates above the law.” Its statement centres privacy, dignity and data protection; Executive Director Nora Mbagathi links the implementation to resistance against extractive treatment of African data. This is the litigant's stated position, **not** the position of all affected Kenyans or all African communities. [Original statement, paragraphs 2 and final paragraph][ki]
- **Actual regulator test of false assurance:** Brazil says X's descriptions of removed posts, suspended accounts and safety measures lacked the necessary concrete technical evidence; preliminary testing found the failures persisted. This directly challenges counting a policy response or removal total as a successful safeguard. [Opening paragraphs][br]
- **Actual evidence-access tension:** South Africa's notice records WhatsApp's argument that disclosure of commercially sensitive security details could undermine safeguards, and the regulator's rejection of taking safeguards merely on trust. This is the authority's account of the company's position; the company's original submission was not recovered. [Notice p.7, B6.3–6.6][za]
- **False-certification risk is a design concern, not an observed prevalence estimate:** GAID permits issuing a CAR certificate upon filing, while separately permitting additional information and requiring risk-based audit. NDPC's original speech account confirms it cited a **NGN766.2 million MultiChoice fine**, but does not establish that an auditor falsely certified that company, that the fine was collected, or that remediation succeeded. [GAID art.10, pp.17–18][ng] · [NDPC, main article third paragraph][ngfine]
- **Rights and alternatives are already part of technical governance:** Nigeria's Schedule 4 asks about less intrusive alternatives, stakeholder consultation, disparate effects, mitigation and processing cessation; Rwanda's arts.37/42/45 specify correction, information and communication to people. A leakage-only annex would omit some of the existing protection objectives. [GAID pp.86–87,92,98–99][ng] · [Rwanda law][rw]

### Inferences — analytical lenses, not invented stakeholder testimony

| Lens | What would make the proposal useful? | How could it fail? |
|---|---|---|
| Regulator | A bounded evidence request tied to an existing duty, named case owner, usable failure threshold and clear escalation record. | More dossiers exceed available review time; sanctions are announced but no one checks correction; foreign processor evidence remains inaccessible. |
| Affected person/community | Unlawful collection stops; data are actually suppressed; a wrongly matched person can obtain correction; a usable service alternative and complaint route remain. | A clean technical test conceals coerced consent, exclusion or unusable redress; deletion proof omits derivatives; a supposedly protective system increases surveillance. |
| Startup/controller | One proportionate, reusable evidence package; staging tests; clear applicability and a confidential way to provide meaningful proof. | Fixed audit costs favour incumbents; vague all-version thresholds prevent predictable release; repeated audits consume runway; test-gaming earns certificates without reducing harm. |

These are hypotheses to measure. **Weak staffing is not established merely because a current technical staffing roster was not found.** Obtain reviewer-hours, waiting time, incomplete submissions and post-order verification workload for the named case. Similarly, five-day evidence demands, 60-day reporting periods and 30-day discretionary approvals are **legal/procedural clocks**, not observed end-to-end performance. [Brazil ANPD section][br] · [WhatsApp notice D/E][za] · [GAID art.35][ng]

**Model implications, without changing scores:**

1. Show direct rights outcomes separately from systemic/frontier spillovers. A confirmed correction or deletion should not need a speculative chain to catastrophic risk to count as a serious protection. Conversely, a model-level covenant should not inherit measured harm prevention from its potential breadth. [Model definitions][model]
2. Separate remedy maturity into **power → issued order → reported implementation → independently verified implementation → persistent outcome**. Brazil currently supplies an order after a failed assurance; Colombia supplies an administratively final obligation; Kenya supplies a litigant's completion report. None supplies the full technical sequence here. [Brazil][br] · [Colombia][co] · [Kenya][ki]
3. Keep domestic court, regulator, controller, processor and evaluator roles distinct. They may be causal dependencies without each being a party whose voluntary consent is legally required. The Kenyan judicial path and Brazil's separate agency powers make this important. [Katiba judgment account][ki] · [Brazil “Medidas adotadas”][br]

### Gaps

No fresh startup interview, representative community study, comparable audit-cost sample or verified staffing allocation was recovered. Company objections are available only as recorded by regulators. There is no defensible numerical estimate here of harm prevented, evasion, audit dishonesty or national AI coverage. Affected persons' preferences, non-biometric alternatives and service-denial outcomes need direct evidence, not simulated testimony.

## 3. What should be tested next, and what would falsify the proposal?

### Takeaway

Three follow-ups are worth taking to an actual decision owner. **All dates below are proposed research milestones, not official commitments.** Each requires an observable adverse result and a consequential response; a document's existence is not the success criterion.

### Cited Findings and proposed tests

**1. Brazil — audit the existing Grok correction-evidence demand.**

- **Owner/instrument:** ANPD inspection owner for file 00261.000178/2026-27; X supplies evidence; independent evaluator only where lawfully authorised. The authority already identified its preventive evidence instrument. [“ANPD”][br]
- **Proposed demand/test:** identify actual versions/interfaces and compare the original failing test record with a distinct held-out set. Review false positives, residual failures and the regulator's disposition. Use lawful non-abusive synthetic proxies, not victim images or public demonstrations producing abuse.
- **Remedy:** ANPD makes case-specific legal findings and escalation decisions; the evaluator does not create an automatic sanction. The original account describes possible additional measures/daily fines and sanction proceedings. [“Próximos passos”][br]
- **Proposed dates:** current case scope/status by **15 December 2026**; evidence audit by **31 March 2027**; persistence check by **1 June 2027**.
- **Falsifier/adverse effects:** no reproducible evidence, failures merely displaced to another interface, or false-positive blocking causes material lawful-expression harms. Confidential access and narrow test scope reduce evidence leakage and victim re-exposure.

**2. Colombia, with Kenya as a separate comparator — verify deletion rather than accept the certificate label.**

- **Owner/instrument:** SIC's competent personal-data authority, the two named obligors and the external certifier required by the reported final decision. Obtain the operative text first. Kenya remains a distinct judicial/ODPC path, not a joint pilot already agreed. [Colombia conclusion][co] · [Katiba][ki]
- **Proposed demand/test:** reconcile all original data, iris codes, secret-shared derivatives, nodes and backup exceptions with deletion evidence. Test residual matching/re-ingestion using authorised synthetic canaries; preserve the distinction between a controller assertion, certifier finding and regulator acceptance.
- **Remedy:** enforce the actual suppression/closure order through the applicable process; a laboratory pass is not permission to restart. Do not invent a new fine.
- **Proposed dates:** order and certificate retrieval by **15 December 2026**; technical review by **31 March 2027**; persistence and subject-facing rights check by **1 June 2027**.
- **Falsifier/adverse effects:** the certifier cannot enumerate derivatives or test the relevant architecture; deletion claims conflict with retained matching ability. Audit collection can reveal sensitive linkages, while indiscriminate deletion can destroy evidence needed by claimants: minimise audit data and respect lawful preservation obligations.

**3. Nigeria — a small audit challenge that measures additional value and cost.**

- **Owner/instrument:** NDPC for interpretation/acceptance; selected CAR-obligated controller's management for remediation; licensed DPCO for audit; accredited certified DPO for DPIA vetting. Use arts.10/28 and Schedule 4. Article 35 is relevant only where an imported interoperable measure actually needs Commission direction, not as an automatic extra approval step. [GAID pp.18,33,40–41][ng]
- **Proposed demand/test:** seed authorised synthetic access/deletion faults in a staging copy of one real AI workflow. Compare ordinary audit findings with independent scoring. Does a material failure change the DPIA's go-ahead/modify/stop result? Can a synthetic subject use correction/deletion rights? Does a held-out retest detect unresolved failure? Record reviewer-hours, cost and missed defects.
- **Remedy:** management corrects or stops the affected processing; NDPC applies existing duties and process to actual legal breaches. A failed experimental test alone is not a new offence.
- **Proposed dates:** eligibility and test access by **15 December 2026**; align with the controller's verified 2027 filing date; retest by **31 May 2027**. GAID distinguishes legacy annual March filing from post-12-June-2023 establishment timing; do not assume one deadline for every startup. [Arts.10(7)–(8), pp.17–18][ng]
- **Falsifier/adverse effects:** the annex detects nothing beyond the normal audit, detected faults never change processing, or cost becomes disproportionate. Limit the first scope, separate remediation from independent retest, protect confidential material and report cost/error ranges rather than a league table that rewards gaming.

### Inferences

The discriminating question across the full set is: **can a named owner connect a legally relevant defect to a changed operation, then demonstrate that the change protects the affected person?** If the answer is only a strategy, assessment count, certificate, order or press release, retain the appropriate intermediate status. A rights-enforcement route can be practically valuable before it has broad frontier spillovers; that does not remove the need to test whether it works.
