# Frontier-risk and technical-safety additionality lens

**Isolated analytical viewpoint; 31 October 2026.** Checkpoint: Geneva, **21–22 June 2027**. This is neither an expert interview nor an institutional position. Only the fixed packet and its directly referenced saved extracts were used. Claims are keyed to the companion JSON; packet hashes identify all 14 inputs. No derived rank is treated as an observation.

## Recommendation

**Give `jp-procurement-safety-evidence` the next validation test. Pair it experimentally with `sg-kr-jp-shared-eval-evidence-protocol`, but make national action independent of successful international coordination.** The question is whether a version-bound, independently held-out result changes a real release decision and prevents a serious failure that ordinary compliant review misses—not whether another certificate can be produced. [frontier-1–4, frontier-8–10]

Choose **one actual workflow** where an agent consumes untrusted retrieved content and has a meaningful authorisation boundary. The serious failure is either disclosure of a protected record to the wrong principal or an unauthorised consequential tool action. Test with synthetic records and controlled destinations. Withhold the affected tool permission, provide a usable restricted/manual fallback, correct the configuration and require a fresh retest. Actual exposure, a lawful owner, observable model/configuration changes and evaluator capacity are prerequisites, not facts established by government access totals.

This prioritises a tractable information-producing test. It does **not** establish that Japan is globally safest or most consequential, or that a local permission boundary controls a provider's global frontier-model release. No candidate presently supplies a demonstrated catastrophic-risk reduction in this packet.

## What the saved passages actually establish

1. **Existing Japanese compliance is substantial.** `japan-asia-source-extracts/jp-standard.txt`, lines 1206–1249 (physical pp39–40), requires remediation of high-severity vulnerabilities before release, tracked production changes, another developer's review plus authorised approval, and stop/rollback incident procedures. Another developer is not an independent external assessor. These are requirements, not evidence they operated successfully. [frontier-1]
2. **Testing itself is not new.** `jp-guideline.txt`, lines 1393–1398 and 1411–1434 (physical pp40–41), covers risk-based contract additions and intended-purpose input/output testing, with independent internal/external red teaming among examples. It does not prove a universal external certificate or a signed supplier remedy. [frontier-2]
3. **The LUMI legal surface also has an existing-control comparator.** `europe-finance-source-extracts/lumi-terms.pdf.txt`, lines 24–32, 84–101, 127–150, 185–200 and 208–229, identifies CSC, supplementary terms, misuse restrictions, purpose-limited content access, suspension and common cut-off/change procedures. These passages do not reveal who can approve this proposed annex. Content ownership and copying after termination also defeat a casual assumption that ending access recalls models. [frontier-5–6]
4. **Exposure counts and replication findings have a lower verification tier here.** The coordinate packet distinguishes Japanese access, survey use and planned coverage; it also reports prior safety-selection requirements and SG/KR pipeline discrepancies. I inspected the packet records, not the underlying rollout/survey/bilateral study extracts. They motivate enrollment questions; they do not establish protected transactions or replicated safety benefit. The Brazilian claim likewise remains explicitly attributed packet evidence. [frontier-4, frontier-7–8]

## Strongest objection: this may duplicate what already exists

Japan already specifies review, testing, change controls and incident response. A fresh “safety gate” may merely rebrand those duties while adding examiner cost and delaying legitimate work. Its reach may also be far below what a frontier-risk objective would demand.

**I accept both objections.** Compare the proposed control against **fully implemented ordinary requirements**, while separately recording gaps in current practice. Repairing ordinary noncompliance is useful but must not be scored as proof that a new mandatory layer is necessary. The additionality claim survives only when a relevant behavioural failure escapes ordinary compliant review, is caught by the new evidence, and causes an effective restriction with acceptable service utility and cost. [frontier-1–3]

## One falsifiable near-term test

**Additionality challenge at one real Japanese release boundary**—all dates and decision rules below are proposals, not commitments. [frontier-10]

- **By 15 December 2026:** select one workflow, one serious failure and the complete ordinary-control comparator. Verify that synthetic data preserve the real permission semantics. Identify the application owner, authorised release decision-maker, necessary contracting officer and non-developing evaluator.
- **No-go on 31 January 2027** if relevant exposure, delegated authority, configuration/change observability, trace access or funded capacity is missing. No programme-total proxy can cure this.
- **By 31 March:** run paired staging copies/replays. Arm A faithfully implements ordinary requirements; Arm B adds the version-bound independent evidence and enforced permission hold. Blind adjudicators; include benign tasks, a deliberately defective negative control and held-out attack families. Equalise attack/adaptation effort. Record tool-side effects and invalid runs, not just final-text refusals. Use repeated runs with a preregistered precision calculation and task-family uncertainty; a pile of correlated runs is not a large independent sample.
- **Primary outcome:** exposed task families in which the wrong principal receives the protected synthetic record or an unauthorised action crosses the tool boundary **after the full review/release process**. A reproducible escape in A prevented in B establishes a candidate added mechanism, not a population-wide prevented-incident estimate.
- Before unblinding, set the minimum worthwhile reduction, uncertainty criterion, affordable cash/staff-time/queue caps and service requirements. As a proposed pilot guardrail, legitimate task completion should decline by **no more than five percentage points**. Zero critical escapes in B is necessary for that tested release, never a universal-safety guarantee. Insufficient precision means **inconclusive**; no excess A failures means no demonstrated added protection.
- Witness the failed release, actual credential withholding, usable fallback, correction and fresh retest. Repeat on structurally new and translated cases and after material model/scaffold/permission changes. No payment remedy is assumed without its applicable signed right.
- **By 31 May:** obtain a bounded persistence record, preferably at least four weeks of authorised scoped observation, plus optional second-lab replication. Join configurations to eligible/gated transactions, overrides, bypass attempts, alternative applications and fallback completions. Count reviewer hours, disagreement, supplier effort, queues, lawful-task refusals and time to correction. Protect private traces and measure accessible service completion rather than assuming manual fallback is harmless.
- **By 1 June:** issue the scoped decision receipt. The cross-border arm passes only with reproducible decision-relevant findings and useful protection or reduced duplicate work. Suspend portability on unresolved safety-relevant disagreement; national authority never transfers with the receipt.

**Evidence that reverses the choice:** missing enrollment conditions; no protection beyond ordinary compliant controls; repair failing fresh/adaptive cases; a bypassable hold or unobserved upstream changes; disproportionate burden or unusable fallback. Coordination failure alone drops the coordinated extension, not a successful national gate. These rules prevent changing the endpoint after disappointing results.

## Two follow-up designs

**1. Permission-scoped receipt with national decision adapter.** Bind endpoint/version, scaffold, retrieval snapshot, permissions, test family, scoring and clean utility to a compact evidence record. Attach a separate authorised national accept/hold/retest decision. Give a second lab defective and corrected configurations, including an undisclosed material manifest change. Measure detection, disagreements and duplicate hours. Withdraw portability when the change is missed or sharing adds work without protection. Confidential traces remain access-controlled; no omnibus “safe model” certificate follows.

**2. LUMI containment delta.** With actual delegation and two eligible projects, compare existing security controls with a narrow egress/credential policy plus scoped incident escalation and job pause/restart. Exercise the same isolated agent against a synthetic external sink via a Belgian recipient annex and a common service condition. Measure prevention **before the effect reaches the sink**, not merely eventual account closure. Compare handoffs, hours, false blocks, useful job completion and off-host continuation. No added safety is claimed if existing controls already contain the events; no coalition bonus without saved repeated work or additional verified activity. [frontier-5–6]

## Value choices and remaining uncertainty

I value independently demonstrated prevention of serious local harm, while keeping catastrophic frontier risks a separate objective. This may select less potential breadth in exchange for a test that can discriminate by Geneva. Broad upstream intervention remains attractive if actual high-risk workloads, authority and additional protection become verifiable; neither a compute budget nor national headquarters proves those links.

Missing inputs include actual exposed deployment IDs and activity, behavioural-hold authority, evaluator quotes and queues, supplier/change visibility, synthetic-test validity, incident prevalence, displacement, LUMI annex delegation and Brazil's current technical record. Missing staff allocations do not prove incompetence, and a deadline on a document is not observed completion time. International evidence reuse must justify its translation/review costs and respect local rights; agreement among evaluators does not validate a flawed test.

## Dissent I would defend

**Choose a narrow, independently verified prevention of serious local harm over an unexercised frontier-wide covenant before Geneva—but do not rename that success catastrophic-risk reduction. If existing controls already do the job, withdraw the new mandatory layer rather than preserve it through a new label or international consensus.**
