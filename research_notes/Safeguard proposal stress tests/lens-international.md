# International scale: test shared implementation without waiting for shared sovereignty

**Evidence cutoff and observation date: 31 October 2026. Decision horizon: Geneva, 21–22 June 2027.** This is an isolated analytical viewpoint, not diplomatic testimony or institutional endorsement. Only the fixed 14-file packet and directly referenced saved passages inform it. The companion JSON hashes that packet and identifies claim-level locators. No new retrieval or peer recommendations were used.

## Recommendation

**Give the next validation test to the matched pair `lumi-belgium-safety-appendix` / `lumi-national-evidence-protocol`.** Test whether a lawful common host condition can carry the *same* version-bound evidence, containment and cure requirement from one Belgian project to one additional national channel. Do not make eleven-country agreement a prerequisite for the Belgian protection. This selects the highest-value uncertainty to resolve, not a demonstrated winner on safety impact. [international-1, international-8]

The reason is narrower than “international reach is bigger.” LUMI's actual terms name CSC as the contracting host, permit additional service terms, apply a common utilisation policy across national and EuroHPC projects, and provide an amendment-notice procedure. These make it possible that the costly national-policy-rewrite design is avoidable. They do **not** show who may authorise this particular safety condition. The bilateral/trilateral evaluation candidates have technical collaboration precedents but still need distinct deployment authorities. [international-2, international-3, international-5]

## Core evidence and its limits

**[international-2: fact] Authority is partly shared already.** LUMI Terms v2.0, last changed **10 February 2026**, preamble (saved text lines 24–32), defines CSC's representative role and supplementary terms. Sections 10–12 (lines 185–229) cover suspension for unauthorised use/suspected terms breaches, shared utilisation cut-offs and at least two weeks' amendment notice. A notice period is not the time required to secure internal approval. An existing suspension clause is not an already adopted safety-test failure clause.

**[international-3: fact] The rights boundary cuts directly against the preferred design.** Section 6 (lines 127–152) limits content access to service accessibility, quality and security, protects confidentiality and leaves IP ownership with users. Section 7 (lines 153–165) specifies personal-data responsibilities. These passages do not grant an outside evaluator unrestricted access to model weights, training data or prompts. Obtain purpose-specific permissions and resolve third-party rights before treating evidence as transferable.

**[international-4: fact] Scope is an envelope, not a benefit estimate.** The saved Belgian access page states Belgium's **7.4%** share of total LUMI time, within the **50%** distributed nationally. It also lists continuous preparatory submissions reviewed once a month; the listed regular 2026 cut-offs are past. The coordinate packet warns that preparatory work is development/benchmarking, that Belgian package-GPU accounting differs from other GPU figures, and that current metered, safety-bound AI usage is missing. Do not add 7.4% to 50%, multiply system AI use by either share, or label awarded hours “protected.”

**[international-5: fact] Harmonisation has real technical value and correlated-error risk.** The saved **20 November 2024** international testing report describes code/log sharing that resolved parsing and token-limit differences, explicitly as a methodological exercise rather than a new safety assessment. The **17 July 2025** report describes distributed translation/annotation contributions and judge/environment limitations. These support testing reproducible evidence reuse. They establish neither legal mutual recognition nor a reduction in real-world harm.

**[international-6: fact] Supplier acceptance is substantive, not a signature count.** In Japan's saved consultation, physical p69, Graffer and Rakuten explain that downstream suppliers may lack upstream training-data/algorithm access. The government describes negotiated reasonable disclosure, not a uniform command. On p38 it retains stronger language despite Graffer's upstream-update/retirement objection. The trade-off is unresolved: cheap portability achieved by discarding critical evidence is not success. These are actual Japanese positions, not evidence that LUMI users or all international suppliers hold the same views.

**[international-7: fact] National action remains credible.** The saved Gen-nai standard, physical pp36–37, requires an authorised production-change approval and another developer's review; pp8–9 specify modularity, migration preparation and purpose-limited logging. These are requirements, not witnessed enforcement, successful substitution or independent external certification. They are nevertheless a serious alternative to waiting for international agreement.

## Value assumptions and strongest contrary case

**[international-8: value judgment]** I give extra weight to reusable safeguards covering *additional distinct activity*, provided they retain the national protection's failure threshold, rights and useful service. I value resolving a common-authority bottleneck before commissioning many duplicate national implementations. I do not value country count, favourable coordination scores or consensus for their own sake. This priority could differ from a reasonable preference for the fastest single deployed gate.

The strongest contrary evidence is LUMI's own purpose-limited access clause and the absence of an identified internal safety-amendment delegation. The shared cut-off is a utilisation rule, not evidence that a safety annex can follow the same approval path. If the pilot merely adds documentation to a narrow benchmark allocation, while a Japanese or Singaporean gate could block a consequential release, international scale has lost its justification. [international-3, international-4, international-7]

**[international-9: inference]** Fragmented national gates can duplicate evidence preparation, delay reviews and reward routing through the least demanding channel. A common host condition could reduce these problems inside LUMI; it cannot stop migration to another host or recall exported weights. Conversely, harmonisation can delay a feasible national gate, spread a shared measurement defect, or dilute requirements until every participant agrees. Neither cost savings, evasion rates nor net coverage gains have been measured in this packet. Coalition incentives—predictable access for recipients, shared review capacity for allocators, operational consistency for CSC—are hypotheses, not consent. Publication, confidentiality and allocation priorities may oppose them.

## One discriminating test: two-channel, equal-safeguard crossover

**[international-10: proposal]** The question is: **can shared administration add a genuinely bound second-channel workload at no weaker protection and acceptable total burden, without delaying the Belgian route?**

1. **Authority and timing.** By **15 January 2027** (a proposed stop date), identify the actual CSC delegation, Belgian decision owner and one other willing national allocator; obtain the precise prospective annex and recipient permissions. Use an eligible development/benchmarking application where required. No assumption of a reopened regular award or eleven separate vetoes. If lawful shared adoption cannot be established, stop the shared arm and continue a feasible national test.
2. **Matched implementation.** Use one Belgian and one distinct other-channel project with comparable, scoped AI tasks. Freeze model/container, tools, permissions, task suite, evidence schema and severity rules. Review each dossier through both a national-specific and reusable-evidence workflow; counterbalance review order with equivalent variants to expose learning effects. Only the properly authorised workflow governs service access. Record extra local tests rather than suppressing them for apparent agreement.
3. **Exercise the remedy.** In isolated staging, seed one prohibited tool/data action and one benign workload. The authorised owner must actually hold the defective job/configuration, preserve minimal evidence, permit cure, require held-out retesting and demonstrate restart. Replay a channel-transfer attempt against the held configuration; separately document out-of-host substitution feasibility. No live harmful release is needed. Successful staging is operational evidence, not a causal population safety estimate.
4. **Staffing and cost.** Name the service owner, allocator decision owners, rights reviewer, operational controller and technically competent non-developing reviewer. Measure their hours, supplier preparation, translation, legal negotiation, queue time, compute spend, reservation/disruption costs and recurring retest burden. Count one-off shared setup as well as marginal savings. No price, spare capacity or funder is presumed. Before running, owners set an explicit affordable effort/delay ceiling; changing it after results invalidates a favourable verdict.
5. **Coverage and outcome.** Join recipient, project, channel, model/configuration, job and accepted-annex version to metered node-hours during a fixed observation window. Count physical work once even if reviewed twice. Report the second channel's distinct bound activity separately from overlap, unused awards, safety holds and work moved outside scope. Measure detected seeded failures, held-out unsafe actions, benign completion, false holds, time to containment and successful cure/restart. Node-hours measure scope, not harm prevented.
6. **Rights and spillovers.** Keep raw private data local where possible; permit only necessary scoped examiner access and record refusals. Preserve national stricter requirements, reasons and a challenge/correction path. Check harm to benign co-tenants and exclusion of smaller recipients. Do not average away a material failure or count an inaccessible evidence item as a pass.

**Decision receipt by 31 May 2027**, leaving time before Geneva. Retain the shared route only if both channels demonstrably accept and exercise the same minimum safeguard, the added activity is deduplicated, material local protections survive, and measured burden meets the predeclared ceiling. Review agreement alone is insufficient.

**Reverse the preference** if delegation/rights fail; the second channel is merely duplicate activity; a failed configuration escapes via channel switching; the shared path delays the national protection beyond the accepted ceiling; or apparent efficiency relies on dropping a critical test. Prefer the viable national arm, or the Singapore national/evidence pair if it obtains an actual gate and costed held-out test first. Conversely, a lawful common annex with distinct additional work and an exercised remedy would justify extending the test to more channels—not asserting proven harm reduction. [international-10]

## Useful follow-up designs

**[international-11: proposal] 1. A common containment annex with local acceptance records.** One host-side manifest specifies configuration, permitted actions, evidence retention, incident routing and scoped pause/cure/restart. Each allocator records its authority, additional tests and exceptions. Recipients submit a reusable core once, with purpose-limited examiner access. The host checks a held configuration across participating channels. This operationalises the preferred pair without pretending the whole consortium has adopted it.

**[international-12: proposal] 2. A portable evidence receipt with a visible non-recognition register.** For the Singapore/evaluation or Japan/procurement pair, separate technical findings from each buyer's acceptance decision. Record exactly why a receipt is reused, supplemented or refused; bind it to versions, language, permissions, judge calibration and expiry/change triggers. Include an upstream-change/fallback rehearsal and public aggregate burden/exception reporting without exposing private evidence. This can reveal which disagreements are technical, legal or protective before anyone proposes binding mutual recognition.

## Dissent I would defend

**Do not require international consensus before a lawful national owner protects its own users. Equally, do not dismiss the LUMI shared route merely because the current design names many countries.** The defensible next step is a time-bounded test of delegated common implementation with an independent national exit. If harmonisation cannot preserve protection, rights and useful additional scope, keep the national gate and publish the disagreement. Agreement is neither effectiveness nor permission.
