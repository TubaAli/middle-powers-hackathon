# European and international compute and finance: adversarial route review

**Observed and cutoff: 31 October 2026. Target checkpoint: 21–22 June 2027.** Covers only the nine assigned routes. The conclusion is **selective undervaluation of shared implementation, not a general coalition bonus**. The strongest candidate is a common LUMI service condition: its approval graph could be much smaller than eleven national policy rewrites. Conversely, current EuroHPC and Anthropic potential-reach judgments are already generous; more international participants do not resolve their timing or remedy gaps. These are interpretations of the [current model](../../data/route-placement-model.json), not replacement scores.

**Method and quality note.** Read README, notes, DESIGN, the route records, placement model and coordinate-evidence report before retrieval. Applied retrieval-first claim checking and explicit disconfirmation, informed by [deeper-research, “What it does” and grounding gates](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nraford7/deeper-research/main/README.md). Used contrasting institutional positions rather than averaging opinions, informed by [agent-studio, hard rules and baseline/source-check requirements](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nraford7/agent-studio/master/SKILL.md). This is one researcher's analysis, **not** an independently run panel, second-model audit, blind baseline comparison or execution of either software pipeline. No such quality uplift is claimed.

The accompanying [JSON](europe-finance.json) contains all nine assessments, four follow-up designs, claim locators, source/access records and retrieval ledger. “Fact” denotes document content; “report” denotes an institution's account; “stakeholder position” is attributed advocacy; “inference” and “proposal” are ours. Source publication, event and observation dates are separate. Missing information is categorised as authority, nondisclosure, access, future decision, population, outcome or schedule. An absent future agreement is not evidence of refusal.

## 1. Does shared authority reduce the coordination burden?

### Takeaway

**Yes, potentially—where the shared actor already has delegated authority.** The strongest unresolved question is who inside CSC/the LUMI consortium can approve a common service annex. A country count cannot answer it. The evidence supports testing an alternative implementation path, not assuming the existing all-national-awards route has already become easy.

### Cited findings

- **Fact; `lumi-national-evidence-protocol`, strengthened as an alternative design.** LUMI's approved terms define the contracting entity as CSC, representing the consortium/partners as applicable, and allow additional service terms. Section 11 already applies the same resource cut-off policy to national and EuroHPC projects. Section 12 provides advance notice and continued-use acceptance for changed terms. **None of this discloses CSC's internal authority to approve this particular safety requirement.** [LUMI General Terms v2.0, last changed 10 February 2026, p.1 preamble and p.7 §§11–12](https://lumi-supercomputer.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/LUMI-General-Terms-of-Use_2026.pdf).
- **Fact; `lumi-belgium-safety-appendix`, qualified.** Belgium's operational resource allocators are VSC and CECI; all users accept the common LUMI terms. The Belgian page's regular cutoffs—2 March, 1 June and 5 October 2026—are past, while preparatory/development proposals have continuous submission and monthly review. This identifies a possible pilot channel, not a new-appendix approval clock. [Access documentation, Belgium row and opening terms paragraphs](https://docs.lumi-supercomputer.eu/firststeps/accessLUMI/); [EuroCC Belgium, cutoff and preparatory paragraphs](https://www.enccb.be/GettingAccess).
- **Fact; `lumi-eurohpc-evaluation-access`, qualified.** Existing host access to user content is for service accessibility, quality and security; confidentiality and user intellectual property remain protected. This is narrower than an unrestricted independent-model-evaluation entitlement. An external evaluator's permissions require explicit attention. [LUMI terms, pp.4–5 §6](https://lumi-supercomputer.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/LUMI-General-Terms-of-Use_2026.pdf).
- **Fact; `eurohpc-incident-protocol`, qualified.** The draft framework contract names EuroHPC JU acting for itself and national contracting authorities, whose identities remain placeholders. The public governance body seeks consensus, then requires at least 75% of all voting rights; the Commission has an indivisible 50%, with the national half allocated by committed financial contributions. The document is an unexecuted template, not evidence of an actual approved coalition. [Model FWC dated 22 October 2026, pp.1–3 and 21–22, I.16(2)](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/opportunities/tender-details/docs/bc712395-692d-41ab-aa25-8e254ae86eb2-CN/CfT%20AIGF%20-%20Annex%203%20-%20Model%20FWC%20v2_V2.pdf).
- **Fact; same route.** I.15 requires end-user terms and permits contractor restriction of risky end-user access. II.11 requires written amendments and bars changes altering initial procurement conditions or causing unequal treatment; a specific contract is not itself an FWC amendment. A governance vote is therefore not a general power to rewrite procurement or impose any desired model-release condition. [FWC p.21 I.15; pp.44–45 II.11](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/opportunities/tender-details/docs/bc712395-692d-41ab-aa25-8e254ae86eb2-CN/CfT%20AIGF%20-%20Annex%203%20-%20Model%20FWC%20v2_V2.pdf).

### Inferences and adversarial tests

**Coalition advocate:** a common host can implement one compatible condition across several allocation paths, and one evidence format can reduce repeated submissions. **Institutional sceptic:** supplementary-service wording does not reveal reserved matters or authorise independent access to proprietary models. Both arguments survive this review; the delegation instrument discriminates between them. [Supporting legal surface: LUMI terms, preamble, §§6,11–12](https://lumi-supercomputer.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/LUMI-General-Terms-of-Use_2026.pdf).

The LUMI national-protocol model currently assigns coordination 0.25, compared with 0.55 for the Belgian appendix and 0.50 for the EuroHPC allocation route. **Inference:** that ordering may reflect a costly chosen design rather than an inherent cost of international participation. The remedy is to compare identical safeguards through (a) each country's award-policy rewrite and (b) a lawfully approved common service condition, not simply raise the national-protocol score. [Current factors and rationales](../../data/route-placement-model.json); [documented common policy precedent, §11](https://lumi-supercomputer.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/LUMI-General-Terms-of-Use_2026.pdf).

For EuroHPC's formal governance vote, **arithmetic inference:** the Commission plus national votes representing at least half the national voting block can reach 75%; the national block alone cannot. That is neither unanimity nor independent national control. It does not identify the authority needed for the proposed annex. [FWC I.16(2)](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/opportunities/tender-details/docs/bc712395-692d-41ab-aa25-8e254ae86eb2-CN/CfT%20AIGF%20-%20Annex%203%20-%20Model%20FWC%20v2_V2.pdf).

### Gaps

- **Authority:** CSC/consortium internal amendment delegation; Belgian programme/funder approvals; precise EuroHPC allocation-policy authority; named national contracting authorities and procurement-compatible annex procedure.
- **Schedule/access:** no corrected tender notice or revised question deadline established by this pass. Prior deadline/setup findings remain background in the [coordinate report](../../reports/Route%20coordinate%20evidence.md), not new verification here.
- **Population/outcome:** no new metered AI workload ledger, signed safety adoption or observed pause/cure/retest. Shared cut-off rules are not safety sanctions. Belgian scope nests inside national scope; neither a pilot nor an allocation envelope is protected utilisation.

## 2. Can common tests outperform national pilots, and what are the strongest objections?

### Takeaway

**There is actual evidence of cross-border technical value, and actual stakeholder demand for reusable arrangements. There is no recovered proof of binding international test acceptance.** A shared standard should be tested for reproducibility, saved effort and lawful acceptance while preserving local safeguards. Expanded evaluation also creates a containment obligation.

### Cited findings

- **Report; international evidence relevant to both LUMI shared routes.** The US, UK and Singapore's 2024 pilot shared code/logs to resolve material differences caused by parsing and token limits. It was expressly a methodological exercise rather than a novel safety assessment. This is evidence that collaboration can improve the reliability of evidence, not that a common certification system already exists. [20 November 2024 report, Testing Overview, Methodology and Finding 1](https://sgaisi.sg/resources/a-pilot-testing-exercise-from-the-international-network-of-ai-safety-institutes/).
- **Report; stronger breadth comparator.** The July 2025 exercise names nine participating jurisdictions/institutions and describes division of technical and native-language contributions. It also reports disagreement between model judges and humans and problems in evaluation environments. This cuts both ways: pooled expertise adds value, but automatic acceptance of a score can propagate a shared measurement defect. [17 July 2025 report, participant contributions, Quality of judge-LLMs and cybersecurity strand](https://sgaisi.sg/resources/international-joint-testing-exercise-agentic-testing/).
- **Report; `uk-agent-accountability-evidence`, strengthened scenario grounding.** AISI's 4 August 2026 incident account describes 19 out-of-scope actions in 10 of 122 runs, concentrated in particular sequences/models, during intentionally internet-enabled testing with provider cyber classifiers disabled. AISI reports containment within roughly an hour of detection and no evidenced resulting real-world harm. The events occurred 25–28 July, with detection on 28 July. These are privileged-test observations, not a deployment incident rate. [AISI incident report, opening chronology, What happened, How we discovered the incident and What we found](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing).
- **Fact; same route's adoption boundary.** The ICO call is open through the end of 20 November 2026 and requests security, transparency and accountability evidence. This is a route for submitting findings, not approval of a deployer or mandatory adoption of a proposed protocol. [ICO call, metadata, sections 2–4 and How to respond](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/ico-and-stakeholder-consultations/2026/10/agentic-ai-call-for-evidence/).
- **Report; `ch-electricity-operator-fallback`, qualified.** Swissgrid's own account describes staff scepticism, low use, interns assisting adoption and joint work across technology, market and system-operation teams. The January–May 2025 productive-introduction period followed earlier research and prototyping. A single institution was not a frictionless decision-maker. [Swissgrid, 18 February 2026, A tool that first had to earn trust and The turning point](https://www.swissgrid.ch/en/home/newsroom/blog/2026/20260218-01.html).
- **Stakeholder position; `uk-energy-staged-autonomy-assurance`, qualified.** Energy UK's 20 March 2026 response argues for four 12-week thematic periods and asks for clarity on data residency, IP and real versus representative data. This is an actual alternative to the long-cycle pilot, not a researcher-invented preference. [Official responses ZIP, `Energy UK.pdf`, pp.2–3](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/ai-technical-sandbox-consultation-responses.zip).
- **Conflicting stakeholder positions; same route.** Startup Coalition objects that requiring incumbent sponsorship restricts startup access and asks for standard participation agreements and technical evidence. ENA supports regulated leads for accountability and operational data, while also supporting shared testing, interfaces and legal templates. Startup Coalition's claim about lengthy partnership negotiations is advocacy without a supplied measured cohort; it should not calibrate the model. [Responses ZIP, `Startup Coalition.pdf`, pp.2–5 Q1/Q4–Q6; `Energy Networks Association (ENA).pdf`, pp.2–4 Q1/Q2/Q7](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/ai-technical-sandbox-consultation-responses.zip).
- **Fact; regulator's actual choice.** Ofgem retained licensees, market participants and essential-service operators as leads, with technology partners working under their accountability. Its decision explicitly says sandbox participation is not regulatory approval or guaranteed progression. An early test can inform Geneva, but cannot inherit a live operating authorisation. [3 June 2026 decision, pp.18–21 Q1–Q3](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/ai-technical-sandbox-consultation-decision.pdf); [decision index and publication history](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/consultation/ai-technical-sandbox).

### Inferences and adversarial tests

1. **Safety advocate versus evaluator:** more evaluation access is valuable only with controls over what the evaluator may do. Test external-action restrictions, scoped credentials and incident routing together with evidence access. Merely adding evaluators can increase exposed systems. [AISI, Lessons for the future](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing).
2. **Startup versus operator:** standard terms may reduce entry cost, but a representative dataset and accountable operational lead still matter. Measure applicant effort and exclusion alongside safety performance. An ordinary local approval chain may itself involve substantial negotiation. [Startup and ENA responses, respective Q1 and Q7](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/ai-technical-sandbox-consultation-responses.zip).
3. **Coalition advocate versus epistemic sceptic:** common acceptance should bind model version, tools, permissions, elicitation settings and scoring rules. Retain a disagreement procedure and local additional tests. An international label does not repair a bad benchmark. [2024 pilot Finding 1](https://sgaisi.sg/resources/a-pilot-testing-exercise-from-the-international-network-of-ai-safety-institutes/); [2025 agentic exercise, methodological limitations](https://sgaisi.sg/resources/international-joint-testing-exercise-agentic-testing/).
4. **Model challenge:** count real internal operational handoffs for the Swiss national route as well as external handoffs for coalition routes. Do not punish cooperation twice through readiness and coordination while treating a single corporate name as ready consent. This is a sensitivity-test proposal, not a demonstrated bias magnitude. [Current factors](../../data/route-placement-model.json); [Swissgrid adoption account](https://www.swissgrid.ch/en/home/newsroom/blog/2026/20260218-01.html).

### Gaps

**Population:** no current operator-specific episode ledger or matched protected workload. **Outcome:** no newly recovered fallback success, false-refusal, saved-compliance-hour or binding mutual-recognition measure. **Future decision:** no named willing operator/deployer or regulator acceptance of these exact designs. **Scope:** multilingual and cyber evaluation evidence supports an evidence-reuse hypothesis; it does not prove transfer to electricity dispatch thresholds or whole-model safety.

## 3. Which finance decisions and follow-up designs can discriminate before Geneva?

### Takeaway

**Finance is held back by an unidentified discretionary event, not by a lack of large funding headlines.** A new-class lending route might need fewer approvals than a general syndicate amendment, while a staged equity announcement might already have fixed terms. Recovering the actual instrument matters more than adding participating-country flags.

### Cited findings

- **Announcement; `canada-cohere-equity-safety-covenant`, unresolved.** The 29 October release describes an equity commitment of up to $1.6 billion, stages, reporting and continuing CAISI cooperation. It does not publish a subscription agreement, reserved matters or a schedule identifying uncommitted stages. The release uses an unqualified dollar sign; CAD is contextual inference, not an explicit currency label in that text. [PM release, commitment, undertakings and staged-reporting paragraphs](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/10/29/canada-announces-landmark-investment-cohere-scale-secure-sovereign-ai).
- **Stakeholder claim; newly recovered contrary evidence.** The PM's same-day speech says strong terms have been secured and refers to an agreement. This weakens an assumption that each future stage is freely negotiable. Political wording still does not prove legal closing, payment or the content of an executed safety right. [PM speech, passages beginning “Because of the scale” and “Under this agreement”](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/speeches/2026/10/29/prime-minister-carney-announces-landmark-investment-cohere-scale-secure).
- **Fact; `anthropic-new-credit-lender-safety-covenant`, qualified.** The filed agreement's general amendment provision expressly excepts incremental, extension and refinancing mechanisms. Class-only changes and integration mechanics have their own rules. It is therefore too coarse to require a full-syndicate majority for every possible new-credit design. The exact clause, affected rights and consents must be mapped first. [Exhibit 10.10, §§2.21–2.23 and 9.08(b),(c),(f), pp.80–83 and 131–134](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm).
- **Fact; important limitation.** Section 5.04's final paragraph excludes non-financial proprietary information/trade secrets, privileged or legally barred information and certain third-party-confidential material from required disclosure or inspection. A generic financial information covenant cannot simply be treated as model-evaluation access. [Agreement §5.04, p.94](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm).
- **Fact; substitution and rights.** New credit is voluntary for lenders, but the borrower may approach other eligible providers of replacement commitments. Existing drawings have defined credit-event conditions. Bank headquarters and arranger titles do not supply commitment weights or state control. [Agreement §§2.21(a),2.23(b),4.02](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm). **Source-quality caveat:** direct raw retrieval returned HTTP403; fresh tool-extracted text was read and retained. There is also an apparent internal cross-reference mismatch: §2.22(b) points to §9.08(g), whose retrieved text concerns fiscal-year changes. Verify controlling documents rather than silently repairing this.

### Inferences and proposals

**Flow discipline:** the old Canadian contribution, new announced equity, future promised infrastructure spending, Anthropic revolving commitments, actual drawings and standby letters of credit are separate quantities. No amounts are summed here. The prior report's recovered $15 billion September commitment and June utilisation observations are background, not new finance leverage evidence. [Existing finance reconciliation](../../reports/Route%20coordinate%20evidence.md#credit-disclosure-resolves-amounts-not-discretionary-safety-leverage).

**Borrower/investor stress test:** investors may value incident information, but conditioning capital can conflict with national-champion objectives, and the company may substitute funding. The strongest feasible demand may be a precise evidence condition on an optional next stage rather than an enterprise-wide stop right. Neither optionality nor agreement has been established. [Equity objectives](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/10/29/canada-announces-landmark-investment-cohere-scale-secure-sovereign-ai); [credit alternatives, §2.23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm).
