I.4.3 Implementation of the FWC
The AIGF set up is structured in two distinct linked phases (Phase 1 for initial deployment and
Phase 2 for expansion) and operationalised through the signature of consecutive specific
contracts under the FWC, for the purchase of AI compute access time under each phase in
accordance with the related condition specified in each specific contract.
The FWC is implemented as follows:
a) The contracting authority (-ies) orders services by sending the contractor a request for
specific contract. The request for specific contract must determine the time limit for the
contractor to state its intention to reply or not (which may not be less than three (3) days
of receipt of the request for specific contract) and the reply time limit (which may not
be less than seven (7) days of receipt of the request for specific contract).
When submitting a request for specific contract, the contracting authority (-ies) may
request the Contractor to implement additional specific technical requirements/ SLAs
that are necessary for the contacting authority (-ies) and the use of the AI Compute
capacity to be ordered. The contractor shall make every reasonable effort to
accommodate such requests and in case it agrees to these additional specific technical
requirements/SLAs, it shall do so at no additional cost for the contracting authority (-
ies).
b) Within the time limit indicated in the request for specific contract, the contractor must
state its intention to reply to the request for specific contract or not. The intention not
to reply must be motivated appropriately.
c) The contractor’s reply to the request for specific contract must be submitted within the
time limit indicated in the request for specific contract.
d) The contracting authorities have the right to proceed with the opening and the
assessment of the contractor’s reply, immediately after its receipt, without having to
wait for the expiration of the submission period specified in the request for specific
contract.
e) The reply is binding on the contractor at least for the validity period mentioned in the
request for specific contract, which should not be less than fifteen (15) days.

I.5.2. Price revision index
Price revision is determined by the formula set out in Article II.20 as amended by Article I.23.2
and using the trend in the following indices:
1. Inflation Index: This will be determined using the weighted combination of two
Eurostat Producer Price Indexes (PPI). To ensure maximum relevance to the
operational realities of an AIGF, this index will specifically use an equally weighted
10
PAGE 11

2. combination of the PPI categories most relevant to IT products with high energy
demands (50% of the Eurostat PPI for “computer, electronic and optical products”
combined with 50% of the PPI for “electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning
supply”).
Advanced AI Compute Proxy Index: This index serves as a hardware market value
proxy by tracking the public on-demand hourly instance rental rates [AM1] of
leading tier‑1 cloud service providers. It will be calculated as an equally weighted
basket (33.3% each) tracking the performance-normalised costs of configurations
powered by three state-of-the-art AI accelerators. The baseline basket will consist
of the public on-demand hourly rates3 for instances deploying:
a) The NVIDIA Blackwell GB200
b) The AMD Instinct MI355X.
c) The Google Cloud TPU v7.
The values for this index will be extracted directly from the official, publicly
accessible online pricing calculators and service pages of the respective cloud
providers. In case, any of the above-mentioned AI accelerators included in the pool
becomes obsolete or is discontinued, such accelerator shall be replaced in the
weighted basket with the vendor’s subsequent cloud instance model utilizing
hardware of equal or higher performance capabilities. This shall be documented by
an amendment to the FWC.

This clause shall apply exclusively to the limited circumstances expressly listed in Article
I.22.2. and it shall by no means constitute a general right to renegotiate service levels, pricing
or contractual obligations. A hardship event occurs when unforeseen circumstances arising after
the conclusion of the FWC fundamentally alter its equilibrium, imposing an excessive burden
on one party (the Affected Party), provided that all the following conditions are met:
(a) the events could not reasonably have been taken into account by the Affected Party at
the time of conclusion of the FWC; and
(b) the events are beyond the reasonable control of the Affected Party and the reasonably
assumed risk for such contract;
I.22.2 Exhaustive List of Hardship Events
A hardship event shall exist only if one or more of the following events occur after the
signature of the FWC, the following shall be considered potential hardship events in the
context of AIGF operations:
• Governmental export bans, export control restrictions, sanctions, or embargoes
affecting critical technologies for which no functionally equivalent alternatives are
commercially available.
• A sustained, extraordinary and significant change in compute capacity costs
directly necessary for the provision of the services (energy, cloud infrastructure,
semiconductor supply, advanced chips or other necessary hardware for AI compute

to the baseline costs as documented at the tender submission (Annex II), where such a

continue;
• A material change in applicable law or regulation or regulatory restrictions that

Appendix II Service Level Agreement (SLA) (minimum
requirements) for the Union and National Contracting
Authority s’ access time
In case the contractor fails to achieve any service level specified herein, the Contracting
Authorities shall be entitled to any remedies provided for in the FWC/specific contract,
including the application of liquidated damages in accordance with Article II.15 and/or
reduction in price in accordance with Article II.16.
1. Service Availability and Continuity
• Service Hours: The AIGF services provided to users must be available 24 hours, 7
days per week, except when there is maintenance. A service can be requested during
support hours.
• Availability Targets: The Contractor must ensure a monthly uptime of:
o Minimum 99.5% for general compute and AI Training workloads.
o Minimum 99.9% for AI Inference workloads (excluding scheduled
maintenance).
• Service Unavailability: Calculated in minutes per calendar month. Outages are counted
even if users do not open an incident. Excludes scheduled maintenance, force majeure,
or user-induced errors.
• Disaster Recovery - Business Continuity (Inference): The Contractor must ensure for
the disaster recovery a Recovery Time Objective (RTO) of ≤ 4 hours, and a Recovery
Point Objective (RPO) of ≤ 1 hour.
• External Connectivity: The Contractor will seek 100% availability for external
connectivity, allowing no more than 3 days of maintenance per year.
2. Performance and Capacity (Workload-Specific)
• Common Requirements: The Contractor must continuously monitor the infrastructure.
To guarantee sustained performance, benchmarks must be executed regularly (at least
every 1 year), achieving at least 90% of the performance measured at the system’s initial
installation.
• Specific Requirements for AI Inference:
o Latency: P95 latency ≤ 300 ms for standard workloads. (Custom thresholds may
be defined by Contracting Authorities and by common agreement of the parties
for complex models prior to specific contracts).

o Throughput: The system must support dynamic auto-scaling to handle
fluctuating workloads and guarantee baseline throughput capacity (measured in
Requests Per Second or an equivalent metric) as defined by the Contractor in
their tender. The Contractor must implement configurable queuing policies
capable of prioritizing critical public sector services during periods of peak
demand.
• Specific Requirements for AI Training:
o Job Stability: The system must guarantee the stability of long-running training
jobs and provide checkpointing support to prevent data loss during hardware
failures.
3. Support and Incident Management
• Support Hours: Standard support is available from 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM (Hosting
Entity local time), Monday through Friday. A single point of contact (helpdesk) must
be established. The maximum response time during standard support shall be 30 min.
For agreed critical inference services, 24/7 extended support must be provided.The
maximum response time during shall be 10 minutes.
• Support Structure: The Contractor must maintain a three-tier support model to ensure
the efficient handling of all service requests.
o 1st Level Support (Service Desk) serves as the initial point of contact for logging
incidents, performing basic triage, and resolving common issues within the
defined response targets (ranging from ≤15 minutes for critical incidents to ≤1
business day for low-severity issues).
o Unresolved, configuration, or API-related issues escalate to 2nd Level Support
(Technical/Application) for advanced diagnostics and workarounds.
o Finally, software defects, security breaches, or challenges requiring root cause
analysis (RCA), patching, or model adjustments are escalated to 3rd Level
Support (Expert/Engineering) for specialized intervention and long-term
resolution.
• Incident Matrix: The Contractor must resolve issues based on their direct impact on
the Contracting Authorities’ AI services and active compute workloads according to the
following targets:
o Critical (Service outage impacting essential services): Response ≤ 15 min |
Resolution ≤ 4 hours.
o High (Significant degradation): Response ≤ 1 hour | Resolution ≤ 8 hours.
o Medium (Partial service impact): Response ≤ 4 hours | Resolution ≤ 2 business
days.
