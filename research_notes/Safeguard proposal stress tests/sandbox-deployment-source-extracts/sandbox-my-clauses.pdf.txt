Arrangements with AI Service Providers under Framework Paragraph 21.3: A Terms
Review Checklist and Model Clauses
To: Submission of Edwin Lee & Partners on Public Consultation Paper No. 1/2026: Artificial Intelligence
(AI) and Personal Data Protection Framework
Why this Annexure has two parts
Paragraph 21.3 of the Framework lists seven matters that the arrangements between a data controller and an
AI service provider acting as a data processor should cover. The list is sound. The difficulty is that most
Malaysian controllers will never negotiate those arrangements. A company that subscribes to a global AI
service accepts the provider's standard terms and, usually, a published data processing addendum. What it
actually controls is the settings it chooses in the service and the records it keeps.
Part 1 is written for that controller. It is a checklist, keyed to paragraph 21.3(a) to (g), of what to look for in a
provider's standard terms, where to find it, what a good answer looks like, what to do if the answer is
missing or weak, and what to record. Used this way, the provider's terms and addendum, the controller's
configuration, and the controller's record together make up the "appropriate arrangements" paragraph 21.3
asks for (Core paragraph 7.10 and Recommendation 6(g)).
Part 2 is written for the smaller number of cases where the controller holds the pen: a local vendor, a systems
integrator, a Malaysian software product, or a custom build. It offers model clauses, keyed to the same seven
matters, as a starting point for non-mandatory clauses JPDP might publish.
Both parts are offered to the drafting team as starting points and can be used, adapted or set aside. In this
Annexure, "Controller" means the data controller, "Provider" means the AI service provider, "Service"
means the AI system, model, functionality or service supplied, and "Act 709" means the Personal Data
Protection Act 2010 as amended. Figures and terms that are for JPDP or the parties to decide are shown in
square brackets.
Part 1: Terms review checklist for standard-form AI services
How to use it. Work through the rows before any personal data goes into the Service. For each row, find the
term in the Provider's documents that answers the question and note where it is. Where no term answers it,
follow the "if missing or weak" column. Keep the completed checklist as part of the AI Processing Record
(Framework paragraph 40.4).
Where the answers usually are. Global providers typically publish, for their business and API customers:
an enterprise or commercial agreement; a data processing addendum (often called a DPA); a usage or
acceptable-use policy; a privacy policy; a list of sub-processors; a security or trust page; and an admin
console in which the customer sets retention, training and access options. Consumer plans usually have only
consumer terms and a privacy policy, and no DPA. The absence of a DPA is itself an answer: the Service is
not being offered on processor terms.

Part 2: Model clauses for negotiated arrangements
These clauses are for the case where the Controller's paper governs. They are keyed to paragraph 21.3(a) to
(g), with one further clause on cooperation and audit. They also serve as the benchmark for reading Part 1:
for each clause, the Part 1 question is whether the Provider's standard terms do the same work.
Para 21.3 Matter Model clause
(a) Nature and
purpose of the
processing
1. Processing. The Provider shall process personal data only to provide the Service to
the Controller as described in [Schedule], and for no other purpose. The Provider
shall not use personal data supplied by or on behalf of the Controller, or generated by
the Service from that data, to train, retrain, fine-tune, evaluate or improve any AI
model, whether its own or a third party's, unless the Controller has agreed to that use
in writing and has confirmed that it holds a basis under Act 709 for it.
(b) Categories of
personal data
2. Data. The categories of personal data and of data subjects to be processed are set
out in [Schedule]. The Provider shall not be required to process sensitive personal
data within the meaning of Act 709, including biometric data, unless [Schedule]
expressly provides for it and the Controller confirms that the conditions of section 40
of Act 709 are met.
(c) Instructions of
the Controller
3. Instructions. The Provider shall process personal data only on the Controller's
documented instructions, which include this Schedule and the Controller's
configuration of the Service. Where the Provider offers settings that control retention,
use or training on personal data, the Provider shall apply the settings the Controller
selects and shall not change them without the Controller's written agreement. If the
Provider considers that an instruction would contravene Act 709, it shall inform the
Controller promptly.
(d) Measures to
protect
personal data
4. Security. The Provider shall implement technical and organisational measures
appropriate to the risk, meeting at least the Personal Data Protection Standard 2015,
including encryption of personal data in transit and at rest, logical separation of the
Controller's data from other customers' data, access limited to personnel who need it
and who are bound by confidentiality, logging of access, and regular testing of those
measures. The Provider shall give the Controller, on request and not more than [once
a year], evidence of those measures, which may be a current certification or
independent assurance report.
(e) Other
processors and
third parties
5. Sub-processors. The Provider shall not engage another processor or disclose
personal data to any third party except as listed in [Schedule] or with the Controller's
prior written agreement. The Provider shall give at least [30] days' notice of any
change to that list, shall impose on each sub-processor obligations no less protective
than this Schedule, and shall remain responsible to the Controller for the sub-
processor's performance. Where any sub-processor processes personal data outside
Malaysia, the Provider shall tell the Controller the country concerned so that the
Controller can comply with section 129 of Act 709.

Para 21.3 Matter Model clause
(f) Security
incidents and
personal data
breaches
6. Breach notification. The Provider shall notify the Controller of any personal data
breach affecting the Controller's personal data without undue delay and in any event
within [24] hours of becoming aware of it, giving the information the Controller
needs to comply with section 12B of Act 709 and Circular No. 1/2025, including the
time the Provider became aware, the nature of the breach, the categories and
approximate number of data subjects and records affected, the AI system or model
and version involved, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.
The Provider shall supplement that information as it becomes available, cooperate
with the Controller's investigation and notifications, and not notify data subjects or
the Commissioner on the Controller's behalf unless the Controller asks it to.
(g) Return,
deletion or
disposal
7. Return and deletion. On termination or expiry of the Service, or earlier on the
Controller's request, the Provider shall at the Controller's option return the personal
data in a commonly used electronic format or securely delete it, and shall delete
remaining copies, including from backups within [the Provider's backup cycle, not
exceeding 90 days], except to the extent that Act 709 or other written law requires the
Provider to keep it. The Provider shall confirm deletion in writing. Personal data
processed through the Service shall not be kept by the Provider for longer than is
needed to provide the Service, unless the Controller instructs otherwise.
Framework
Part G; Core
9.5
Cooperation
and audit
8. Cooperation. The Provider shall assist the Controller in responding to data subject
requests under Act 709 relating to personal data processed through the Service,
including requests for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal data generated
by the Service, within a time that allows the Controller to meet its statutory deadlines.
The Provider shall make available the information reasonably needed to demonstrate
compliance with this Schedule, including model or system documentation for the
Service, and shall allow audits by the Controller or an independent auditor on
reasonable notice, not more than [once a year] unless a breach or regulatory request
requires otherwise.
A note on the notification window
The figure in row (f) of Part 1 and Clause 6 of Part 2 is bracketed because it is for JPDP to state (Core
Recommendation 8(d)). A window of 24 hours leaves the Controller 48 hours within the 72-hour period
under Circular No. 1/2025. In our experience that is the minimum a small controller needs to confirm what
happened, decide whether the threshold is met, and prepare the notification.
