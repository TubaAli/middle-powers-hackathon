# Plain single-assistant baseline

Evidence cutoff: **31 October 2026**. Decision horizon: **Geneva, 21–22 June 2027**. This is an analytical prioritisation, not stakeholder opinion, institutional consent or a calibrated forecast. Only the fixed packet and four referenced saved text extracts were used; no new retrieval or experiment was performed. Claim IDs below resolve to evidence and limitations in `baseline.json`.

## Recommendation

**Test Japan's procurement safety-evidence route next, through one Gen-nai application's version-specific release and fallback decision.** It has the clearest directly checked connection between an existing operational approval process, a prospective procurement opportunity and a useful activity that can be withheld or restored. Test whether that connection actually works before expanding the requirement. This is a recommendation for the next validation investment, not a conclusion that Japan prevents the most harm. [baseline-1–4]

The incremental safeguard is independently checked, version-bound model-behaviour evidence connected to the actual release decision—not another generic security review or an assumed government veto over a supplier's global model. Gen-nai already requires prior authorised approval, another developer's review and recovery including rollback; that second developer is not an external independent evaluator. Its standard also calls for migration testing and necessary-function continuity. These are obligations on paper, not a witnessed successful hold. [baseline-2]

## Strongest contrary evidence and reversal

**Japan's own supplier consultation is the strongest challenge.** Graffer describes unannounced upstream updates and forced model retirement outside an integrator's control; Graffer and Rakuten challenge access to upstream evidence. Official replies say evidence examples are not invariably mandatory, and disclosure is reasonably negotiated; they also retain potentially demanding expectations for important systems. A mature purchasing process therefore does not prove deliverable safety rights. DS-920 asks planners to consider risk-specific contractual provisions, rather than demonstrating signed acceptance of every example. [baseline-3–4]

Reverse the choice if the selected owner cannot lawfully withhold the relevant configuration, cannot obtain evidence sufficient to identify/test it, or cannot sustain useful service during a forced update without bypassing the gate. Also reverse if independent review exceeds a pre-agreed affordable workload or the added gate provides no improvement over existing review. **Prefer LUMI instead** if a competent host/allocator establishes a lawful common condition, scoped evaluator rights and two eligible national-channel projects with a costed pre-Geneva test. **Prefer the Singapore pair instead** if a real release owner and second recipient accept the same version-bound evidence and demonstrate lower total review effort without missed failures. These are future discriminators, not claimed agreements. [baseline-12]

## Assumptions and unresolved questions

- One suitable Gen-nai application and a still-changeable procurement or lawful internal acceptance record can be selected. Who exactly controls its release, credentials, contract and emergency override? [baseline-1–4]
- A non-developing reviewer can obtain sufficient traces without exporting sensitive prompts or supplier secrets. What are the actual quote, reviewer-hours, queue and recurring update burden? Missing staffing evidence is not evidence of incapacity. [baseline-3,11]
- Necessary administrative work has a usable manual or restricted-function fallback. Which people lose service, face erroneous refusals or cannot correct a result? Security success alone is insufficient. [baseline-2,11]
- How much real activity passes the gate, and what moves to other departmental tools or overseas APIs? A local gate does not bind worldwide model use. [baseline-11]
- For LUMI, who can approve the exact new requirement, and does it reach the selected projects without unnecessary disclosure or disruption to unrelated jobs? For Singapore, which national recipients recognise evidence rather than merely endorse cooperation? [baseline-5–7]

## One feasible discriminating test

**A cost-capped Gen-nai release/retirement rehearsal**, proposed for completion by **31 May 2027**, with a decision receipt by **1 June**. By **15 January**, first require the actual release delegation, applicable acceptance terms, protected-data permissions, named reviewer and a quoted time/cost cap. If unavailable, record a failed feasibility gate; do not label a mock approval operational validation. These dates are proposed milestones. [baseline-12–13]

Use a staging copy of one useful workflow, its genuine release pipeline and synthetic records. Freeze model endpoint/version evidence, application, retrieval data, tool permissions and evaluation harness. Blind the existing review and a separate reviewer to matched clean, deliberately faulty and repaired builds. Include one tool-side disclosure/unauthorised-action defect and a simulated upstream update or retirement. Observe whether the authorised owner rejects the faulty build, limits credentials, maintains legitimate work through fallback and permits the repaired build only after fresh held-out tests. Do not release a deliberately unsafe build to users. [baseline-13]

Predeclare the critical-defect rule, task-completion and correction standards, allowable false holds and review budget. Count missed defects, tool-side events, clean-task completion, recovery time, reviewer-hours, queue time, cash cost and bypass attempts. Require the critical seeded defect to be detected and held; evaluate broader performance against the declared thresholds rather than inventing a universal acceptable error rate. Failure to beat current review on meaningful protection, or loss of useful service beyond the agreed floor, defeats scaling. A staging pass establishes feasibility only: link the final acceptance to real configuration/activity logs before claiming operational coverage, and do not claim measured harm reduction from seeded tests. [baseline-11–13]

## Two useful follow-up designs

1. **A confidential evidence receipt with national acceptance fields.** Retain the configuration, test limits, unexplained disagreements, independent review and accept/hold/retest decision; keep sensitive content locally controlled. Trial reuse with one second purchaser and retain its own authority and context tests. Continue only if additional protected activity or saved total effort exceeds translation/retest burden. [baseline-14]
2. **A LUMI same-obligation, two-channel experiment.** Hold the safety requirement and workload type fixed; compare Belgian-only processing against a lawfully delegated common service annex with a second national channel. Measure elapsed approval, total staff effort, scoped job pause/restart, disclosure burden and migration off-platform. Neither Belgian nor national quota shares count as protected consumption. [baseline-15]

## Important minority alternative

**Validate Brazil's existing Grok corrective-evidence demand first** if the objective gives greater weight to immediate rights protection in an already identified harmful service. The rights shard reports an ANPD technical finding that claimed safeguards failed, a named file and a demand for verifiable evidence; this is a more concrete adverse case than prospective procurement. Its source page and full technical note were not independently available among the extracts inspected here, current disposition is unknown, and no successful retest is established. The historical long sanction duration does not measure the speed of this preventive demand. Access to the actual technical record and a current responsible case owner could make this the better next test. This alternative has substantial merit without invoking speculative frontier-risk spillovers. [baseline-8]

Other routes remain useful but do not displace the shortlist on this packet: finance lacks a demonstrated discretionary event and exact rights; India's documented commercial-continuation path challenges an assumed workload stop; Swissgrid warrants a bounded fallback replay but still lacks pilot authority and model-specific exposure; sandbox badges or training contracts do not establish operational holds. These are distinct unresolved mechanisms, not judgments of national competence. [baseline-9–10]
