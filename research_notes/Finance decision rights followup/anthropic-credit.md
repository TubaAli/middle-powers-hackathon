## Key Question 1: What decision rights does the credit agreement actually allocate, and to whom — are non-US lender allocations disclosed?

### Takeaway

The agreement is a Morgan Stanley-agented syndicated revolver that restates a May 14, 2025 facility. Seventeen institutions are named on the cover with arranger/bookrunner titles — including nine non-US-headquartered banks — but the actual Lender roster, per-lender commitments and the facility size are all undisclosed (omitted Schedule 2.01, redacted amounts, omitted signature pages). Voting power sits in "Required Lenders" holding **more than 50% of outstanding Incremental Term Loans plus Revolving Facility Commitments** (or, post-termination, credit exposure), with Defaulting Lenders disregarded. Every amendment requires the Borrower's written agreement plus the requisite lender vote; unanimity applies only to enumerated money-term and structural matters. No safety-linked right or obligation exists anywhere in the instrument.

### Inferences

- **Arranger titles are not lender status and not allocations.** The nine non-US-headquartered institutions named (Mizuho, MUFG, SMBC, BMO, RBC, TD Securities (USA), BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole CIB, plus Barclays (UK) and UBS/Deutsche securities affiliates) appear only with arranger/bookrunner titles; the operative creditors are "the Lenders party hereto", whose identities and shares are inside the omitted signature pages and Schedule 2.01. Any Leverage Map edge from this agreement must carry per-holder allocation = null, and "named non-US lender is an actual Lender" = unverified.
- **The coalition arithmetic is genuinely non-computable from the filing.** Required Lenders = >50% of an undisclosed denominator. Even if all nine non-US-headquartered named institutions were Lenders, nothing in the public record shows whether they approach half of total commitments. The prior audit's caution ("Named arranger roles do not establish each bank's funded share or a coalition majority") is confirmed and sharpened: not merely unfunded-share uncertainty, but complete absence of any commitment figure.
- **Borrower consent is a hard gate on every use of lender power**: no amendment, waiver, or covenant addition binds Anthropic without its written agreement (§9.08(b)), and Anthropic holds multiple unilateral exit/replacement rights (see KQ2–KQ3). Conversely, no bank or coalition can force a safety covenant on Anthropic through this instrument.
- **Covenant policy sits at Required-Lenders level by design**: because covenant amendments are expressly excluded from the unanimity clauses, a >50% coalition plus the Borrower could add (or strip) covenants — including a safety-reporting covenant — over the objection of every other lender. This is the strongest existing collective right in the instrument, and it runs both ways (it also lets a willing majority remove conditions).
- Bank nationality establishes nothing: the agreement is New York law, administered by Morgan Stanley; no provision references any lender's home-state authority. The only middle-power-relevant channels are the banks' own internal credit policies (KQ3) and lawful regulator disclosure (KQ4), not state direction.

## Key Question 2: Can lenders block existing committed draws — or only decline new credit?

### Takeaway

**Committed compliant draws cannot be blocked by any individual lender or coalition.** Draw conditions are objective (borrowing request, representations true, no Default), funding is mechanical, and a lender that refuses to fund becomes a "Defaulting Lender" that loses voting rights and can be replaced. What lenders hold in "sole discretion" is the opposite category: *new* credit — incremental commitments (§2.21), maturity extensions of their own commitments (§2.22), and refinancing participation (§2.23) — plus issuing-bank carve-outs on Letters of Credit. The filed instrument's own text establishes the distinction the task requires: no obligation to extend new credit coexists with an unconditional obligation to fund compliant draws.

### Inferences

- **The "block a draw" theory fails on the instrument's face.** Any strategy premised on a named non-US bank withholding funding on a compliant Borrowing Request is self-defeating under the filed text: the bank becomes a Defaulting Lender, loses its vote toward Required Lenders, funds the amount with penalty interest anyway (§2.06(b)), and can be replaced. The prior synthesis line "a draw schedule ... does not establish a fresh discretionary veto over a compliant borrower" is now verifiable at clause level.
- **The bank's real discretionary decisions are all forward-looking**: whether to provide Incremental Commitments (§2.21), extend its own commitment (§2.22), participate in a Replacement Revolving Facility (§2.23), or issue a Letter of Credit (with policy carve-outs, §2.05). This is where a safety condition could attach — as a condition of *new* money, never as a brake on *committed* money.
- The Collective-action design favors the Borrower: unanimity is confined to money terms and voting mechanics; covenant-level demands are Required-Lenders matters; and holdouts on genuine unanimity items can be forcibly replaced (§2.19(c)). Anthropic also controls who can even become a Lender (consents in §9.04(b), the DQ list in §9.04(h)) and can shrink the facility at will (§2.08).
- Assignments are open in principle to any "commercial bank, any insurance company, any finance company, any financial institution, any fund that invests in loans and any other 'accredited investor'" (§1.01 "Eligible Transferee") — no nationality bar — but each assignment needs Borrower consent (absent a Specified Event of Default), Administrative Agent and (for revolving) Issuing Bank consents, $10,000,000 minimum/$5,000,000 increments, and is void toward Disqualified/Defaulting Lenders (§9.04(b)). A coalition cannot quietly accumulate voting share: the Register is maintained by the Administrative Agent "as a non-fiduciary agent of the Borrower" and assignments are effective only when recorded (§9.04(b)(iii)).

## Key Question 3: Which prospective windows could carry negotiated safety terms, and what are the consent bottlenecks (incremental credit, extensions, refinancing, maturity)?

### Takeaway

The agreement builds in three Borrower-initiated new-money windows — Incremental Commitments (§2.21, headroom at least $7.5bn and possibly unlimited), Extensions (§2.22) and Refinancing Amendments (§2.23) — plus a five-year runway to the September 4, 2031 Stated Maturity. Each requires the participating lender's sole-discretion election, which is the genuine decision point a willing named bank (if it is a Lender) could condition on safety-reporting terms. But every window is Borrower-gated and constraint-wrapped: only the Borrower may open it; incremental revolving terms materially more restrictive than the existing class need Administrative Agent sign-off or post-maturity application; extension offers must be pro rata and same-terms to the whole class; refinancing terms may not be more restrictive to the Borrower unless the Borrower elects or the Administrative Agent accepts; and all three require no Default and true representations. A one-bank bespoke covenant is therefore possible only inside a Borrower-requested incremental facility — not via extensions or bilateral refinancing.

### Cited Findings

- **Incremental (§2.21):** "the Borrower may, by written notice to the Administrative Agent ... request Incremental Term Loan Commitments and/or Incremental Revolving Facility Commitments, in an amount not to exceed the Incremental Amount" from "one or more Incremental Lenders ... willing to provide such ... Commitments ... in their sole discretion", each subject to Administrative Agent (and, for revolving, Issuing Bank) approval "not ... unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed". §2.21(b)(i): "the interest rate and fees in connection with any Incremental Commitments shall be determined by the Borrower and the Incremental Lenders in their sole discretion". An Incremental Revolving Facility that is a new Class with "terms ... materially more restrictive (taken as a whole) than the then outstanding Class of Revolving Facility Commitments" must "(I) be reasonably satisfactory to the Administrative Agent, (II) be included for the benefit of the holders of the Revolving Facility Commitments or (III) apply only after the latest Stated Maturity Date". A withdrawing Issuing Bank's exit must be "on terms and conditions reasonably satisfactory to such Issuing Bank in its sole discretion". — [EX-10.10 §2.23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Maturity and availability:** §1.01 "Maturity Date" — "with respect to the Revolving Facility in effect on the Closing Date, the fifth (5th) anniversary of the Closing Date (the 'Stated Maturity Date')" (i.e. 4 September 2031), with a springing mechanism if Scheduled Principal Payments above $500,000,000 fall due within 91 days unless Liquidity covers them; §2.08(a): "the Revolving Facility Commitments of each Class shall automatically and permanently terminate on the applicable Maturity Date"; "Availability Period" runs from Closing Date to that Maturity Date. — [EX-10.10 §1.01; §2.08(a)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Security sunset (collateral/guarantee structure):** §1.01 "Guarantee and Collateral Release Date" is "the first day on which the Borrower (i) has obtained a Debt Rating that is an Investment Grade Rating from at least two of S&P, Moody's and Fitch, (ii) is in compliance, on a Pro Forma Basis, with the last paragraph of Section 6.01 and (iii) shall have provided the Administrative Agent written notice that it wishes to trigger the occurrence of the Guarantee and Collateral Release Date (it being understood that absent any such written notice by the Borrower, the Guarantee and Collateral Release Date shall not occur)". Pre-Release the facility is secured and guaranteed: the Collateral and Guarantee Requirement (§1.01) mandates executed Collateral and Guarantee Agreements, pledges of "all outstanding Equity Interests directly owned by the Loan Parties" and intercompany Indebtedness, and perfection including "filings with the ... United States Copyright Office and the United States Patent and Trademark Office" (cl. (e)) — IP filings are within the lien perimeter. Affiliate-transaction (§6.10) and security-invalidity (§7.01(l)) covenants apply only "prior to the Guarantee and Collateral Release Date". Pricing pre-Release is flat (Term Benchmark/RFR 1.000%, ABR 0.000%, commitment fee 0.10%) and becomes a Debt Rating grid afterwards (§1.01 "Applicable Margin"/"Applicable Commitment Fee"). — [EX-10.10 §1.01; §6.10; §7.01(l)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Financial covenant:** §6.09: "Permit Liquidity to be less than $5,000,000,000 as of the last day of any fiscal quarter beginning with the fiscal quarter ending December 31, 2026." "Liquidity" for this purpose is the Unrestricted Cash Amount only (clause (b) — unused commitments — excluded); Unrestricted Cash is cash and Permitted Investments "that would not appear as 'restricted'", capped at $10,000,000,000 for leverage tests, excluding Financing SPV cash (§1.01). Breach of an Article VI covenant is an immediate Event of Default with no cure period (§7.01(d)). — [EX-10.10 §6.09; §1.01; §7.01(d)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Cross-default perimeter:** §7.01(f) fires on default/acceleration of "Material Indebtedness" — borrowed-money debt over $1,000,000,000 — but expressly **excludes** "Indebtedness arising under any Qualified Receivables Facility or Indebtedness of a Financing SPV arising under any Permitted Contracted Revenue Financing or **Compute Financing Obligations of a Compute SPV** or any Guarantee in respect thereof". §1.01 "Helix Transaction Documents" confirms the OpCo I stack: "Master Lease Agreement entered into by, among others, AI Infrastructure OpCo, LLC, dated as of August 13, 2025 and ... that certain Note Purchase Agreement ... by, among others, Anthropic, PBC, dated as of August 13, 2025; provided that such transaction documents shall, in each case, be on terms consistent with the summary term sheet provided to the Lenders prior to the Closing Date." — [EX-10.10 §7.01(f); §1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Change-of-control tripwire:** §7.01(g) — an Event of Default occurs if "there shall have occurred a Change of Control": acquisition by any person or group "other than one or more Permitted Holders" of "50.0% or more of the aggregate ordinary voting or designation power with respect to the election of the Board of Directors". "Permitted Investor" means "(a) the Class T Holders" (defined as the Anthropic Long-Term Benefit Trust), "(b) the Founders and (c) any other direct or indirect equityholder in the Borrower on the Closing Date with holdings that represent, when aggregated with all of its Affiliates, more than 10% of the fully diluted Equity Interests of the Borrower on the Closing Date". — [EX-10.10 §7.01(g); §1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)

### Inferences

- **The one genuinely openable negotiation channel is a Borrower-requested Incremental Facility** (§2.21): it is the only window where the willing lender and the Borrower fix terms "in their sole discretion", where new covenants can bind a defined tranche, and where headroom is objectively large ($7.5bn-plus floor; up to $20bn linked to equity proceeds — which an IPO closing would generate via "Qualified Equity Interests" cash; unlimited at ≤3.75x/≤5.50x leverage). The consent bottleneck chain is: Anthropic must ask (no lender can compel a request) → Anthropic must agree to the covenant (no lender can force it) → the Administrative Agent (Morgan Stanley) must approve the lender and be "reasonably satisfied" if the tranche is materially more restrictive, or the restrictions must apply post-maturity or benefit existing holders → no Default and true representations at effectiveness.
- **Extensions and refinancings are deliberately anti-bespoke**: pro rata, same-terms offers (§2.22) and a "no more restrictive to the Borrower" standard (§2.23) mean a single bank cannot extract a safety covenant by threatening not to extend — it can only decline, and Anthropic can replace the capacity via §2.23 or the §6.01 debt baskets (substitution is cheap given the repo-documented alternatives: Amazon's $20bn facility, Google and Broadcom note programs — [financing-audit.md](../Financial%20leverage%20for%20safer%20AI/financing-audit.md)).
- **The Collateral/Release architecture cuts against treating security as a standing safety lever**: the liens (which reach equity, intercompany debt and IP filings) exist for the lenders' benefit but expire whenever Anthropic elects release at investment-grade ratings — an Anthropic-controlled sunset, not an IPO-triggered one. Nothing in the credit agreement changes automatically at the S-1 offering: "Qualified IPO" (§1.01) matters only through the Contribution Incremental Amount and reporting substitutions (§5.04(d)), not through any lender right.
- **The Change-of-Control EoD protects Anthropic's mission/founder control**, not lenders' safety oversight: it accelerates the loan if anyone *outside* the Trust, the Founders and the closing-date >10% holders takes 50% voting power. It gives lenders a governance-change remedy, not a safety remedy.
- Timing map: the realistic decision windows are (1) any Borrower-requested incremental (date unknown; Borrower-controlled), (2) any extension offer cycle (Borrower-controlled), (3) refinancing around the 4 September 2031 Stated Maturity (or earlier by choice), and (4) per-draw LC issuance requests (continuous). None is dated in the public record; none is coalition-triggerable.

### Gaps

- **Current Incremental Amount utilization and Consolidated Total Assets: unknown** — the "Model" used for covenant calculations is "the financial model delivered by the Borrower to the Arrangers on August 11, 2026" (§1.01), not public; no financial statements for Anthropic are filed yet.
- The "summary term sheet provided to the Lenders prior to the Closing Date" for the Helix/OpCo I documents is not public; the actual Atlas/Helix credit documents are not filed in this S-1 (the prior audit located the Atlas Credit Agreement only as a cross-reference in EX-10.11).
- The last paragraph of §6.01 (referenced in the Release Date test) was not extracted; the exact secured-debt compliance condition for release is unverified in this pass.
- Whether the IPO closing (if it occurs) counts toward the Contribution Incremental Amount, and how much, depends on proceeds and the 65%/$20bn cap — no proceeds figure exists pre-pricing.

## Key Question 4: Do existing information covenants or any other provisions reach AI safety?

### Takeaway

**No.** A full-text scan of the filed agreement finds zero AI-safety, model, weights, or machine-learning provisions: "safety" appears only three times, all in environmental/health-and-safety boilerplate inside lien and debt carve-outs; the only "Model" is a financial projection model; "weights" never appears. The information covenants are strictly financial (annual audited statements within 160 days, quarterly within 60 days, Compliance Certificates, SEC filings once public, and "other customary information" on Administrative Agent request), with an express carve-out for "non-financial trade secrets or non-financial proprietary information" — which would shield model/safety information. The nearest existing analogues to a safety condition are all lender-protective, not safety-protective: MAE notices (financial materiality standard), the Material-IP-inside-the-perimeter covenant (§5.12), and the Change-of-Control EoD.

### Cited Findings

- **Vocabulary scan (whole document):** pattern `safety|artificial intelligence|model weight|AI safety` → 3 matches, all environmental ("the protection of human health or safety" in the "Environmental Liability" definition; "bonds ... to secure health, safety and environmental obligations in the ordinary course of business" in §6.01(f) and §6.02(g)); `machine learning` → 0; `weights` → 0; `models` → 3, all meaning "the financial model delivered by the Borrower to the Arrangers on August 11, 2026" (§1.01 "Model"; its use in the Consolidated EBITDA definition). — [EX-10.10 §1.01; §6.01(f); §6.02(g)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **§5.04 (Financial Statements, Reports, Etc.):** annual audited statements "within 160 days after the end of each fiscal year (commencing with the fiscal year ending December 31, 2026)"; quarterly unaudited "within 60 days after the end of each of the first three fiscal quarters ... (commencing with the fiscal quarter ending September 30, 2026)"; a Compliance Certificate within five Business Days certifying no Default and computing §6.09; SEC reports "promptly after the same become publicly available" (or 10-K/10-Q substitution once public); and "(e) ... such other customary information regarding the operations, business affairs and financial condition of the Borrower or any of the Restricted Subsidiaries, or compliance with the terms of any Loan Document, as in each case the Administrative Agent may reasonably request in writing (for itself or on behalf of any Lender)". Final carve-out: the Borrower need not deliver or discuss anything that "constitutes non-financial trade secrets or non-financial proprietary information of the Borrower or its Subsidiaries and/or any of its customers and/or suppliers", or that is privileged or subject to third-party confidentiality. — [EX-10.10 §5.04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **§5.05 (Other Notices):** prompt notice of any Default and of "any other development ... that has had, or would reasonably be expected to have, a Material Adverse Effect" (non-public matters only); notice of the Borrower becoming a "'covered foreign person' as that term is used in the Outbound Investment Rules" (§5.05(b)); post-Release, notice of non-public Debt Rating changes. "Material Adverse Effect" is defined financially: "any material adverse effect on (a) the business or financial condition of the Borrower and the Restricted Subsidiaries, taken as a whole, or (b) the rights and remedies of the ... Agents ... and the Lenders under the Loan Documents" (§1.01). — [EX-10.10 §5.05; §1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **§5.07 (Records/Inspection):** the Administrative Agent may inspect "financial records and the properties" on reasonable notice; **any Lender** may do so only "upon the occurrence and during the continuance of an Event of Default"; discussions with officers and accountants; all "subject to reasonable requirements of confidentiality". — [EX-10.10 §5.07](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Nearest analogue to a safety condition — collateral-protection, not safety:** §5.12: "The Loan Parties shall not transfer (whether by assignment or exclusive license) to any Subsidiary that is not a Guarantor any of their respective Material Intellectual Property" — with "Material Intellectual Property" being "any Intellectual Property that is material to the business ... as determined by the Borrower in good faith" (§1.01). — [EX-10.10 §5.12; §1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Confidentiality/regulator channel (§9.16):** Credit Parties may disclose Confidential Information "(i) to the extent necessary to comply with applicable laws or any legal process or the requirements of any Governmental Authority purporting to have jurisdiction over such Credit Party", "(ii) as part of reporting or review procedures to, or examinations by, Governmental Authorities", and to advisors/rating agencies/remedy enforcers with Borrower consultation or consent; and "nothing in this confidentiality provision shall prohibit any Person from voluntarily disclosing or providing any information ... to any governmental, regulatory or self-regulatory organization". — [EX-10.10 §9.16](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)
- **Loan Parties/Guarantors:** the Guarantee Agreement (form Exhibit F, `[***]` omitted) is "dated as of the Closing Date, ... among the Borrower, each Guarantor and the Collateral Agent" (§1.01); "Material Subsidiary" and the Collateral and Guarantee Requirement tie Guarantors to the Loan Party group; "Compute SPV" and "Financing SPV" subsidiaries are structurally separated (excluded from Material Subsidiaries, from Material Indebtedness, and from Unrestricted Cash). — [EX-10.10 §1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1010-sx1.htm)

### Inferences

- **An "incident reporting" or "pre-release testing" obligation cannot be read into this agreement.** The reporting package is financial-condition reporting for credit monitoring; a safety incident would reach the lenders only if it rose to a financial "Material Adverse Effect" (Borrower-good-faith standard) or a misrepresentation/default. The trade-secret carve-out affirmatively protects non-financial proprietary information — the category into which model, evaluation and security information would fall.
- **A middle-power government's realistic informational reach is supervisory, not contractual**: through the named banks' home-country prudential reporting (lawfully permitted by §9.16(a)(i)–(ii) and the voluntary-disclosure paragraph), supervisors of Mizuho/MUFG/SMBC/BMO/RBC/TD/BNP/CA-CIB could lawfully learn facility-level facts their supervised banks know. That is an information channel to the banks, not a decision right over Anthropic — and it cannot be converted into one without new contract terms.
- The §5.12 Material-IP covenant is the only clause that constrains where Anthropic's "crown jewel" intangibles may sit (they must stay inside the Guarantor perimeter) — relevant to the "protected weights" condition as an *existing structural fact* (weights-associated IP cannot be moved to an Unrestricted Subsidiary without breach), but it protects collateral coverage, not safety, and "material" is Borrower-determined.
- The instrument confirms, rather than fills, the six-condition gap: none of the six demands (pre-release testing, incident reporting, protected weights, essential-user continuity, known chip locations, local stop with due process) has any counterpart clause here. Any such term would have to be *added* — via Borrower + Required Lenders amendment (§9.08(b)), a Borrower-requested incremental tranche (§2.21), or a replacement facility the Borrower elects to make more restrictive (§2.23).

### Gaps

- Whether the Guarantee/Collateral Agreements or any unfiled side letter contains anything safety-relevant cannot be verified from the filed versions (Exhibits F and I are `[***]`); absence in the filed agreement is verified, absence in unfiled documents is not.
- The full text of §5.10 (Additional Security; Further Assurances) and §6.01's final paragraph were not extracted in this pass; no safety-related content was indicated by the vocabulary scan, but they remain unread in part.
- The prior audit's note that the financial covenant was "Section 6.12" is corrected here: the Liquidity covenant is **§6.09** (p. 112); §6.10 is affiliate transactions — but only "prior to the Guarantee and Collateral Release Date" (a scope limit the earlier audit did not record).

## Key Question 5: Verdict on the one complete path, and the map-ready candidate

### Takeaway

**Verdict: PARTIAL — the path is traceable at every section level except the last link, which does not exist.** A named non-US bank (if it is an actual Lender) holds real, individually-exercisable decision authority over *new* credit — the sole-discretion elections in §§2.21/2.22/2.23 — inside real, dated structural windows (any Borrower-initiated incremental; extension offers; refinancing before the 4 September 2031 Stated Maturity). But (1) **no existing enforceable safety condition exists anywhere in the instrument** (verified by full-text scan), so no lender's present rights reach one; (2) the final link can only be *created* prospectively, and every creation route requires **Anthropic's own consent** — the coalition can offer, never compel; (3) the coalition-arithmetic link (assembling Required Lenders) is non-computable from public filings because allocations and even the lender roster are omitted. The path is therefore a **borrower-gated prospective negotiation channel, not an existing enforceable safeguard**: a partial pass that fails specifically on "existing enforceable safety condition" and on "coalition can constitute the requisite consent".
