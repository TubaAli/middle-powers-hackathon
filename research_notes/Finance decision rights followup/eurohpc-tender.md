## 1. What is the complete documented chain from legal basis to signed contract, and who holds which rights (authority, lots, deadlines, award vs call-off, budget, membership)?

### Takeaway

The chain from a dated legal authorisation to an enforceable, remedied contract is fully documented from primary sources up to the published call: Council Regulation (EU) 2026/150 (16 January 2026) empowers the EuroHPC JU to run AI-gigafactory joint procurement with Participating States; the JU Governing Board adopted the call text and the draft Framework Contract (GB Decision 34/2026, 9 July 2026), approved the model Joint Procurement Agreement (GB Decision 30/2026, 1 June 2026) and approved launch (GB Decision 47/2026, 22 July 2026); the call was published 30 July 2026 (TED OJ S 145/2026) with two lots, a EUR 10 billion estimated value, and specific decision rights allocated to the Governing Board and to a per-facility Public Governance Body. The final link — the actual Framework Contract text approved as Annex 2 to GB Decision 34/2026 — is referenced but not published in the decision PDF, and is accessible only through the F&T portal, which this session could not render.

### Cited Findings

Legal basis and objectives:

- Council Regulation (EU) 2026/150 of 16 January 2026 (published 19 January 2026, OJ L 2026/150) amends Regulation (EU) 2021/1173 and inserts **Article 12b** on AI gigafactories. Read in full from the Official Journal HTML via the Publications Office CELLAR service. ([Regulation, full text](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150); ELI: http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/150/oj; adoption/publication dates also confirmed in [GB Decision 30/2026](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/document/download/fbdaaacf-ac6b-4a52-94d3-9689a9848bc2_en?filename=Decision%2030.2026%20Approval%20of%20the%20JPA%20for%20AI%20Gigafactories.pdf))
- Article 12b(1): an AIGF must be located in a Participating State that is a Member State; it is financed by a partnership between the Union and one or more Participating States (represented by the JU) and an AIGF Consortium legally represented by an AIGF Coordinator; the JU–Coordinator partnership "shall take the form of a **Hosting Agreement**"; each contributing Participating State concludes an administrative agreement with the JU covering payments, reporting and — verbatim — "the agreed access time of the Participating State, the schedule for access, conditions of payment, **reporting requirements and audit requirements**." ([Reg. 2026/150, Art. 12b(1)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))
- Article 12b(4): AIGFs "shall be selected based on a **joint procurement** between the Joint Undertaking and one or more contracting authorities from the Participating States"; a consortium needs an explicit Member State financial commitment provided **before** the call launch. Article 12b(19): the Governing Board selects consortia "through a fair and transparent process, with the support of a panel of independent experts and of an **accredited financial institution**", on criteria including technical quality, infrastructure, "quality of service, including **security and trustworthiness**", impact, and financial feasibility. ([Reg. 2026/150](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))
- Article 12b(5): Union contribution covers up to **17% of CAPEX** of the overall computing infrastructure, or takes the form of a pre-agreed guaranteed purchase of access time of equivalent value; Participating States must at least match; consortium covers the rest plus OPEX. ([Reg. 2026/150, Art. 12b(5)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))
- Statutes amendments in the same Regulation: Governing Board tasks for AIGFs (Art. 7(4a)) include **(d) approve the selection of the AIGF Consortia**, **(e) determine the conditions of the Union's access time**, (f) decisions on the AIGF public governance body, (g) approve framework contracts for essential components. Voting: for tasks (a)–(e) and (g), the remaining 50% of votes are held by Participating States that are Member States, with a **qualified majority = Union + at least 55% of those states comprising at least 65% of their population** (Statutes Art. 6(5a)); for task (f), votes are distributed **in proportion to committed financial contributions to that AIGF** and decisions require **at least 75% of all votes, including absent members** (Statutes Art. 6(5b)). ([Reg. 2026/150](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))

### Inferences

- The "decision rights" on this file are unusually well documented and layered: the **Governing Board** (Union + Participating States that are Member States, with a double qualified majority for AIGF tasks, and a 75% contribution-weighted majority for public-governance-body decisions) holds the rights to approve launch, approve the selection (award), determine the conditions of the Union's access time, and approve framework contracts for essential components; a **per-AIGF Public Governance Body** (Commission + funding Participating States) holds ongoing prior-approval rights; the **Executive Director** manages the procedure within Board-approved texts. A middle-power coalition that is already inside EuroHPC governance (e.g., Nordic, Baltic, Iberian and central-European states among the 18 JPA signatories; Canada and the UK as Participating States outside the joint procurement) has a named, dated channel to influence requirements — but only through the documented instruments (Board adoption before launch; clarification/corrigendum during the procedure; access-time conditions and Specific-Contract negotiation after award).
- The EUR 10bn TED "value" is the public offtake (access-time purchases), not a grant pool; the enforcement currency of this procedure is **conditional purchase and contractual remedies**, which is exactly the shape the six-condition coalition proposal needs. The 17%-of-CAPEX cap plus the at-least-matching rule means public money is a minority anchor; the guaranteed, remunerated reserved capacity (confirmed reserved capacity is paid whether or not fully used **[via tracker Q&A 106467]**) is the credible incentive that makes award conditions worth accepting for consortia that must raise EUR 4–5bn privately. ([TED](https://ted.europa.eu/en/notice/529135-2026/html); [Reg. 12b(5)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150); [tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/))
- The regulation deliberately separates two instruments: the **Framework Contract/Specific Contracts** (the joint procurement buying access time) and the **Hosting Agreement** (JU–Coordinator partnership, governed by Union law, which Article 12b(17) mandates must include "provisions ensuring the effective and proportionate scrutiny and control of the AI gigafactory by the Union for safeguarding the Union's strategic assets, interests, autonomy or security" plus detailed governance, access, accounting and service-level terms). Any safety-condition strategy should therefore be drafted against **both** instruments, not only the procurement contract. ([Reg. 2026/150, Art. 12b(1), (17)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))

## 2. What assurance, security, access, incident, audit, exclusion, subcontractor and flow-down provisions are already documented, and who can require changes before award under what constraints?

### Takeaway

The documented package already contains enforceable sovereignty, security, control, service-level and audit machinery with named remedies — EU data residency and jurisdiction, protection from unauthorised third-country access, EU-control assessment of the AIGF Coordinator, a Public Governance Body with prior-approval rights over third-country access agreements and structural changes, annual benchmark floors with lump-sum damages, price reduction and termination, and technical audits/validation before each phase. What is **not** documented anywhere in the accessible record is any AI-model-safety obligation (evaluator access, incident reporting, weight custody, user continuity, or a scoped stop power): the enforceable obligations stop at infrastructure sovereignty and service quality.

### Cited Findings

Sovereignty, security and control requirements (primary, from the regulation and GB decisions; tender-pack details via S&C):

- Article 12b(3): participation of legal entities from non-Participating States "shall be subject to restrictions or exclusion where such participation is considered contrary to the Union's strategic assets, interests, autonomy or security"; the call may admit entities from listed associated/third countries provided they comply with requirements "to guarantee the protection of the security interests of the Union and the Member States, and to safeguard classified information", set out in the work programme; entities of third countries with an **AI gigafactory Cooperation Agreement** with the Union are in principle exempt from those restrictions. ([Reg. 2026/150, Art. 12b(3)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))
- Article 12b(18): each AIGF has a **public governance body composed of representatives from the Commission and the Participating States providing public funding** to that AIGF; without prejudice to the consortium's management and operational autonomy, the following "shall require explicit prior approval": (a) "any proposed **access agreements with entities from third countries** that might raise concerns regarding the Union's strategic assets, interests, autonomy or security"; (b) "substantial changes to the legal and financial structure or control of the AI gigafactory … such as a change in its **ultimate ownership or control, any relocation of critical assets outside the Union** or major financial restructuring decisions"; (c) "significant change in the strategic purpose". ([Reg. 2026/150, Art. 12b(18)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))
- Article 12b(19): the technical award criteria include "quality of service, including **security and trustworthiness**". Article 12b(20): where the consortium selects suppliers itself, tender specifications must "address the security and resilience of the supply chain". Article 12b(17)(b): the Hosting Agreement must include "provisions ensuring the **effective and proportionate scrutiny and control** of the AI gigafactory by the Union". ([Reg. 2026/150](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026R0150))
- Tender-pack requirements **[via S&C]**: core AIGF operational data must be stored and processed in the EU under Member-State jurisdiction; infrastructure "must be protected from unauthorised third-country access or control"; each tenderer must appoint an EU-incorporated, EU-headquartered, effectively EU-controlled **AIGF Coordinator** (control = decisive influence assessed across the ownership chain, voting/veto rights, governance and financial dependence; minority blocking rights can count as negative control — Annex 1F(A) §§3.1–3.3, pp. 113–121); an EU-based **Hosting Entity**; all sites in EU Member States; **core technical operations and data processing cannot be subcontracted outside the EU**; customers of the AIGF "may retain operational autonomy over their partitioned computing capacity", which may place those customer-side activities outside certain AIGF-level sovereignty and data-residency requirements (underlying infrastructure still must prevent data leakage/unauthorised access). ([S&C memo](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/EuroHPC-Joint-Undertaking-Launches-AI-Gigafactories-Call))
- Offtake mechanics **[via S&C]**: purchased access split 50/50 Layer 2/Layer 3 (Lot 1) and 60/40 (Lot 2) under the first Specific Contract, adjustable ±10% by the contracting authorities; service mix in later Specific Contracts "may be adapted to meet evolving requirements at the relevant time"; Phase 1 prices fixed for the duration of the first Specific Contract; Phase 2 prices under the call's indexation mechanism. ([S&C memo](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/EuroHPC-Joint-Undertaking-Launches-AI-Gigafactories-Call))
- Official clarification (14 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 107399]**: **all maintenance tasks and activities** must meet the call's security requirements; data must be stored and processed exclusively in EU territory, subject to European standards and to the jurisdiction of a Member State. ([tracker summary of Q&A 107399](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/), original at [F&T proc-qa 107399](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/support/proc-qa/bc712395-692d-41ab-aa25-8e254ae86eb2-CN/107399))

Service-level assurance, verification and remedies (tender pack + official Q&A via tracker):

- Annual benchmark tests (at least once per year per Annex II) must not fall below **90% of the performance measured at first installation**; a breach is an SLA violation that can trigger **lump-sum damages under Framework Contract Article II.15 and/or a price reduction under Article II.16**, and the contracting authority may **terminate the Framework Contract or a running Specific Contract under Article II.18**. Official clarification of 9 October 2026 **[via tracker, Q&A 106705]**. ([tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/), original at [F&T proc-qa 106705](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/support/proc-qa/bc712395-692d-41ab-aa25-8e254ae86eb2-CN/106705))
- The annual cap for lump-sum damages is **10% of the annual value of the Specific Contract, in aggregate**; service credits, catch-up capacity and additional compute-capacity credits fall outside the cap (20 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 107989]**. Liability generally capped at **3× the total value of the affected Specific Contract**, with carve-outs for gross negligence, intent, personal injury and certain IP infringements (5 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 106075/106076]**; joint tenderers are jointly and severally liable; an SPV bidder carries its own liability and must be incorporated and registered before the tender deadline (20 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 107945/107946]**. ([tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/))
- **Technical audits** gate operational readiness (Phase 1, no later than 18 months after signature) and Phase 2 activation **[via S&C, citing specs pp. 17–18, 24]**; before access time is provided in each phase the contractor must confirm compliance with the tendered specifications and the contracting authority runs **technical validation tests**; the offered accelerator model is contractually binding and may only change with the contracting authority's **prior written consent**, demonstrable benefits, preserved specifications, and without changing "the original conditions of the procurement procedure" (14 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 107371]**. ([S&C memo](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/EuroHPC-Joint-Undertaking-Launches-AI-Gigafactories-Call); [tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/))
- EU-control obligations are continuous: ownership/control declarations are due with the tender, must already hold at the tender deadline, later changes during evaluation need the authority's consent, and during performance a contractor that falls under the control of a non-eligible state or company can see the contract terminated; substantial changes to legal, financial or control structure need the **explicit consent of the Public Governance Body** (7 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 106485/106486]**. The Public Governance Body is composed of one representative of the Commission and of each national contracting authority and approves substantial changes to legal/financial/ownership structure and strategic purpose (20 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 107993]**. ([tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/))
- Assurance of the *physical and commercial* base: Phase 1 prices must be offered for the whole Framework Contract term and are not re-set at operational milestone T0 (20 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 107980]**; Phase 2 is a **mandatory, integral** part of the offer and the Framework Contract — it cannot be tendered as an option, and refusal after funding confirmation triggers damages and termination of the whole Framework Contract (7 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 106478]**; there is no guaranteed annual minimum of chip-hours for the buyers, but confirmed reserved capacity is remunerated whether used or not, with up to 20% annual rollover (7 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 106465/106467]**. ([tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/))
- Confidentiality/disclosure: opened tenders become the contracting authority's property and are treated confidentially; after the award decision non-excluded bidders may on written request learn the winner's name, the winning offer's characteristics and relative advantages and total price; trade secrets, unit prices and competitively sensitive data can be withheld or redacted (9 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 106702]**. ([tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/))

Subcontractor, financier and developer flow-down:

- **Subcontractors**: all subcontractors must be identified by name in the offer with commitment declarations; main subcontractors with more than **20% economic share** face the evidentiary rules described in the tender documents; two separately submitted offers cannot be merged after the deadline (7 October 2026) **[via tracker, Q&A 106450/106453/106484]**. Core technical operations and data processing cannot be subcontracted outside the EU **[via S&C]**. ([tracker](https://euaigf.b2badgen.com/); [S&C memo](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/EuroHPC-Joint-Undertaking-Launches-AI-Gigafactories-Call))
- **Financier flow-down**: the call does not prohibit non-EU debt or equity, but financing must not give a non-EU person direct or indirect control over the AIGF Coordinator or compromise EU control, jurisdiction, autonomy or security; the control assessment covers equity, loans, guarantees, debt waivers, grants and related **governance, consent, veto and enforcement rights**; if the tenderer relies on a financier's financial capacity, the contracting authorities may require the financier to **become a party to the Framework and/or Specific Contract or provide a joint and several first-call guarantee** for performance **[via S&C, citing specs pp. 34, 38, 87–88]**. ([S&C memo](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/EuroHPC-Joint-Undertaking-Launches-AI-Gigafactories-Call))
- **Model developers / AI tenants as such are not obligors** in the accessible record: the procurement buys compute access and platform/software/model services; no clause requires an AIGF customer or model developer training on the facility to accept evaluator access, incident reporting, weight-custody, continuity or isolation obligations. The sole indirect hooks are infrastructure-level (data residency, no unauthorised access, security requirements extending to maintenance) and the customer-partition autonomy noted above, which limits how far AIGF-level rules reach into what customers run. ([TED](https://ted.europa.eu/en/notice/529135-2026/html); [S&C memo fn30](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/EuroHPC-Joint-Undertaking-Launches-AI-Gigafactories-Call))

Who can require changes before award, and documented constraints:

### Inferences

- The existing machinery is genuinely enforceable but aimed at **infrastructure sovereignty and service quality**, not at the behaviour of AI models trained on the machines. The closest existing analogue to a "safety-like" condition is the Art. 12b(18) prior-approval regime (third-country access agreements, ownership/control changes, relocation of critical assets, strategic-purpose changes) plus the continuous EU-control regime with termination as the backstop. A coalition wanting model-safety terms has to add them; it cannot read them out of what is documented today.
- Three lawful insertion points are visible in the documents themselves: (1) **before the Governing Board adopts future call texts** (this route has now closed for EUROHPC-2026-CEI-AIGF-01 — the text was adopted 9 July 2026 — but the Board will "determine the conditions of the Union's access time" and approve future work-programme/strategy documents); (2) **clarifications/corrigenda during the procedure**, constrained to interpretations and extensions that do not alter the original conditions; (3) **post-award discretionary decisions** — GB access-time conditions under Art. 12b(14) (including "specific rules … for the allocation of the access time of the Union for projects and activities related to security"), Phase-2 Specific Contracts that are not yet concluded and whose service mix "may be adapted to meet evolving requirements", and Hosting-Agreement terms under Art. 12b(17). These are inference-level openings from documented texts, not established rights to insert any particular clause.
- The **financier flow-down** clause (authorities may require a financier to become a contract party or guarantee performance when the tenderer relies on its capacity) is the most direct documented bridge between "finance leverage" and "contractual obligation" on this file — it shows the drafters already contemplate pulling non-tenderer capital into the contract perimeter. It is not a safety clause, but it is a procedural precedent for third-party flow-down into the FWC/Specific Contracts. ([S&C memo fn37](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/EuroHPC-Joint-Undertaking-Launches-AI-Gigafactories-Call))

### Gaps

- No incident-reporting, model-evaluation, weight-custody, model-continuity or workload-isolation clause was found in any accessible document; whether the unread Tender Specifications/FWC contain anything relevant under their security or "security and trustworthiness" headings is **unknown and must not be assumed either way**. The FWC's Articles II.15/II.16/II.18 are known only through Q&A summaries and S&C's article references; their full text, and the SLA/Annex II wording, remain unread.
