# Quantitative measurement for Routes to AI Safeguards

Observation date: **31 October 2026**. Owner scope: measurement design and empirical demonstrations only. The 21–22 June 2027 Geneva deadline is the assignment's planning target, not an independently verified event date here. Companion: `measurement.json`. This is a proposed replacement method, not a rescoring of the live routes.

## 1. What should the two continuous axes actually measure?

### Takeaway

Use **specified-population safeguard coverage** vertically and **verified required-approval coverage** horizontally, backed by a graph of who must authorize, sign and deliver. Keep veto blockers, leaders' documented alignment and critical-path timing visible beside the ratio. These are coverage and agreement readiness, not measured safety impact and probability of success. **None of the numerical examples below establishes a current route's operational safeguard coverage, agreement readiness or probability of delivery by Geneva.**

### Cited Findings

- The existing explorer's impact and feasibility values are explicitly editorial ordinal judgments; the source-layer weights and replacement years do not supply a measured policy-effect denominator. Local sources: [`data/routes.json`](../../data/routes.json), [`data/layers.json`](../../data/layers.json), [`DESIGN.md`](../../DESIGN.md).
- Actual quantities can describe different things: an official LUMI access call offers a GPU-hour envelope; EuroHPC reports allocated projects; ASML reports sales; OECD reports procurement relative to GDP. None is itself a causal safety-effect estimate. Sources and exact locators are in section 2, S1–S5.

### Inferences — proposed product and measurement contract

**Executive default:** show the title “Safeguard reach × agreement readiness,” separate coverage and approval-denominator selectors, an as-of date, and a target deadline. Show “unknown” off the plot rather than at zero. Each point says: “X of Y [coverage units] are subject to [obligation]; A of N required approval acts evidenced; [named blockers].” Compare only compatible populations, periods, enforcement states and approval-counting conventions. Separate panels for GDP/GVA, single-stage supply chain and policy-subject coverage prevent switching denominators silently. Forecasts, when available, occupy an explicitly different horizontal-axis mode.

**Vertical axis, operational coverage:**

`C = sum(w_i × covered_i) / sum(w_i)`

The population is a frozen eligible inventory; `covered_i = 1` only when the relevant obligation is in force for unit i, an evidence route exists, and a usable remedy is identified. Record these components separately. Weights are observed quantities, not judgments of strategic importance. The denominator must be positive. A counterfactual proposed scope is allowed as a visibly different `potentialCoverage` series, never mixed with current operational coverage. An executed duty alone earns an adopted-coverage record, not an operational one.

| Selector | Numerator / denominator | Required evidence; comparison limits |
|---|---|---|
| Economic activity | Value added attributable to legally covered activities / value added of the chosen economy or sector | Same national-accounts vintage, geography, currency basis and period; legal-to-industry-to-firm crosswalk; covered activity fractions within firms. Prefer **GVA coverage** because GDP also contains net product taxes. Calling the ratio GDP coverage requires a consistent allocation of those taxes. National GDP share is an exposure envelope, not the fraction influenced by a policy. |
| Relevant spending | Payments for covered eligible AI procurements / all eligible AI-procurement payments | Actual paid ledger entries, same fiscal period, net of cancellations/refunds, identified vendor and duty. Separately show signed commitments and future ceilings. Spending is a buyer lever, not vendor dependence; compute the latter against the SAME vendor's corresponding total revenue. |
| Compute use | Covered delivered accelerator-hours / eligible delivered accelerator-hours | Metered use, same chip model and precision/workload regime. Separate allocations, installed stock, availability and consumed hours. Do not add CPU core-hours to GPU-hours. Across hardware, use separately reported strata or measured effective FLOPs under the same workload, not an assumed GPU conversion. |
| Enforceable subject population | Unique covered deployments, contracts or providers / eligible units of the same kind | Enumerated register, legal jurisdiction, exemptions and effective dates; identify the obligor and remedy. Provider count and deployment count answer different questions. Avoid claiming that covering one small firm and one frontier lab has equal safety value. |
| Supply-chain stage | Covered physical output or transactions at one defined stage / total eligible output or transactions at that stage | Matched products, period and unit; transaction-specific legal reach, including exemptions and licences. Report separate lithography, foundry, packaging and memory panels. Never sum their percentages: serial stages often serve the same final chips. |
| Adoption and compliance | Eligible units actually using the instrument, or independently passing a specified test / eligible or tested units | Distinguish census adoption from sampled compliance. Register opt-outs and missing responses; voluntary respondents alone cannot represent the target population. |

**Held leverage is a separate diagnostic**, not another name for coverage. Measure the fraction of the target's necessary input that an identified decision-maker can withhold on the relevant horizon, after subtracting verified replacement supply. A proposed formula is `L(H) = max(0, Q_withholdable(H) − Q_deliverable_substitute(H)) / Q_required(H)`, with `0 ≤ Q_withholdable ≤ Q_required`. Identify buyer, seller, existing contracts, stockpiles, compatibility, spare capacity, lead times and horizon H. Unverified substitution makes L unknown. Market share, a firm's headquarters and national investment exposure do not establish legal withholding authority. Supply-chain network modelling needs paths and capacities; a weighted sum of stage shares cannot identify this quantity.

### Agreement graph: power, signatures, alignment and handoffs

**Replace the earlier seven-equally-weighted-gates proposal.** Funding, staffing, instrumentation and go-live remain operational dependencies, not arbitrary equally weighted substitutes for required consent. Build a versioned directed acyclic graph for each concrete route variant, with an operational-delivery root. Unroll iterative negotiation/rework into dated states rather than introducing cycles. Leaves are exact approval acts, signatures, evidence deliveries or operational milestones. Every leaf names its decision holder, delegated signatory, instrument/version, approval scope, evidence requirement, date and predecessors.

- **AND:** every child is necessary. For fully known binary states, `s_AND = min(s_i)`.
- **OR:** any legally sufficient alternative is enough, `s_OR = max(s_i)`. Alternatives must be supported by an instrument or documented design choice, not invented to avoid an opposed actor. Selecting an alternative changes the route variant and may change its vertical coverage.
- **Threshold:** `s_T = 1{sum(v_i × approved_i) >= q}`, or `>` when the instrument requires strictly more than q. Record quorum, eligible electorate, class consents and reserved matters as additional conditions. For k-of-n rules, v_i = 1 only where each vote really has equal legal weight.
- **Veto:** a necessary approval leaf present in every legally sufficient path to delivery. Separately distinguish legal veto, exclusive operational dependency, and advisory influence. A minister, evaluator or summit supporter is not automatically a signatory; multiple signatures by one institution are not multiple independent actors.

Use three-valued states (satisfied / unsatisfied / unknown); evaluate the root twice by setting unresolved leaves to 0 and 1 to obtain logical bounds. These are possibility bounds, **not probabilities**. If an AND path has a known unfulfilled veto, label it blocked/pending regardless of high coverage elsewhere. An opposed actor on an unused OR branch does not block the selected alternative. Approval at time t can expire or be conditional; verify scope and validity at the delivery date.

**Count what the user needs to negotiate:** report distinct necessary decision holders, distinct required approval acts, agreements needing execution, outstanding signatory slots, cross-owner handoffs, unresolved authority assignments and shortest/longest supported critical paths. These are separate counts, not a summed difficulty score. A signatory slot means actor × instrument/version × required legal capacity; shared slots across documents or branches require deduplication according to the actual instrument. An approval decision may require multiple signatures; show both units without switching between them. For an incomplete graph, report “at least N identified,” never N as a complete denominator.

**Observable agreement fraction:** for a *complete* required approval set A of a declared route variant, `R_A(t) = sum(a_j(t)) / |A|`, where a_j=1 only for evidenced, valid, scope-matched approval of the exact instrument; otherwise retain confirmed-pending, opposed and unknown states. No percentage until completeness is supported and |A|>0. With U unverified approval states, publish the lower/upper **evidence bounds** `[S/N, (S+U)/N]`, not a midpoint; confirmed pending/opposed acts do not enter U. The lower bound may be titled “documented approvals,” but not treated as the true approval fraction. A separate signature mode uses a complete signatory-slot register. Never count advisory supporters, press endorsements or recurring technical tasks as approvals. A complete set of approvals does not establish operational delivery.

For OR/threshold choices, no single mandatory set may exist. Keep the graph truth evaluation and show the explicitly selected sufficient set. If comparing alternative sufficient sets, display each set's fraction and its paired impact population; report the min/max over **enumerated, source-supported alternatives** only as a scenario range. Do not select the easiest denominator after observing approvals or average branch fractions. Party count by itself is not ease: one unwilling indispensable holder can dominate many willing signatories.

**Legally weighted support:** when the instrument supplies weights and the eligible roster is complete, `V_approved = sum(v_i × approved_i) / sum(v_i)`. Show the exact threshold alongside it and evaluate any mandatory individual/class consent separately. For an exact, documented commitment to support the proposal, `V_committed` uses that distinct status; it is not an executed vote or signature. Financing amount is a voting weight only when the contract makes it one. Do not use GDP, prestige, diplomatic closeness or arranger names as inferred political weights. Reaching 1 means all eligible legal weight is evidenced as approving/committed, not that delivery is certain.

**Who is aligned:** for each relevant decision holder and collaborating leader, separately record `exact-instrument-approved`, `exact-instrument-support`, `conditional-support`, `general-goal-support`, `opposed`, or `unknown`; preserve conditions, dissent, source/date, expiry and whether the speaker can bind the institution. These labels have **no 0–1 encoding**. “General AI safety cooperation” is not assent to a named reporting clause, public disclosure or release veto. Approval is an act with instrument evidence; alignment is contextual evidence about a holder. A leader can express support while a required budget authority remains uncommitted. Report counts of documented statuses within the complete holder roster, but do not turn these into assumed actor probabilities.

**Handoffs and timing:** each edge records sender, empowered receiver, deliverable, acceptance evidence, earliest release and latest needed date. A handoff-completion fraction can be displayed only against a complete, fixed set of required handoffs; it is not approval coverage. For task duration d_i, release r_i and predecessor set P_i, `finish_i = max(r_i, max(finish_p for p in P_i)) + d_i` for AND prerequisites. OR prerequisites use the earliest legally sufficient branch; a threshold prerequisite is ready at the first time its received valid votes meet its exact rule. This computes an earliest completion under the stated resource assumptions, **not a predicted date**. Resource sharing must add scheduling constraints; parallel tasks cannot silently reuse the same full-time reviewer. Slack is `target_date − finish_root`, in days, not a fabricated 0–1 “time feasibility” score. Unknown durations mean unknown critical-path timing. Contractual maximums are not expected durations; documented past task durations support scenarios, not certainty.

**Evidence-based sensitivity:** vary only source-supported consent interpretations, eligible weights, approval expiry, alternative pathways and duration bounds; preserve dependencies by propagating each internally consistent scenario through the whole graph. Show which veto, handoff or date changes root satisfaction or misses the deadline. If unverified weights or a missing required-party list prevent bounds, show unknown instead of [0,1] dressed up as research. No independent Bernoulli actor assumptions or products of guessed cooperation probabilities.

### What can go live now, and the staged route to 0–1 axes

1. **Now:** publish the four existing empirical **proxy** fractions in clearly labelled contextual panels; graph skeletons with “incomplete” badges; sourced actor roles and unresolved alignment; counts only as lower bounds where incomplete; target dates with evidence status. Keep all eight agreement-coverage points and delivery forecasts unavailable. These documents are a delivery specification, not a claim that the UI has been changed.
2. **After inventory verification:** a complete approval/signature register enables actual S/N ratios and evidenced bounds. Show the fraction, raw S and N, graph completeness and veto blockers together. Finite N gives discrete ratios; a 0–1 axis does not make them continuously observed probabilities. Use no jitter or interpolation to pretend otherwise. Preserve the total number of parties and handoffs beside the ratio; 100% of two and 100% of twenty describe different processes.
3. **After quantity and authority reconciliation:** eligible voting/commitment amounts can provide finer-grained weighted fractions where actual legal weights exist. On the vertical axis, matched actual spending, GVA, output or delivered compute can provide similarly fine-grained coverage. Multiple model-free observables are more defensible than manufacturing one universal smooth score. Legal weights may still be finite and lumpy.
4. **After outcome collection:** a calibrated time-to-delivery model may produce genuinely probabilistic 0–1 forecasts using necessary-holder counts, handoffs, instrument-specific support, veto structure and durations as candidate predictors. Validate the whole-route outcome model on held-out routes and periods; learn dependencies rather than multiplying assumed independent actors. Until then, neither extra parties nor supportive leaders receive a numerical feasibility bonus/penalty.

No new empirical agreement fraction is supplied: none of the reviewed regional records provides a complete, exact-instrument approval register. The existing four fractions remain reproducible context, not illustrative invented negotiations.

**Optional forecast panel:** `P(operational safeguard by 2027-06-21 | information available on 2026-10-31)`. Operational means an in-force duty, enrolled subjects, working evidence pipeline and usable remedy, not an announcement or tender close. Register a cohort of comparable policy/contract processes at the same starting stage, entry dates, success dates, failures, withdrawals and right-censoring. Establish reference-class eligibility before observing outcomes. Estimate time-to-operation with censoring and competing failure outcomes; report sample size, uncertainty, horizon, out-of-time calibration and Brier score. Do not use allocated-project counts or adoption among volunteers as this base rate. There is no suitable cohort in the retrieved material: **forecast = null**, not 0.5, and readiness is not a conversion to probability.

**Endpoints and uncertainty:** Coverage 0 means a complete eligible population has no covered units; 1 means all its measured units are covered, not all AI risk removed. Agreement readiness 1 means every required approval in the declared complete set is evidenced and valid; it does not guarantee operational delivery or safety. A true readiness 0 requires verified nonapproval throughout; zero documented approvals with unknown states is only a lower bound. Probabilities 0/1 are mathematical endpoints but should not be inferred from finite all-failure/all-success cohorts. Empty denominators are null. For an eligible census with unresolved units, bound coverage using observed covered weight and unresolved weight. If the denominator itself is incomplete, mark the estimate non-identifiable or supply a justified denominator range; do not silently remove missing firms. Report rounding, staleness and classification uncertainty separately from statistical sampling uncertainty. Preserve negative or inconclusive safety effects in their own effect plot; forcing safety effects into [0,1] would conceal harm.

**Coalition accounting:** union stable subject/transaction IDs before adding measured weights. EU plus member-state GDP double-counts the member; add member economies once, and never mix PPP and nominal amounts. At the same stage, subtract overlapping jurisdictional coverage. Across stages, compute the union of traceable final units, not a sum of percentages. The same accelerator-hour cannot be counted separately under procurement, subsidy and loan conditions. Distinct allocation quotas may be added only if their disjointness is documented; a quota union does not imply that its decision-makers have agreed to act together. Joint delivery probabilities cannot be multiplied without a supported dependence model.

## 3. How should validation by Geneva and an independent audit work?

### Takeaway

Select routes against an explicit mechanism and falsifiable test, then report the **highest validation level actually reached**: adopted → instrumented → behavioural outcome → causal safety effect. A summit-ready contract or successful drill is not proof of reduced catastrophic risk. The following dates, sample sizes and thresholds are **proposals**, not existing institutional commitments or inferred scientific facts.

### Cited Findings

The local route inventory separates actor, obligor, instrument, safeguard, verification, remedy and gaps; these are the units to audit rather than a generic country score. Several proposed vendor obligations, consent and operational-evidence paths remain unresolved in that inventory. Source: [`data/routes.json`](../../data/routes.json), especially EuroHPC, Japan procurement and Singapore leakage-gate route records. No retrieved source supplies a causal evaluation of these proposed routes or a calibrated delivery base rate.

### Inferences — proposed validation protocol

**Planning sequence:** by 15 December 2026 freeze candidate route definitions, comparison populations and baseline measurement plans; by 31 January 2027 seek an authorized route owner and adoption evidence; by 31 March verify instrumentation; during April–May collect controlled behavioural evidence; lock data on 7 June; independently reproduce results by 14 June for reporting against the assignment's 21–22 June target. These are evaluation checkpoints, not asserted legal windows. If a real decision window occurs later, report the achievable lower validation level and preserve failures in the register.

| Level | Evidence sufficient for this level | Failure / falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Adopted | Executed instrument or effective rule; named obligor; in-scope safeguard; decision rights and remedy | Draft or announcement only; no power over relevant subjects; material exemption swallows the target population |
| Instrumented | Reconciled subject register; timestamps and logs; owner and reviewer; tested evidence transfer and escalation | Cannot enumerate subjects, collect needed logs, identify missed reports or execute remedy |
| Behavioural outcome | Prespecified comparison shows changed reporting, deployment or leakage performance in the target setting | No detectable relevant improvement, increased harm, selective reporting, or displacement outside coverage |
| Causal safety effect | Identified intervention effect on a specified real safety endpoint, credible counterfactual and uncertainty | Only paperwork or benchmark change; confounding or spillovers invalidate attribution; proxy-to-real-harm link unsupported |

**Test A: incident-reporting and response route.** Population: participating operator teams and prespecified incident classes within covered compute environments. Baseline: current workflow. Proposed design: within each of at least 20 participating teams, randomize the order of one baseline and one new-protocol simulated exercise, using equivalent but different incidents; 40 team-exercises minimum, team is the cluster. Record recognition time, notification completeness, evidence preservation, escalation and time to mitigation. Failure: missed seeded incident, report outside the instrument's deadline, missing required evidence or inability to invoke the agreed remedy. Proposed success rule: no lost critical evidence and an interval for the paired reporting-time improvement excluding no improvement, with no increase in missed incidents. Twenty teams is an operational pilot target, **not a justified powered confirmatory trial**; estimate variability and cluster dependence before choosing a confirmatory sample. Blind graders to protocol condition where possible; account for order and learning effects. A positive drill result reaches instrumented/behavioural simulation evidence only, not real-incident causal safety impact.

**Test B: deployment leakage gate.** Population: the specified agent/model versions and data-access configurations eligible for the Singapore-type deployment gate. Freeze an externally held-out attack set and target data before testing. Use **300 distinct attack scenarios**, each run on the baseline and gated configuration (600 runs minimum); keep model, tasks and permissions otherwise matched. Cluster analysis by attack family and model; record repeated seeds separately. Failure: any unauthorized disclosure of a planted secret to an unauthorized sink, including via tool calls. Independently adjudicate ambiguous outputs; treat missing logs as unresolved failures rather than clean successes. Proposed pass rule: a one-sided 95% upper bound on the gated failure rate below 1%, plus a paired improvement over baseline and no material task-utility regression. Under the very strong independent-identically-distributed Bernoulli assumption, zero failures in 300 yields `1 − 0.05^(1/300) = 0.009936`; this algebra explains the chosen pilot scale, **not an observed result**. Correlated or adversarially selected attacks defeat that population interpretation; use clustered intervals and expand independent families rather than assert the bound. A zero-failure benchmark never proves zero real-world leakage or catastrophic-risk reduction.

**Test C: procurement compliance and adoption.** Population: all eligible contracts in a fixed pilot procurement cycle, enumerated before invitations. Record `n_eligible`, `n_invited`, `n_signed`, `n_operational`, `n_tested`, attrition and reasons; do not invent these counts now. If phased rollout and consent permit, randomize agencies/contracts to immediate versus delayed introduction, preventing supplier spillover where possible. Baseline: existing requirements and compliance. Primary behavioural endpoint: fraction of independently tested deployments for which complete evidence reaches the reviewer by the required deadline. Failures include unverifiable model identity, missing evaluation access, false incident reporting or unenforceable remedy. Select sample size after measuring baseline, desired minimum effect and clustering; do not claim power from a generic contract count. If randomization is impossible, preregister an eligible matched comparison and assess pre-trends; clearly qualify causal claims. New terms with no changed evidence flow reach adoption only.

**Minimum machine-readable data beyond the companion examples:**

- Identity: metric ID/version; route IDs; actor, obligor, instrument ID/version, safeguard, remedy, target date; metric kind (`measured-coverage`, `proxy`, `readiness`, `forecast`) and validation level.
- Population: stable unit IDs; eligibility/exemption rule; geography; unit of analysis and weight; denominator definition; overlap IDs for coalition unions; census versus sampling frame.
- Values: numerator and denominator with value, unit, population, period and source IDs; formula; `value01` nullable; current versus potential coverage; lower/upper bounds with method and assumptions; unobserved weight; raw precision and display rounding.
- Evidence: exact source URL, page/table/row locator, publisher, published date, event or reporting-period dates, retrieval date, access status; derived versus independently observed fields; exact approval and signature evidence, validity and conditions.
- Agreement graph: versioned actor, node and handoff IDs; declared route variant and sufficient set; completeness attestation and unresolved authority questions; AND/OR/threshold rules; eligible legal weights, quorum, exceptions and class consents; distinct party/approval/agreement/signatory/handoff counts; alignment scope, speaker authority and source; duration bounds and critical-path assumptions.
- Validation: protocol version, baseline/control, allocation method, sample and cluster counts, prespecified endpoint and failure definition, proposed threshold, actual outcome, adverse results and missingness; analysis code and provenance of any exclusions.
- Forecast only: training reference class, eligibility and censoring rules, outcome dates, model version, prediction timestamp/horizon, validation cohort, calibration and interval. No suitable training data means null.

**Independent measurement auditor's stress test:**

1. **Denominator shopping:** choosing one cooperative ministry can produce 100% coverage. Freeze the intended population, show its size and wider-market relevance; recalculate with excluded units. A missing independent inventory falsifies a population-wide claim.
2. **Quota laundering:** displaying allocated, offered or purchased hours as delivered safeguarded use inflates reach. Reconcile call → award → contract → meter → tested safeguard; unmatched hours are not operational coverage.
3. **Task splitting and selective pathways:** multiplying easy approvals or choosing an already-agreed branch raises the score. Freeze the exact-instrument approval register and counting convention, publish sufficient-set choices and veto blockers, and rerun using the original denominator and paired impact population.
4. **Double counting:** the same GPU, vendor or final chip reappears across jurisdictions or supply-chain stages. Require union IDs or validated bounds; inconsistent inventories invalidate the coalition total.
5. **Legal reach by logo:** headquarters, market share or a public announcement is displayed as government control. Demand the operative clause, effective date, obligor, exceptions and remedy. An exemption or lack of authority can falsify the route even with a high market share.
6. **Benchmark rehearsal:** vendors train to the visible test. Use independent held-out scenarios, version locking, preregistered exclusions and adversarial retesting; leakage or test-set exposure invalidates the test result.
7. **Reporting paradox:** better reporting raises counted incidents; fewer reports can mean concealment. Use seeded incidents, detection denominators, independent audits and severity, not raw incident count alone.
8. **Selection and survivor bias:** successful pilots remain while failed negotiations disappear. Register all route attempts, refusals, delays and withdrawals before outcomes; publish attrition.
9. **Nominal success with displacement:** safeguarded workloads migrate to uncovered facilities or less transparent vendors. Track migration and substitute use; a net increase in risky uncovered activity can falsify the proposed mechanism.
10. **Expired evidence:** a 2022 allocation scheme or 2025 call is given a 2026 badge. Display observation, publication and resource period separately; do not carry proxies forward without a new source.

### Gaps

No route has been evaluated with these protocols in this work. No sample outcomes or independent adopter commitments are invented. The next discriminating measurement question is: **for one selected route, can its owner provide an auditable, dated inventory linking each eligible subject to an effective obligation, evidence channel and remedy, with a complete denominator?** Without that mapping, additional macroeconomic or market-share data will improve context but will not identify safeguard reach or delivery feasibility.
