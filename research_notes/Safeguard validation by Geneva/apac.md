## 1. Can Japan's Gen-nai vendor terms actually change before Geneva?

### Takeaway

**Prioritise a conditional FY2027 procurement refinement, not a proposal to introduce safety evidence from scratch.** The retrieved call already asks for safety evidence. A separate paid procurement is planned, with a useful November 2026–April 2027 sequence; the final tender and power to incorporate new terms still need verification. All observations are dated **31 October 2026**. The parent is checking the assignment's June 21–22, 2027 Geneva date; this note uses a proposed June 14 evidence freeze, not an independently verified summit date.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences

**Scenario JP — versioned acceptance and lifecycle evidence.** Proposed actor: Digital Agency purchaser/platform operator, with ministry system owners and consenting suppliers. Proposed lever: a lawful new tender that prices model-version identification, deployed-system tests, material-change review, incident evidence preservation/reporting and proportionate cure/suspension. AISI can advise if engaged; no AISI veto is established. The marginal claim is that the **deployed version remains tied to actionable evidence**, not that government first starts asking about safety.

The published planned evaluation uses **300 independent text questions** across 35 capability categories and eight themes, with a 15-hour maximum. A stateful, multi-tool leakage test could reveal failures this format misses; whether vendors already perform equivalent tests remains unknown. This is a testable marginal hypothesis, not proof the baseline is inadequate. — [Annex 2, section 3](https://www.digital.go.jp/assets/contents/node/information/field_ref_resources/207cadc2-c218-42f6-92ec-c0eda476b49c/05201362/20260529_news_genai_outline_02.pdf)

**Purchaser stress test — assumption:** a delayed April rollout, fewer eligible domestic models or costly retesting could undermine the documented adoption/demand objectives. **Vendor stress test — assumption:** vague tests and unrestricted public disclosure can impose unpredictable costs or expose IP. Proposed response: bounded published tests, confidential detailed evidence, priced retests and a tested fallback. Multiple candidates and users' competing AI use do not prove cheap substitution. Anchor: [policy objective and government-reported trial participation](https://www.digital.go.jp/news/207cadc2-c218-42f6-92ec-c0eda476b49c), [survey](https://www.digital.go.jp/assets/contents/node/basic_page/field_ref_resources/f9ed1b1c-b22f-4466-8e50-c1c9adf9f8eb/7908367c/20261030_policies_genai_outline_01.pdf).

**Selection gate, proposed November 30:** retrieve the actual new call and tender owner; establish a lawful way to include a bounded clause before bids. The May plan is insufficient to declare current negotiability. If terms cannot be added, downgrade to voluntary testing rather than claim procurement adoption.

**Proposed April–May pilot:** compare selection-only baseline with versioned acceptance/containment on matched, held-out agent tasks using synthetic secrets. Blind human adjudication; separate correctness from leakage; record all attempts, delay, cost and fallback migration. Include a deliberately failing candidate version and a model-change/incident drill. A gate that only generates a report but never changes release or permissions has not demonstrated the proposed mechanism.

**By the June evidence freeze, conditionally validate:** adopted/signed clause, version traceability, an actual withheld or remediated shadow deployment, timeliness of a drill and bounded test improvement. **Cannot validate:** global release control, catastrophic-risk reduction, all intended users reached, or full-year performance. **Stop:** no authorised terms/supplier agreement; baseline already equivalent; apparent improvement explained by task non-completion or migration.

**Measurement design, not results:** report signed-and-evidenced deployed versions / all eligible versions, and affected transactions / all eligible transactions. Measure leak-free correctly completed tasks / all attempted tasks in each arm, with raw counts, uncertainty and separate leakage/correctness rates. Difference between arms may be negative; do not clip it into a flattering 0–1 score. The bounded rates are 0–1; their causal difference is not.

## 2. Does Singapore have gate authority, and is there a better near-term candidate?

### Takeaway

**A government-wide AISI pass/fail gate remains unverified. Select a narrower purchaser-backed sandbox pilot only if an actual release owner signs on.** The rolling sandbox supplies an empirically used three-month testing channel, not regulatory authority or guaranteed admission.

### Cited Findings

- On **October 6, 2026**, MDDI described risk-calibrated public-service controls over agent data access and actions. It explicitly said frontier-model access is **not mandatory**, preferring collaboration; stronger high-risk requirements remain under study. This establishes a current political stance but not the proposed AISI pass/fail instruction. — [Parliamentary answer](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/mddi-response-to-pq-on-providing-pre-deployment-government-access-to-frontier-ai-models-for-independent-evaluation-of-national-security-risks-and-vulnerab/)
- The **Global AI Assurance Sandbox** describes ongoing participation, production/intended-production applications, commercial choice of tester, some funding and completion of testing plus a case study **within three months**. Its observed footprint is **30 applications, 12 geographies and 14 industries**. Admission, funding and any 2027 slot are not guaranteed. — [Programme and journey](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/)
- Participant-authored **Earlybird/Knovel** material describes accounting-agent testing with manipulated documents, malicious instructions and memory-influenced workflows. It reports **approximately 100 documents**, **3 days** spent by Earlybird and **25 days** contributed by Knovel. This is unusually useful effort evidence, but neither currency cost nor staff-day/elapsed-day interpretation is specified. Containment is reported qualitatively without a full attack-success denominator or causal comparison. These are not predicted costs/results for our pilot. — [Participant case study, sections 04–05](https://go.gov.sg/sandbox-earlybirdxknovel); [official programme listing](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/case-studies/)
- SG/KR AISI results explicitly report score differences between independently implemented pipelines despite attempts to align environments. Method sharing therefore cannot be equated with reproducibility, certification or purchaser recognition. — [Joint evaluation, results p. 12](https://sgaisi.sg/wp-api/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/SG_KR_AISI_Agent_Evaluation_Data_Leakage_2026.pdf)
- **Observed benchmark fractions, not treatment effects:** Table IV's KR pipeline on the SG scenario suite records fully correct-and-safe runs of **9/60 = 0.15** for anonymised agent A and **21/60 = 0.35** for B. Denominator: test runs of that agent/suite, not applications, users or national supply. The anonymity and pipeline variation prevent treating these as a purchaser-specific baseline or as evidence the proposed gate improved safety. — [Joint evaluation, Table IV, p. 12](https://sgaisi.sg/wp-api/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/SG_KR_AISI_Agent_Evaluation_Data_Leakage_2026.pdf)

### Inferences

**New scenario SG — purchaser-backed release condition in a rolling sandbox.** Actor: one named deploying organisation's authorised system owner/procurement signatory, its supplier and a commercially chosen tester. IMDA/AIVF decide programme participation; the purchaser must decide whether a release or sensitive permission proceeds. Proposed safeguard: hold back external sending or sensitive access until leakage/prompt-injection tests pass; remediate/retest and revisit after material changes. A private purchaser is viable if no government signatory is found; do not relabel that outcome a government mandate.

**Political/economic inference:** a bounded application test is more compatible with the documented collaborative stance and commercial programme than demanding universal frontier access. This is not institutional endorsement. **Purchaser assumption:** a report without release consequences is poor value. **Vendor/tester assumption:** bespoke thresholds and public case studies create cost, confidentiality and liability concerns. Proposed response: signed release responsibility, bounded tests, disclosed limitations, compatible publication agreement and priced remediation. Alternative tester, simpler permissions or human workflow are possible substitutions to cost and test, not assumed equivalents. Anchors: [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/mddi-response-to-pq-on-providing-pre-deployment-government-access-to-frontier-ai-models-for-independent-evaluation-of-national-security-risks-and-vulnerab/), [sandbox commercial process](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/).

**Proposed schedule:** by January 31, identify owner/delegation, vendor/tester, inventory and consent; February 1–April 30 test; May independent retest; June 14 evidence freeze. These are project choices fitting a three-month programme design, not official deadlines or a reservation.

**Counterfactual/pilot:** freeze the current release process and evaluate matched held-out tasks before and after permission containment plus a genuine release condition. Use synthetic data, unseen malicious documents and memory-reset/persistent-memory runs. Add human validation to automated judges. Optional KR/JP replication tests portability only; a shared acceptance regime would require separate authorities' agreement.

**Measurable 0–1 outputs:** gated apps / all eligible purchaser apps; withheld-or-remediated failures / all failing versions; leak-free correctly completed tasks / all attempted tasks; identical cross-lab decisions / all paired cases. Record raw counts and undefined denominators. **No measured value yet.** Thirty programme apps is neither a numerator for successful safeguards nor a denominator for all Singapore apps; national or economic coverage is unknown.

**By Geneva, conditionally validate:** a named gate owner, an actual changed deployment/permission decision, a reproducible bounded improvement and measured cost. **Cannot validate:** mandatory nationwide authority, frontier-model access, regional recognition or rare catastrophic outcomes. **Stop:** no empowered purchaser, incompatible publication/contract conditions, no participating supplier by the selection date, or testing never changes behaviour.

### Gaps

No adopting government-wide instruction, delegated signatory, authorised budget, live purchase, supplier agreement or executed gate was retrieved. Existing government controls should not be treated as absent; they are simply insufficiently specified to establish this particular mechanism. Company case studies are self-reported. The HTX/Knovel PDF fetch failed; Earlybird and Japan PDFs were recovered and checked with built-in macOS PDFKit after initial extraction failures. No national eligible-app inventory or spend denominator is available.

## 3. Are Korean notices/enforcement a real opening, and what continues through 2027?

### Takeaway

**Do not put a confirmed January 2027 Korean drafting opportunity on the timeline.** The actual retrieved consultation closed February 26, 2026. Keep a conditional safety-case/incident rehearsal candidate, with legal-status and participation checks before selecting it.

### Cited Findings

- MSIT's commencement release states the Act/decree took effect **January 22, 2026**, promises **at least one year** of enforcement grace (with exceptional serious-harm investigations), and describes confidential/anonymous support and refinement during grace. Thus January 22, 2027 is an **earliest anniversary calculation**, not a verified end of grace or consultation deadline. The release explicitly emphasises industry promotion/minimum regulation. — [MSIT commencement release](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?bbsSeqNo=42&mId=4&nttSeqNo=1214&sCode=eng)
- The official **February 6, 2026** administrative-notice page links a consultation attachment whose section 3 closes comments on **February 26, 2026**. Both page and HWPX attachment were read; the latter was extracted directly from ZIP/XML in memory. — [Notice page](https://www.msit.go.kr/bbs/view.do?sCode=user&mPid=103&mId=109&nttSeqNo=3186337&bbsSeqNo=84); [consultation attachment](https://www.msit.go.kr/ssm/file/fileDown.do?atchFileNo=52459&fileOrd=7&fileBtn=A)
- **Draft, not verified current law:** articles 4–7 cover lifecycle risk identification, evaluation, mitigation and incident handling; article 8 proposes initial evidence within three months, material-change/new-risk updates within one month, incident notice within 24 hours, initial measures within seven days and results within 15 days. Article 3(4) requires business consent for expert verification of submitted compute data. Article 9 specifies three-year review from July 1, 2026, not annual 2027 revision. The cover contains a 2025/2026 numbering inconsistency; the promulgated text must be obtained. — [Draft attachment, arts. 3–9](https://www.msit.go.kr/ssm/file/fileDown.do?atchFileNo=52459&fileOrd=1&fileBtn=A)

### Inferences

**Conditional scenario KR — executable safety case and incident rehearsal.** Actor: a consenting developer's authorised safety/compliance owner, with MSIT as legal recipient only where current law applies; evaluator by agreement. Incremental safeguard: actually replay mitigation and exercise escalation/update procedures rather than possess a written policy. No newly invented statutory obligation is proposed from the draft.

**Purchaser/compliance-owner assumption:** evaluating the wrong legal version or an out-of-scope system wastes scarce effort. **Vendor assumption:** external access exposes secrets beyond its legal duty. Proposed response: verify operative instrument and applicability first; scoped confidential evidence and an internal simulated incident; separate voluntary testing from statutory submission. The ministry's grace/confidential-support stance and draft consent provision anchor these concerns; they do not prove any firm's willingness. — [MSIT](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?bbsSeqNo=42&mId=4&nttSeqNo=1214&sCode=eng); [draft](https://www.msit.go.kr/ssm/file/fileDown.do?atchFileNo=52459&fileOrd=1&fileBtn=A)

**Selection gate:** no legislative-window selection without an actual live revision. Before a proposed February–April rehearsal, retrieve final notice/amendments and current enforcement position; identify an in-scope obligor and consenting evidence recipient. If unavailable by the proposed January 31 cutoff, record “not selected—authority/window unknown,” not a promise of pre-Geneva validation.

**Counterfactual/test:** compare existing written policy with a rehearsed workflow on matched unseen synthetic incident/model-change events; track correctly routed, complete and timely evidence, plus independent reproduction of one mitigation. Never send a false official incident. Use draft deadlines only if verified against operative law. **0–1 measures:** complete timely drill responses / all drill events; evidenced in-scope versions / all developer in-scope versions. Numerators/denominators and legal population are presently unknown; do not insert zero or an LLM score.

**Possible pre-Geneva result:** documented route selection/rejection and, if legal/participant checks pass, bounded drill performance. This is neither real-world incident-risk reduction nor a national enforcement finding. **Stop:** final legal basis/applicability not established, no consenting obligor, or no measurable addition to existing readiness.

**July–December 2027 follow-through, proposed only:** quarterly model-change retests, incident exercises and matched-workflow monitoring under actual agreements; verify renewal/consultation instruments when published. These outcomes belong after Geneva. No additional official late-2027 APAC deadline was established. Do not create a synthetic deadline merely to fill a timeline.

**Across scenarios:** keep selection, signed adoption, operational implementation, changed behaviour and measured safety separate. Use adoption evidence as such; GDP/economic shares describe exposure, not regulatory reach. No aggregate 0–1 feasibility score is defensible from these sources. National procurement and voluntary sandbox pilots can be compared on measured owner/contract coverage and outcomes once like-for-like denominators exist, not on an assumed coordination bonus.

Failure to retrieve them is not proof they do not exist. The January release is historical policy evidence, not a current-law opinion. Australia was not expanded into because this bounded pass did not establish a clearly superior window. No contacts, spending, installations, agents, background jobs or git operations occurred.
