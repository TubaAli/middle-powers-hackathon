## Key Question 1 (Japan): Which concrete, dated, holder-level levers exist over AI safety via procurement, compute support and safety-institute/testing cooperation, and what is each one's legal status?

### Takeaway

Japan's only binding instruments as of 2026-10-31 are **contract and deployment rules it sets for itself**: the whole-of-government AI platform "Gen-nai" (about 180,000 officials) now carries a published safety/governance standard ("源内基準", 2026-10-20) drafted **with AISI Japan**, plus rolling procurements of domestic foundation models with an explicit AI-sovereignty rationale. The AI Act (in force, no penalties) and the annually updated AI Basic Plan are institutional channels, not restraints on labs; AISI Japan holds **no veto** — the Second AI Basic Plan (2026-07-14) only *commits to build mechanisms* that translate AISI evaluations into policy and operations, which is the exact hook a coalition proposal can use.

### Cited Findings

Legal instrument — AI Act (enacted, in force):
- Cabinet Office AI Act page: "令和7年6月4日にＡＩ法が公布・一部施行され、９月１日にはＡＩ戦略本部の設置に係る規定等も含め、全面施行されました" — promulgated and partially effective 2025-06-04; **fully in force since 2025-09-01**, including the AI Strategy Headquarters provisions. ([Cabinet Office, AI Act page, observed 2026-10-31](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_act/ai_act.html))
- Same page links the statute text (e-Gov: [laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/507AC0000000053](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/507AC0000000053), i.e. Act of 2025 No. 53 format ID) and an official provisional English overview PDF. The overview states: "Establishment: 28th May, 2025. Partial enforcement: 4th June, 2025. Full enforcement: 1st September 2025." Government basic measures include "Guidance, advice and announcement to business operators and citizens" and "Collecting information, investigating cases where human rights and interests are infringed"; business-operator duties are endeavour-based ("Business operators shall endeavor to cooperate…"). The act's stated ambition is "Aiming to be the friendliest country to develop and utilize AI." **No penalty provisions appear in the official overview; nothing in the fetched sources gives any ministry a lab veto.** ([AI Act overview, English translation, Cabinet Office PDF, observed 2026-10-31](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_hou_gaiyou_en.pdf))
- AI Strategy Headquarters (chair: the Prime Minister; members: all cabinet ministers) exists with a Cabinet Office page: [www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_hq/ai_hq.html](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_hq/ai_hq.html) (observed 2026-10-31).

Policy instrument — AI Basic Plan (cabinet decisions; annually updated):
- Phase I was decided in December 2025 (Digital Agency: "同年12月に「人工知能（AI）基本計画」が閣議決定されました"; plan file on the Cabinet Office page is dated 2025-12-23). ([Digital Agency Gen-nai page](https://www.digital.go.jp/policies/genai); [Cabinet Office AI Basic Plan page](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_plan/ai_plan.html), both observed 2026-10-31)
- Phase II ("Second AI Basic Plan", **Cabinet decision 2026-07-14**) commits to: "Establish and enhance AISI's technical evaluation capabilities for AI model assessment, traceability, guardrails, and crisis-time information sharing, **while creating mechanisms to translate evaluation results into policy, regulatory, and operational improvements**"; "Lead the development of AI governance through the AISI International Network and other international standardization initiatives"; "convening an AI Summit in Japan at an early opportunity"; "Foster international cooperation with ASEAN and Global South: Elevate AISI"; "Update this Basic Plan **annually** for the foreseeable future"; and, on compute, "Expand domestic production and supply capacity for AI infrastructure — data, community-friendly data centers, computing resources, and semiconductors" under an open "AI Sovereignty" concept (strategic autonomy + indispensability). It also commits to "proactively and continuously review and update legal, regulatory, and policy frameworks, **including the AI Act**," citing "research under Article 16 of the AI Act" (the information-collection/rights-infringement investigation basis per the act overview). ([Second AI Basic Plan English summary, Cabinet Office PDF, "Cabinet Decision on Jul.14.2026", observed 2026-10-31](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_plan/aiplan_g_eng_20260714.pdf))

Procurement / deployment instrument — Government AI "Gen-nai" (源内) and the Gen-nai Standard:
- Digital Agency operates the whole-of-government generative-AI environment "Gen-nai" (English name "GENAI" in the Phase II plan): "2026年度中には、全府省庁約18万人の政府職員が生成AIを利用可能とする予定" (all ~180,000 national officials in FY2026), with deployment schedule: Digital Agency internal use from 2025-05; trial for some ministries from 2026-01; large-scale rollout to ~180,000 officials from 2026-05; "本格利用" (full-scale use) from FY2027 funded by participating ministries' budgets. The page frames domestic-model procurement as safety- and sovereignty-serving: "国内で開発されたAIの調達・利用を通じ、安全・安心なAI活用推進、性能向上、安定需要の創出を実現し、日本のAI自律性確保を推進していきます" (via procuring and using domestically developed AI). ([Digital Agency, Government AI "Gen-nai" page, last updated 2026-10-30, observed 2026-10-31](https://www.digital.go.jp/policies/genai))
- Rolling domestic-LLM procurement with dates from the same page: public call for domestic LLMs for trial issued 2025-12-02; results published 2026-03-06; pre-announcement ("予告") of a further domestic foundation-model call 2026-05-29; trial of domestic foundation models on domestic cloud started 2026-07-10; "国会答弁作成支援AIアプリ" (Diet-answer drafting support app) provided from 2026-10-30. ([Digital Agency Gen-nai page](https://www.digital.go.jp/policies/genai))
- **Gen-nai Standard (源内基準), published 2026-10-20** — "ガバメントAI 源内 ガバナンス・セキュリティ・セーフティに関する基準": binding rules for government AI-agent use covering three roles — "利用者" (users), "管理者" (administrators, including each ministry's AI統括責任者 = CAIO, PMO, system administrators) and "開発者" (developers) — with agent environments "エージェントチャット" (from 2026-10-20) and "源内工房" (targeted by 2026-12), for ~180,000 officials, described by Digital Agency as a world-first at this scale; Digital Agency states it was "AIセーフティ・インスティテュート（AISI）の協力を得て策定しました" (drafted **with the cooperation of AISI**) and positions it as a model case ("モデルケース") for local governments and private businesses. Gen-nai OSS Ver.2.0 (usable/modifiable/redistributable under a commercial-use licence) is slated for release around February 2027. ([Digital Agency release, 2026-10-20](https://www.digital.go.jp/policies/genai/agent-safety-standard))
- Standard contents (official overview PDF): user duties include human-in-the-loop ("職員が確認していないAIの出力を、行政資料や公文書に直接転記しない"), hallucination verification, prompt-injection vigilance, a **risk-based approach with a high-risk determination sheet** and CAIO reporting for high-risk generative-AI use ("高リスクの可能性が高い場合は…CAIOへの報告等の必要な手続を実施"), **shadow-AI prohibition**, confidential-information handling, and token management; the standard requires a **foundation-model evaluation/selection system** ("基盤モデルの評価・選定体制の確立", "国内外の基盤モデルの適切な選択") and ties AI sovereignty to "自律性・代替性・耐遮断性" (autonomy, substitutability, resistance to blocking). The overview references the **"AI調達・利活用ガイドライン" (AI Procurement and Utilization Guideline)** and its high-risk determination sheet as the rule the standard implements, and cites "AI事業者ガイドライン第1.2版（2026.3.31 総務省・経産省）" for its AI-agent definition; its governance diagram states: "AI法を基礎に、基本計画・重点計画・調達利活用ガイドラインが連携し、政策から実務までをカバー" (AI Act as basis; basic plan, priority program and procurement guideline linked). It also quotes the Second AI Basic Plan (2026-07-14 Cabinet decision) and the Digital Priority Program (2026-07-21 Cabinet decision). ([Gen-nai Standard overview PDF, Digital Agency, 2026-10-20](https://www.digital.go.jp/assets/contents/node/basic_page/field_ref_resources/b033337d-dad1-4493-9316-d929597e4667/4c25aead/20261020_policies_genai_guideline_01.pdf); full standard: [guideline_02.pdf](https://www.digital.go.jp/assets/contents/node/basic_page/field_ref_resources/b033337d-dad1-4493-9316-d929597e4667/9bbaf559/20261020_policies_genai_guideline_02.pdf))

### Inferences

- Japan's enforceable lever is **demand-side**: the state is the single largest coordinated AI buyer in the country (180,000 users on one platform with one standard), so model-entry conditions into Gen-nai are the nearest thing Japan has to a pre-deployment gate. The standard's own text ("AI調達・利活用ガイドライン" linkage) means vendor-facing procurement conditions are the natural place to add evaluation requirements — no legislation needed.
- The Phase II plan's commitment to "translate evaluation results into policy, regulatory, and operational improvements" is an announced mechanism, not an operating one; treating AISI Japan as already having a veto would be wrong (the parent's warning stands).
- Japan's procurement preference for domestic models ("AI自律性") creates the incentive side for a compute/procurement safety gate: labs get stable government demand in exchange for eval access.

## Key Question 2 (South Korea): Which concrete, dated, holder-level levers exist over AI safety via procurement, compute support and safety-institute/testing cooperation, and what is each one's legal status?

### Takeaway

Korea is the only APAC middle power with a **binding cross-cutting AI statute in force** (AI Basic Act, in force 2026-01-22, with an enforcement grace period of at least one year, so fact-finding and fines begin around **January 2027**), an Enforcement Decree already in force, and a statutory AI Safety Institute (Act Art. 12). Its compute lever is a National AI Computing Center being contracted **right now** through a single-bidder public-private SPC (15,000+ GPUs by 2028) plus ~50,000 public-sector GPUs under the NVIDIA partnership — and its "Sovereign AI Foundation Model" project already uses staged evaluations as funding gates. But nothing fetched shows safety evaluation attached to compute access yet: the attachment point is open, not yet used.

### Cited Findings

Legal instrument — AI Basic Act (enacted 2025-01-21; in force 2026-01-22; enforcement deferred):
- Official English title per MSIT: "**Basic Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and the Establishment of Foundation for Trustworthiness** ('AI Basic Act')"; passed the National Assembly plenary **2024-12-26** (Science ICT Committee 2024-11-26; Legislation & Judiciary Committee 2024-12-17; 19 bills merged); "second country in the world" after the EU; "The new law to take effect in January 2026, a year after the Cabinet vote and promulgation." ## Key Question 3 (cross-cutting): Emerging patterns across Japan and Korea, and what Asia-Pacific actors can concretely do — two joint actions, clearly marked as proposals

### Takeaway

Pattern: both countries legislate lightly and subsidize compute heavily, while their real enforcement capacity is being built in **contracts and deployment standards rather than statutes** — Japan already hard-wired its safety institute into a government-wide AI standard (Gen-nai), while Korea's statute gains teeth only in ~January 2027 and its compute terms are being set in 2026. The two AISIs began direct technical cooperation two weeks before this note (2026-10-08/09) on exactly the missing piece — multilingual, multicultural evaluation. The two highest-value joint actions are therefore: (1) a compute-access safety gate with mutual recognition of AISI evaluations attached to subsidized compute and sovereign-model programmes; (2) a shared APAC public-procurement safety profile ("evaluated once, accepted across").

### Cited Findings (patterns)

- Statutes diverge on teeth: Japan's AI Act is non-penal with endeavour-based operator duties ([AI Act overview](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_hou_gaiyou_en.pdf)); Korea's Act creates high-risk obligations with deferred fines ([release 1215](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1215), [release 1191](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1191)).
- Both pair compute buildouts with safety institutes and "sovereignty" framing: Japan — Gen-nai domestic-model procurement + Phase II "AI Sovereignty" + AISI-evaluation-to-policy commitment ([Digital Agency](https://www.digital.go.jp/policies/genai), [Phase II plan](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_plan/aiplan_g_eng_20260714.pdf)); Korea — AI Highway / National AI Computing Center + Sovereign AI Foundation Model project + Art. 12 institute ([release 1179](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1179), [release 1212](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1212), [release 1058](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1058)).
- Institute-to-institute testing cooperation just became concrete and bilateral: first Japan–Korea AISI joint technical workshop 2026-10-08/09 (30 researchers; multilingual/multicultural evaluation methods; JAI-Trust benchmarks; multimodal red-teaming; "lay the foundation for ongoing information sharing and collaboration") ([AISI Japan workshop article](https://aisi.go.jp/activity/activity_international/261016/)); both are members of the International Network of AI Safety Institutes (launched San Francisco 2024-11-20; 10 participants incl. Korea, US, Japan, UK, EU) ([MSIT release 1057](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1057)).
- Procurement-as-safety is already half-built: Japan's Gen-nai Standard (2026-10-20) is a published, AISI-co-drafted, role-based safety standard explicitly offered as a model for local governments and the private sector ([Digital Agency release](https://www.digital.go.jp/policies/genai/agent-safety-standard)); Korea's Act supports voluntary private verification/certification (Art. 30) and the state's own demand-side instruments (computing center, sovereign models) are mid-formation.
- Regional ambition is official: Japan's Phase II plan commits to "Foster international cooperation with ASEAN and Global South: Elevate AISI and convene an AI Summit in Japan" ([Phase II plan](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_plan/aiplan_g_eng_20260714.pdf)); Korea frames itself as "the AI hub of the Asia–Pacific region" and AI G3 ([release 1183](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1183), [release 1214](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1214)); Korea also chairs APEC PPSTI 2026–2027 (first Korean chair, elected 2025-11-04) ([MSIT release 1201](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1201)).

### PROPOSAL 1 — "Compute-for-safety reciprocity" between Japan and Korea (proposal, not an existing programme)

One-line ask: **no subsidized public compute without a pre-deployment safety evaluation, and each country's AISI evaluation is mutually recognised by the other.**
- Holders: Japan — Digital Agency (Gen-nai model procurement, FY2027 full-deployment contracts) and METI/NEDO (GENIAC-family subsidies — terms unverified, see Gap); Korea — MSIT + the National AI Computing Center SPC (access terms being finalized now) and MSIT/NIPA/TTA (Sovereign AI Foundation Model stages).
- Instrument: grant and access **contract conditions** (no new legislation required in Japan; in Korea, Art. 30/32 hooks plus the SPC implementation plan). Status of the underlying instruments: Japan Gen-nai calls rolling (next windows: further domestic-model calls after the 2026-05-29 pre-announcement; FY2027 budget contracts through Mar 2027); Korea SPC terms and Phase-2 evaluations during 2026.
- Safety demand: pre-release testing (DESIGN.md condition #1) — safety/security benchmarks of the JAI-Trust type plus Korea's red-teaming for models entering subsidized compute or government deployment, with a joint Japanese/Korean-language evaluation suite (both institutes already committed to multilingual evaluation methods at the 2026-10 workshop).
- Verifier: Japan AISI + Korea AI Safety Institute under a mutual-recognition arrangement; Remedy: staged suspension of compute access / grant clawback / contract termination — enforceable as contract terms.
- Incentives: developers receive scarce subsidized compute plus stable government demand (Japan's 180,000-user platform; Korea's center and sovereign-model market); governments obtain enforceable checks without new statutes; both close the Japanese/Korean-language testing gap that frontier labs under-serve.
- Risk-reduction mechanism: catches capability and harm failures in the two languages before wide deployment; builds shared eval infrastructure the institutes are already separately constructing; reciprocal recognition widens the evaluated model set without duplicated spending.
- Scores (DESIGN.md weights): safety 8.5, feasibility 8, clarity 9 → **weighted 8.35**. Feasibility rests on: institutes already cooperating (2026-10-08/09), contract-level instruments on both sides, open windows in 2026–2027.

### PROPOSAL 2 — APAC public-procurement safety profile ("evaluated once, accepted across") (proposal)

One-line ask: **governments in the bloc accept one another's AISI safety evaluations as fulfilling public-sector AI procurement requirements, using a common minimum clause set.**
- Holders: Japan's Digital Agency (Gen-nai Standard as the published template; the underlying AI Procurement & Utilization Guideline), Korea's MSIT (Art. 31/32 transparency/safety obligations and Art. 30 certification support) — extendable to Singapore and Australia (DESIGN.md's country set) through the AISI International Network; Korea's APEC PPSTI chairmanship (2026–2027) is a ready-made venue, and Japan's planned AI Summit is the second.
- Instrument: procurement standard/clause set + mutual-recognition MoU between institutes; in Korea's case the ~Jan 2027 end of the grace period makes safety documentation commercially necessary anyway, aligning private incentives with the profile.
- Safety demand: vendor disclosure of evaluation evidence, third-party (AISI-network) evaluation acceptance, incident reporting (DESIGN.md conditions #1/#2), and risk-tiered use restrictions mirroring Japan's high-risk determination sheet and Korea's Art. 31 labeling rules.
- Verifier: each country's AISI, cross-accepted; Remedy: existing procurement remedies (non-selection, suspension, damages) — the strongest non-statutory enforcement channel available to middle powers.
- Incentives: vendors get one evaluation accepted across multiple government markets (Japan's unified platform is the single largest coordinated demand pool in APAC; Korea adds its public-sector compute); governments de-risk public-sector AI adoption and set a regional default that neither can set alone; both leadership ambitions (Japan's AI Summit; Korea's AI G3/APAC-hub framing) gain a concrete deliverable.
- Risk-reduction mechanism: public procurement is a mass distribution channel for AI systems; contract remedies bite without legislation; a shared profile prevents a safety race-to-the-bottom between two governments competing for the same "AI G3" positioning.
- Scores: safety 7, feasibility 6.5, clarity 8 → **weighted 6.9** (higher coordination cost than Proposal 1: multiple agencies per country; no single contract hook).

### Inferences

- Proposal 1 outranks Proposal 2 on the team's criteria because its windows are concretely dated and already open (Korea's SPC implementation plan and Phase-2 gates in 2026; Japan's FY2027 contracts and rolling model calls), while Proposal 2's mechanism (procurement recognition) requires cross-agency coordination but scales better and directly serves DESIGN.md's six-conditions framing.
- Both proposals deliberately use instruments already in force or in formation; neither requires treating a safety institute as having a veto (they are verifiers), and neither treats closed consultations as open.

### Gaps

- Joint-proposal feasibility depends on unknowns listed under Key Questions 1–2: GENIAC terms (Japan), SPC contract status and content (Korea), decree-level powers of the Korea AI Safety Institute, and the substance of Korea's 2026 Act amendment.
- No fetched evidence shows either government currently conditioning compute access on safety evaluation; both proposals are therefore genuinely new demands, and the coalition-builder should display them as proposals attached to named windows, not as existing rights.
- Next discriminating research questions: (a) What do the GENIAC Pro grant agreements require of adopters (NEDO call documents)? (b) What does the National AI Computing Center SPC implementation plan say about access conditions (Korean-language MSIT releases / SPC disclosure, 2026)? (c) What powers does the Enforcement Decree give the Korea AI Safety Institute? (d) What changed in Act No. 21311 (effective 2026-07-21)?
