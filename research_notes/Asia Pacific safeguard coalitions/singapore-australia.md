## KQ1: What concrete leverage does Singapore actually hold, and does the Digital Infrastructure Act create usable obligations today?

### Takeaway
Singapore's binding instruments govern infrastructure resilience and sustainability, not AI safety. The Digital Infrastructure Act 2026 passed Parliament on 7 Oct 2026 but **has not commenced** (cl 1: the Minister appoints a commencement date by Gazette; none located as of 31 Oct 2026), so it creates **zero operative obligations today**, and its scope is cloud providers and data centres with **no AI-model provisions**. The state's live, concrete lever is discretionary allocation of scarce data-centre capacity (DC-CFA2: 200 MW, awards 2026), and its most institutionalised safety instrument is the testing ecosystem around the Singapore AI Safety Institute (IMDA + Digital Trust Centre).

### Cited Findings

**Digital Infrastructure Act (DIA) status and scope**
- Digital Infrastructure Bill 2026 (Bill 20/2026), as-enacted text: "This Act is the Digital Infrastructure Act 2026 and comes into operation on a date that the Minister appoints by notification in the Gazette" (cl 1). Published 8 Sep 2026; reader still titled "Digital Infrastructure Bill, as enacted" (updated 2–14 Oct 2026). Parliament **passed the Bill on 7 Oct 2026**. No Gazette commencement notification located as of 31 Oct 2026 → no operative obligations yet. — [Singapore Statutes Online, as-enacted text](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Bills-Supp/20-2026/Published/20260908?DocDate=20260908) (text read via [laws.sg mirror](https://laws.sg/legislation/digital-infrastructure-bill-2026)); passage date per [Drew & Napier client update, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.drewnapier.com/Publications/Upcoming-Digital-Infrastructure-Act). Origin: announced at COS 2024 by the inter-agency Taskforce on the Resilience and Security of Digital Infrastructure and Services — [MDDI newsroom](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/new-digital-infrastructure-act/).
- DIA **holder → instrument**: the Authority is IMDA (cl 2). Two licensing regimes: **major FDI licence** for major foundational digital infrastructure services — cloud IaaS/PaaS with ≥S$100m/yr average revenue from Singapore users (3-year lookback) and data-centre facility services carrying ≥10 MW critical IT load — and **DC licence** for data centres ≥3 MW critical IT load (government, public-authority, educational-institution and specified statutory-licensee DCs excluded, cl 3(5)). Codes of practice issued/approved by IMDA (ss 16(1), 37(1)). — Bill cl 2–3 (SSO, above); thresholds per [Drew & Napier, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.drewnapier.com/Publications/Upcoming-Digital-Infrastructure-Act).
- DIA **obligations and remedies** (as passed, not in force): FDI licensees must implement physical-security and cybersecurity measures, minimise unexpected interruptions, maintain business-continuity/disaster-recovery plans, and notify IMDA of prescribed cybersecurity incidents or service disruptions. DC licensees face facility- and equipment-level energy/water-efficiency duties, regular compliance reporting, a 7-day change-in-control notice, and licence conditions covering commencement of operations, **"agreed economic or strategic commitments"**, low-carbon energy projects, greenhouse-gas emissions, imported electricity and energy sources. Enforcement: financial penalties up to the higher of **S$1m or 10% of annual Singapore turnover**; licence suspension/revocation; IMDA may order DC licensees to **reduce critical IT load**; broad investigation powers (entry, inspection, records). — [Drew & Napier, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.drewnapier.com/Publications/Upcoming-Digital-Infrastructure-Act); penalties/licence concepts consistent with bill Parts 4, 6 (SSO/laws.sg contents).
- DIA purpose is **service resilience and DC sustainability, not model safety**: "seeks to improve the security and resilience of digital infrastructure services and the environmental sustainability of data centre operations" (D&N ¶2). No AI-system or AI-service category appears in cl 2 definitions. The 2024 consultation-era framing was also resilience/security (MDDI newsroom, above).

**Compute gatekeeping (live lever)**
- EDB + IMDA launched the **second Data Centre – Call for Application (DC-CFA2) on 1 Dec 2025**, making **at least 200 MW** of capacity available, "with potentially more through the adoption of new and innovative green energy pathways" (registration of interest closed 5 Dec [2025]). — [IMDA DC-CFA2 page](https://www.imda.gov.sg/proposal-submission/call-for-application-data-centre-2) (search-cache; page did not render on direct fetch).
- **Outcome**: EDB and IMDA "provisionally allocated 200 megawatts (MW) of new capacity to four data centre (DC) operators through the Second Data Centre – Call for Application (DC-CFA2) exercise" — Digital Realty, Equinix, Keppel Data Centres and ST Telemedia GDC, 50 MW each, on Jurong Island. — [EDB release](https://www.edb.gov.sg/en/news-and-insights/four-data-centre-proposals-selected-as-part-of-second-data-centre) (search-cache/footer only; page "last updated 30 Oct 2026"; award date not shown); operators and 50 MW each per [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/singapore-govt-allocates-200mw-of-power-for-four-data-center-developers-on-jurong-island/) and [Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/tech/four-operators-approved-to-expand-data-centres-200mw-of-new-capacity-allocated) (news locators).
- Context: Singapore is a regional DC hub with **>1.4 GW** capacity across 70+ facilities; the Green Data Centre Roadmap (launched 30 May 2024) targets **≥300 MW additional near-term capacity** tied to efficiency/green energy. — [IMDA Green DC Roadmap](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/how-we-can-help/green-dc-roadmap/green-dc-roadmap.pdf) and [launch factsheet, 30 May 2024](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/38/c49f426a-8053-4c7f-bbaa-8437b743a18f/files-Speeches%202024-1__Factsheet_IMDA_Green_DC_Roadmap.pdf) (context locators).

**Testing cooperation (the advanced-model-safety instrument)**
- The **Singapore AI Safety Institute** is the **Digital Trust Centre (DTC)** — hosted at NTU, funded by a **S$50m NRF+IMDA grant from 1 Oct 2022** — in partnership with **IMDA** (overall government agency: baseline policy, international engagement). Work areas: technical testing in (1) multilingual/multicultural evaluation and (2) agentic-AI testing; **societal impact studies**; ASEAN/Asia-Pacific capacity building via workshops and red-teaming challenges; participation in the International Scientific Exchange on AI Safety. — [sgaisi.sg/about-us](https://sgaisi.sg/about-us/) (fetched 31 Oct 2026).
- Joint/bilateral testing with other AISIs: multilingual model evaluations led with **Japan and the UK** under the network's joint testing effort (evaluation report Jun 2025); **3rd joint agentic-testing exercise** covering sensitive-information leak, fraud and cybersecurity threats (report Jul 2025); agentic data-leakage testing with **Korea AISI** (blogpost Jan 2026); LLM-judge consistency with **Canada AISI**; local-benchmark translation with Japan AISI; tools include **Moonshot** (AI Verify Foundation open-source evaluation toolkit). — [sgaisi.sg resources](https://sgaisi.sg/resources/) (fetched 31 Oct 2026; asset paths date outputs Feb 2025–Jan 2026).
- The network now operates as the **"international Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science (NAAIMES)"** (formerly styled "International Network of AI Safety Institutes" on the same site's earlier resource posts). — sgaisi (both URLs above).
- Private testing market: the AI Verify Foundation (IMDA-owned non-profit) launched an **AI Tester Accreditation Programme (AI TAP)**, reported as Asia's first scheme certifying external AI testers, launch by Q3 2026. — [EDB news](https://www.edb.gov.sg/en/news-and-insights/new-singapore-scheme-to-certify-firms-that-test-and-jailbreak-ai-systems) (search-cache).

**Lever chains (holder → instrument → actual discretion → window → verifier/remedy)**
- *Compute access*: EDB+IMDA → DC-CFA allocation rounds + (post-commencement) DC/FDI licences and IMDA codes of practice → discretion to choose winners and attach negotiated commitments (including strategic commitments as licence conditions) → windows: next CFA round (timing unknown) and DIA commencement + first codes (now–2027) → remedy: administrative (allocation/conditions) today; post-commencement penalties up to 10% turnover, licence suspension, critical-IT-load reduction. **No safety criteria observed in any CFA element** (efficiency, green energy, AI-compute readiness, economics).
- *Testing*: IMDA+DTC → AISI programmes, AIVF tools and tester accreditation → discretion to convene labs, set methodologies, accredit testers → window: live through 2025–26 → **verifier/remedy: none (voluntary, science-building)**.

### Inferences
- The DIA is the most enforceable compute-governance statute in the region, but as of 31 Oct 2026 it is passed-not-commenced; describing it as current law, or as AI-safety regulation, would be wrong on both counts. Its leverage is prospective: commencement and the first codes of practice are the insertion points.
- Singapore's genuine frontier-safety leverage is indirect but real: gatekeeping scarce capacity and licensing hyperscalers (≥S$100m cloud revenue) gives the state bargaining power that could lawfully be linked to safety-testing participation — nothing observed does so today.
- Testing cooperation is Singapore's strongest present contribution to advanced-model safety: methodologically advanced (multilingual, agentic), internationally networked (NAAIMES), with an emerging accredited private tester market (AI TAP) — but entirely unenforceable.

### Gaps
- DIA Gazette commencement date: not located (laws.sg still lists "Bill, as enacted"; D&N 15 Oct 2026 still calls it "upcoming").
- DC-CFA2 award announcement date, full scoring criteria, and whether winners' "strategic commitments" include AI-compute or safety elements: not verified (EDB/IMDA pages did not render).
- **Singapore government procurement: no public whole-of-government AI procurement policy located in this pass** (no dedicated search completed within budget; nothing surfaced in fetched pages). GovTech/IMDA internal procurement requirements: explicitly unknown.
- SG AISI designation announcement date; lab participation terms in joint exercises; DTC resourcing beyond the 1 Oct 2022 S$50m grant: unknown.
- Any pre-deployment testing requirement imposed on labs by Singapore anywhere: no evidence found.

## KQ2: What concrete leverage does Australia hold; do the DTA AI policy/procurement framework and 2025–26 announcements create usable obligations?

### Takeaway
Australia's only binding AI-specific instrument in force on 31 Oct 2026 is the whole-of-government **deployment** policy (Policy for the responsible use of AI in government, v2.1, mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities, with a public transparency register). The 2024 mandatory-guardrails proposal was dropped in the **National AI Plan (2 Dec 2025)**; the PM's **"AI in Australia's interests" package (15 Jul 2026)** announced **Australian Standards for AI** — legal obligations for **large data centres** (power, water, siting) plus AI copyright — with **legislation expected early 2027**, so nothing beyond the DTA policy is law yet. The **Australian AI Safety Institute** (announced 25 Nov 2025, operational 2026) is analytical/advisory; no pre-deployment testing mandate was verified.

### Cited Findings

**Binding deployment policy (in force)**
- "Policy for the responsible use of AI in government": **v1.1 effective 1 Sep 2024; v2.0 effective 15 Dec 2025; minor updates published 21 Oct 2026 (v2.1)**; "applies to all non-corporate Commonwealth entities, with some exceptions". — [digital.gov.au policy page](https://www.digital.gov.au/ai/ai-in-government-policy) (search-cache; direct fetch timed out 31 Oct 2026).
- What it obliges: mandatory requirements for departments/agencies on **accountable officials** and **transparency statements**; the DTA publishes a **central register of AI transparency statements** for all reporting Commonwealth entities, publicly available. — [DTA AI transparency statement page](https://www.dta.gov.au/ai-transparency-statement) and [DTA register announcement](https://www.dta.gov.au/articles/new-central-register-ai-transparency-statements-commonwealth-entities) (search-cache); v2.0 "sets requirements for the use and governance of AI and applies to all non-corporate Commonwealth entities" — [APS Academy](https://www.apsacademy.gov.au/learning-experiences/resources/policy-responsible-use-ai-government) (search-cache).
- Reported, **UNVERIFIED** (third-party blog; primary page not retrieved): AI use-case registers mandatory for ~94 Commonwealth entities from 15 Jun 2026; a second wave adding AI impact assessments and incident reporting due **Dec 2026**. — [trusenta.com.au](https://trusenta.com.au/blog/dta-ai-policy-mandatory-register-government-suppliers-australia-2026).

**From dropped guardrails to announced standards**
- Australia consulted in 2024 on **ten mandatory guardrails for high-risk AI** (proposals paper; general-purpose AI treated as high-risk; options from adapting existing laws to a new AI Act). The **National AI Plan** — released **2 Dec 2025** — indicated reliance on **existing technology-neutral laws**, voluntary guidance and the new AISI, rather than economy-wide mandatory guardrails. — Proposal: [aipolicytracker.org](https://aipolicytracker.org/policies/australia-mandatory-guardrails-proposal); plan page: [industry.gov.au/publications/national-ai-plan](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/national-ai-plan) (search-cache; plan exists, content corroborated by trackers [aipolicytracker](https://aipolicytracker.org/jurisdictions/australia) and [airiskaware](https://airiskaware.com/australia-ai-policy)); release date per [Piper Alderman](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/australia-now-has-a-national-ai-plan-now-what/).
- **15 Jul 2026 PM media release** (fetched in full): Government "will introduce a set of **Australian Standards for AI**, building on the **Data Centre Expectations**"; the standards "set out clear rules for **large data centres** — including a legal obligation to **underwrite their own new power supply**, pay their full share of connection costs, **reduce power when needed** to strengthen the grid, and be as **water efficient** as possible"; federal–state coordination on siting with local-community input; "**Effective today, the Office of AI will be established within the Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet**"; approach to be "considered by **National Cabinet in August**, with standards expected to be **legislated early next year** [2027]"; the framework will "deliver a simple, consistent regulatory framework for **large data centres and AI training** and be the first to be legislated by a government worldwide"; copyright: "no company should use Australian creative works to train AI without the artist's control"; whole-of-government **AI consumer-safety priorities** "in coming weeks", "building on the recent establishment of the AI Safety Institute". Quotes from PM Albanese; Sen. Tim Ayres (Minister for Industry and Innovation; Minister for Science); Dr Andrew Charlton (Assistant Minister for Science, Technology and the Digital Economy). — [pm.gov.au media release, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/ai-australias-interests).
- Office of AI: "established in the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet on **15 July 2026**". — [pmc.gov.au](https://www.pmc.gov.au/domestic-policy/office-ai) (search-cache).
- **Australian AI Safety Institute (AISI)**: announced **25 Nov 2025**, to become **operational in early 2026**; part of the **Department of Industry, Science and Resources**; "analyses emerging AI capabilities, risks, harms and trends"; announced as a **key action under the National AI Plan**; inaugural **General Manager Dr Kate Conroy** appointed **29 May 2026**. — [industry.gov.au AISI page](https://www.industry.gov.au/science-technology-and-innovation/technology/artificial-intelligence/ai-safety-institute) and [industry.gov.au news](https://www.industry.gov.au/news/australia-establish-new-institute-strengthen-ai-safety) (search-cache; news page timed out on fetch); announcement date per [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/australian-government-to-establish-ai-safety-institute); GM per [ABC News, 29 May 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-29/kate-conroy-leading-ai-safety-institute/106736306).
- Reported, **UNVERIFIED** (tracker, status "as at 19 Oct 2026"): National Cabinet endorsed mandatory data-centre standards **26 Aug 2026**; the federal **Joint Select Committee on AI** sent early advice to the PM **12 Oct 2026**, full report due **30 Nov 2026**. — [safeaiaus.org](https://safeaiaus.org/safety-standards/australian-standards-for-ai/).

**Lever chains**
- *Deployment/procurement*: DTA → whole-of-government policy (mandatory for NCCEs) + central transparency register → discretion to set/update requirements (updated twice in 15 months; v2.1 published 21 Oct 2026) → window: live; reported second wave Dec 2026 → verifier: DTA + public register → remedy: policy-compliance mechanisms only; **no statutory penalties verified**.
- *Standards track*: PM/Office of AI (PM&C, since 15 Jul 2026) → announced Australian Standards for AI (legislation targeted early 2027; National Cabinet Aug 2026) → discretion over drafting scope; energy/water/siting + copyright specified; "AI training" claimed in scope with **no model-safety obligation specified** → window: now–early 2027 → verifier/remedy: none until legislation passes.
- *Testing*: Australian AISI (DISR) → capability/risk analysis, advice to regulators and the public service → window: live since early 2026 → remedy: none observed.

### Inferences
- **Usable obligations today**: yes, but only for government's own AI use (accountability + public transparency register) — a genuine, citable compliance hook for advocates watching government AI deployment. It is a deployment policy, not a procurement framework as such; supplier-facing effects are reported but unverified. For frontier-model safety it creates no obligation on any lab.
- Australia converged with Singapore on the same pattern: the first binding compute-related rules target data centres — energy, water, siting — not AI safety. Neither the DIA nor the announced Australian standards contains model-safety obligations; the PM release's "AI training" reference is unaccompanied by any specified safety duty.
- The 15 Jul 2026 package reverses the Dec 2025 plan's deregulatory drift; the drafting window before early-2027 legislation is the single highest-leverage advocacy moment in either country, and it is dated.

### Gaps
- Full requirements of policy v2.1 (page timed out); the reported Dec 2026 second wave (impact assessments, incident reporting) is blog-reported only.
- The **"Data Centre Expectations"** predecessor document named in the PM release: date, content, binding force — unknown (not located this pass).
- Whether the Australian AISI has any pre-deployment testing role, lab MOUs, or **NAAIMES membership**: not verified.
- National Cabinet August 2026 outcome; Joint Select Committee on AI advice (12 Oct 2026) and report (30 Nov 2026): tracker-reported only.
- What the standards will require of "AI training" (any notification, compute-threshold or safety-testing duty): unspecified in the release; unknown.
- Size of Commonwealth AI procurement spend (to gauge the buyer lever): no data located.

## KQ3: Patterns, what Asia-Pacific can do, and two coalition pilots [Proposals]

### Takeaway
The concrete, enforceable lever both middle powers hold is compute siting/licensing; the safety-specific lever both are building is voluntary, networked testing; and both have dated legislative windows (Nov 2026 – early 2027) where safety provisions could attach. The highest feasibility plays connect the two: make capacity allocation and government contracts conditional on participation in networked pre-deployment safety testing.

### Cited Findings (patterns)
- **Infrastructure first, safety absent**: Singapore's binding law (DIA) covers cloud/DC resilience + sustainability; Australia's announced binding standards cover DC power/water/siting + copyright. Neither contains model-safety obligations. (KQ1/KQ2 sources.)
- **Testing is the most institutionalised, most international instrument**: SG AISI (IMDA+DTC) runs multilingual and agentic evals with Japan, Canada and Korea under NAAIMES, plus ASEAN/AP capacity building (sgaisi.sg, above); Australia stood up its AISI inside DISR in early 2026 (industry.gov.au, above). Both announced/operational within 12 months of each other — institutionalisation is fast.
- **Administrative instruments move fastest**: DTA updated the mandatory policy twice in 15 months with a public register (digital.gov.au, above); Singapore attached negotiated commitments in DC-CFA2 and will hard-wire them as licence conditions under the DIA (EDB/IMDA + D&N, above). Both show obligation-creation without new primary legislation.
- **Calendar convergence**: DIA commencement + first IMDA codes of practice (post-7 Oct 2026) and Australian Standards legislation (target early 2027; National Cabinet Aug 2026; committee report reported due 30 Nov 2026) fall in the same quarters. (SSO/D&N; pm.gov.au; safeaiaus.)

### Inferences — what Asia-Pacific can do (Leverage Map inputs)
1. **Treat capacity allocation and licensing as the safety-conditional hook.** Singapore's CFA rounds and the DIA's flexible licence conditions ("agreed economic or strategic commitments", IMDA-issued codes, penalties up to 10% SG turnover) are a lawful, non-statutory route to attach safety-testing participation to compute access. Safety impact: potentially high (touches the scarce input and the hyperscalers hosting frontier models); feasibility: medium-high (existing discretion; political novelty); clarity: holder (EDB/IMDA) and remedy named.
2. **Make governments the first demanding customers.** Australia's mandatory NCCE policy + public register could add a frontier-model safety clause to procurement; Singapore's procurement rules are the parallel gap to fill (explicitly unknown, KQ1).
3. **Scale networked testing into a shared AP capability.** Joint methodologies (multilingual, agentic — SG's specialisms) and mutual recognition of test reports via NAAIMES, with AIVF AI TAP-accredited testers as a regional service layer. Impact: medium (enforcement-free, but builds the measurement layer and norms); feasibility: high (already operating).
4. **Coordinate calendars.** A simultaneous safety annex in Singapore's DIA codes and Australia's standards legislation would set a two-hub norm that other capacity-constrained AP jurisdictions can copy; leverage via demonstration, not treaty.

### Coalition pilots (both labelled **[Proposal]** — they do not exist; incentives and safety mechanisms stated)

**Pilot A [Proposal]: "AI-ready capacity compact" (Singapore anchor)**
- **Holder**: EDB + IMDA (allocation/licensing); SG AISI (IMDA + DTC) and AIVF accredited testers (AI TAP) as technical verifier.
- **Instrument**: scoring in the next DC-CFA round, plus (post-commencement) DIA DC/major-FDI licence conditions and IMDA codes of practice.
- **Safety mechanism**: applicants for new capacity — and major-FDI cloud providers — earn allocation/facilitation credit by completing **pre-deployment safety evaluations** of the frontier/agentic models they host or operate, using Moonshot/AI Verify methods and accredited testers, with results shared through NAAIMES joint-testing channels.
- **Incentives**: for firms — scarce MW allocation, expedited licensing, "trusted AI hub" positioning; for Singapore — differentiation as the safe place to deploy frontier models in Asia.
- **Verifier/remedy**: SG AISI technical committee + IMDA compliance audits; allocation penalties/conditions now, and post-commencement IMDA directions, financial penalties and critical-IT-load reduction.
- **Window**: next CFA round (timing unknown — flag) and DIA commencement→codes drafting (now–2027).
- **Judging sketch**: safety impact medium-high (reaches hyperscalers at a genuine choke point; participation remains a negotiated condition); feasibility medium-high (no new primary law; uses existing discretion); clarity high except political willingness and CFA-3 timing (unknowns flagged).

**Pilot B [Proposal]: "Frontier procurement clause + AISI pre-deployment test" (Australia anchor)**
- **Holder**: DTA (policy owner), with Office of AI (PM&C) coordination and the Australian AISI as technical verifier.
- **Instrument**: next revision of the Policy for the responsible use of AI in government (v2.2; the cycle is already active — v2.1 published 21 Oct 2026, reported second wave Dec 2026), adding a mandatory clause for NCCE procurement of general-purpose/frontier AI.
- **Safety mechanism**: suppliers must provide pre-deployment safety evaluation reports or AISI testing access before government deployment, disclosed via the public transparency register — filling the gap that the announced 2027 standards do not (so far) specify model-safety duties.
- **Incentives**: for labs — eligibility for Commonwealth contracts and register visibility; for government — adoption confidence aligned with the "AI in Australia's interests" framing.
- **Verifier/remedy**: Australian AISI + DTA assurance, with the public register as an external check; contract remedies/ineligibility through existing government-contracting machinery.
- **Window**: administrative revision cycle now; could ship before legislation (early 2027 target).
- **Judging sketch**: safety impact medium (deployment-scoped, but shapes lab behaviour across a whole-of-government buyer); feasibility high (administrative instrument, updated twice already; no statute needed); clarity high, except that the policy's exact v2.1 text was not retrieved (gap above).

### Gaps (KQ3)
- Not verified this pass: ASEAN-level commitments that could host a coalition (e.g., ASEAN Guide on AI Governance updates); NAAIMES membership beyond the SG/JP/UK/CA/KR exercises observed; **whether Australia participates in NAAIMES**; any Australia–Singapore bilateral AI arrangement (e.g., digital economy agreement AI modules). The Coalition Builder should not assume these exist.
- No quantified government AI spend located for either country to size the buyer lever.
