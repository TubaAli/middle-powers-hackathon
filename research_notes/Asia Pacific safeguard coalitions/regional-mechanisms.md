## KQ1. What ASEAN mechanisms exist (guides, model clauses, DEFA), and are they voluntary or binding?

### Takeaway
ASEAN's AI governance track is **voluntary, ministerially-endorsed guidance** (2024 Guide; 2025 GenAI Expanded Guide) produced by an IMDA-chaired working group, with **no ASEAN "model clauses" for AI** — the regional "model clauses" instrument exists only for cross-border data transfers. The one binding-track instrument, the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA), concluded negotiations in May 2026 and is **not yet signed** (targeted for the 49th ASEAN Summit, November 2026); it reportedly covers AI as a substantive area, but its text was not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics: ASEAN-endorsed framework for organizations designing/deploying "traditional AI" in commercial, non-military applications; focuses on alignment and interoperability of AI frameworks across jurisdictions — [ASEAN, book page (launched at 4th ASEAN Digital Ministers' Meeting, 2 Feb 2024)](https://asean.org/book/asean-guide-on-ai-governance-and-ethics/); [ASEAN PDF, published Feb 2024](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ASEAN-Guide-on-AI-Governance-and-Ethics_beautified_201223_v2.pdf); its status is "guidance — voluntary" for organizations across ASEAN — [AI Policy Tracker (secondary)](https://aipolicytracker.org/policies/asean-guide-on-ai-governance-and-ethics).
- Expanded ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics — Generative AI: supplements the 2024 Guide with GenAI policy considerations; unveiled 17 Jan 2025, developed by the ASEAN Working Group on AI Governance (WG-AI) — [ASEAN PDF, published Jan 2025](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Expanded-ASEAN-Guide-on-AI-Governance-and-Ethics-Generative-AI.pdf); [IMDA WG-AI page](https://www.imda.gov.sg/about-imda/international-relations/asean-working-group-on-ai-governance); status "guidance — voluntary; developers and deployers of generative AI across ASEAN" — [AI Policy Tracker (secondary)](https://aipolicytracker.org/policies/asean-expanded-guide-on-generative-ai).
- The guides were launched at the 4th ADGMIN (Singapore, Feb 2024) and 5th ADGMIN (Thailand, Jan 2025) respectively — [Dataprotection Report (secondary), Feb 2024](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2024/02/article-title-asean-releases-joint-guide-to-asean-model-contractual-clauses-and-eu-standard-contractual-clauses-and-ai-governance-guide/); [Legal 500 (secondary), 22 Jan 2025](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/asean-guide-on-ai-governance-and-ethics-covering-generative-ai-released/).
- **No ASEAN model contractual clauses on AI were found.** The "ASEAN Model Contractual Clauses" (MCCs) are standard contractual terms for cross-border *personal-data* transfers within ASEAN, with a Joint Guide comparing them to EU SCCs — [ASEAN, Joint Guide PDF, Feb 2024](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Joint-Guide-to-ASEAN-Model-Contractual-Clauses-and-EU-Standard-Contractual-Clauses.pdf). Nothing equivalent for AI procurement, testing or incident handling surfaced in any search (2026-10-31).
- ASEAN DEFA: Senior Economic Officials resolved all outstanding issues at the Second Meeting of the 57th SEOM, Manila, 27–29 May 2026 — [SEOM Chairperson Statement, ASEAN, 30 May 2026](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/THE-CHAIRPERSON-OF-THE-ASEAN-SEOM-ON-THE-CONCLUSION-OF-ASEAN-DEFA-NEGOTIATIONS.pdf) (accessed via search-result excerpts; full PDF not retrievable — see Gaps); confirmed by Singapore's trade ministry — [METI Singapore press release, 31 May 2026](https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/singapore-and-asean-member-states-conclude-negotiations-on-landmark-asean-defa/).
- DEFA signature is expected **November 2026** alongside the 28th AEC Council Meeting and the 49th ASEAN Summit (para 12 of the Chairperson Statement); DEFA "breaks new ground in addressing emerging areas such as AI, financial technology, and source code protection" — [SEOM Chairperson Statement (via excerpt)](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/THE-CHAIRPERSON-OF-THE-ASEAN-SEOM-ON-THE-CONCLUSION-OF-ASEAN-DEFA-NEGOTIATIONS.pdf); [Allen & Gledhill (secondary)](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/33129/asean-member-states-conclude-negotiations-on-landmark-digital-economy-framework-agreement); regional media report the agreement covers AI, e-commerce, cybersecurity, digital payments and data governance — [Manila Bulletin (secondary), 31 May 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/05/31/asean-concludes-negotiations-for-landmark-digital-economy-pact).
- DEFA was initiated under Indonesia's 2023 ASEAN chairmanship and is described as the world's first region-wide digital economy agreement — [Indonesian Ministry of Economic Affairs (secondary), 7 May 2026](https://ekon.go.id/publikasi/detail/6918/kesepakatan-asean-defa-digital-economy-framework-agreement-berhasil-diselesaikan-penandatanganan-di-ktt-asean-november-2026); [WEF (secondary)](https://www.weforum.org/stories/economic-growth/asean-defa-digital-economy-pact-negotiations).

### Inferences
- Decision-making: the ASEAN Digital Ministers' Meeting endorses the guides; the WG-AI (chaired by Singapore/IMDA) drafts them. Verification/enforcement: none — these are guidance documents. ASEAN's leverage channel is **diffusion and interoperability**, not compulsion.
- DEFA is the only instrument on a binding treaty track, but as of 2026-10-31 it is **negotiated-not-signed**: no binding obligations exist yet, and the AI provisions' legal character (cooperation clauses vs. enforceable rules) is unverified.
- The MCC precedent shows ASEAN can produce **model clause instruments** when it wants to; AI is the obvious unexploited extension (see Proposals under KQ5).

### Gaps
- DEFA full text is not publicly retrievable with my tools (PDF fetch unsupported; ASEAN statement page redirected). Wording, and whether any AI provisions are enforceable or merely cooperative, is **unverified**.
- Whether the Expanded GenAI Guide contains incident-reporting, model-card or procurement clauses was not verified (document not read in full).
- Korea.net's English press release on the AI Basic Act repeatedly failed to fetch (transport error); Dec-2024 passage facts rest on the MSIT snippet plus law-firm analyses.

## KQ2. What cross-border AISI/evaluation cooperation exists, and is testing "recognition" emerging?

### Takeaway
The International Network of AI Safety Institutes (10 members incl. **Japan, Korea, Singapore, Australia**) has become APAC's most institutionalized voluntary cooperation channel, and Singapore co-leads its Testing & Evaluation track: a running series of **joint testing exercises** with Japan, Korea and the UK (multilingual LLM guardrails 2024–25; agentic evaluations 2025; a Korea–Singapore agent data-leakage test published Jan 2026) plus an industry-facing **Global AI Assurance Pilot**. These produce common *methodologies* and evidence formats, but there is **no legal mutual recognition of test results anywhere in APAC** — outputs are voluntary and non-binding. The OECD has a ready-made common incident reporting framework that no APAC institution has yet formally adopted.

### Cited Findings
- The International Network of AI Safety Institutes launched in San Francisco on 21 Nov 2024 by institutes/offices of **Australia, Canada, European Commission, France, Japan, Kenya, Republic of Korea, Singapore, UK, US**, aiming to "facilitate a common technical understanding of AI safety risks and mitigations" — [Mission Statement PDF, hosted by Japan's AISI, 21 Nov 2024](https://aisi.go.jp/assets/pdf/Mission_Statement_International_Network_of_AI_Safety_Institutes.pdf); [NIST copy](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2024/11/20/Mission%20Statement%20-%20International%20Network%20of%20AISIs.pdf). A 2026 IGF workshop proposal lists the same ten members — [IGF 2026 WS #218](https://media.intgovforum.org/en/content/igf-2026-ws-218-building-best-practices-for-ai-safety-institutes). Taiwan and India are not among the ten.
- Singapore AISI: the Digital Trust Centre (NTU) was designated Singapore's AISI on 22 May 2024 at the AI Seoul Summit, extending AI Verify toward GenAI safety testing — [IMDA factsheet PDF, 22 May 2024](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/news-and-events/media-room/media-releases/2024/05/imda-factsheet--digital-trust-centre-designated-as-spore-ai-safety-institute-22-may-2024.pdf); [SAISI site](https://sgaisi.sg/about-us/).
- At the AI Action Summit (Paris, 10–11 Feb 2025) Singapore announced: (i) the **Global AI Assurance Pilot** (AI Verify Foundation + IMDA) — a testbed convening AI assurance/testing vendors with firms deploying real GenAI applications, to shape future AI assurance standards and grow the third-party assurance market; (ii) a **Joint Testing Report with Japan** under the AISI Network, as **co-lead of the network's Testing and Evaluation Track** — guardrails assessed across **10 languages** (incl. Cantonese, Japanese, Korean, Malay, Mandarin) and 5 harm categories (violent crime, non-violent crime, IP, privacy, jailbreaking); (iii) the Singapore AI Safety Red Teaming Challenge Evaluation Report 2025 (IMDA + Humane Intelligence; Nov 2024 challenge; 50+ participants from 9 APAC countries; tested Aya, Claude, Llama, SEA-LION for cultural bias in non-English languages; data to develop benchmarks and automated testing) — [MDDI press release, 11 Feb 2025](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/singapore-announces-new-ai-safety-initiatives/); [joint testing exercise report PDF](https://sgaisi.sg/publication/data/International-Network-of-AI-Safety-Institutes-Joint-Testing-Exercise-Improving-Methodologies-for-AI-Model-Evaluations-Across-Global-Languages.pdf).
- The joint testing series continued: an evaluation report on "Improving Methodologies for LLM Evaluations Across Global Languages" (June 2025) aims "to seed a common approach for multilingual safety testing of frontier models at scale"; a **3rd Joint Testing Exercise** on agentic evaluations across domains (sensitive-information leak, fraud, cybersecurity threats) with participating AISIs published July 2025; and **Korea and Singapore AISIs jointly tested AI agents for data-leakage risks** in realistic tasks (blogpost, Jan 2026) — [SAISI resources page, accessed 2026-10-31](https://sgaisi.sg/resources/); [3rd exercise report PDF, July 2025](https://sgaisi.sg/wp-api/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/International-Joint-Testing-Exercise_3JT-Eval-Report-v2.pdf); [Korea–SG agent test, Jan 2026](https://sgaisi.sg/resources/testing-ai-agents-for-data-leakage-risks-in-realistic-tasks/); [multilingual evaluation report, June 2025](https://sgaisi.sg/wp-api/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Improving-Methodologies-for-LLM-Evaluations-Across-Global-Languages-Evaluation-Report-1.pdf).
- OECD (Japan, Korea, Australia are members) published **"Towards a common reporting framework for AI incidents"** — a common framework providing "a global benchmark for stakeholders across jurisdictions and sectors" — preceded by "Defining AI incidents and related terms" (2024) and a 2023 stocktaking, and complemented by the live AI Incidents Monitor (AIM) — [OECD.AI incidents portal, accessed 2026-10-31](https://oecd.ai/en/site/incidents/publications); [framework publication page](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/towards-a-common-reporting-framework-for-ai-incidents_f326d4ac-en.html); [definitions report](https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/science-and-technology/defining-ai-incidents-and-related-terms_d1a8d965-en). The OECD also hosts the Japan-origin **Hiroshima AI Reporting Framework** (voluntary transparency/risk-mitigation reports by advanced-AI developers) — [OECD.AI transparency portal](https://oecd.ai/en/transparency/overview).

### Inferences
- Who decides/verifies: the network is a forum of national institutes — it has **no legal authority**; individual institutes decide national adoption. Verification is scientific (methodologies, evaluation reports), not regulatory. Nothing in APAC **enforces** these outputs.
- Partners who gain: Singapore (hub role, standards influence, host of upcoming network-related convening — see KQ5), Japan (multilingual testing serves its language exposure; Hiroshima framework legacy), Korea (AISI Korea co-produces evidence that feeds its new binding act — see KQ3), Australia (network seat with light domestic machinery), smaller ASEAN states (free access to methodologies; no compliance burden).
- Effect on real model risk: shared multilingual/agentic testing reveals failure modes English-centric testing misses (documented in the exercise reports), and common formats cut duplicated effort — but causal effect on frontier-lab behavior is **unproven**; no evidence found that exercise outputs have triggered any regulatory decision.
- A genuine "testing recognition" regime (regulators accepting another jurisdiction's AISI evaluation as compliance evidence) **does not yet exist in APAC**; what exists is converging methodology, a precursor.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of AISI-Network convenings or new outputs at the India summit (Feb 2026) or of network membership changes in 2025–26 (searches surfaced nothing beyond the ten founding members and the joint exercises).
- Whether any APAC regulator has formally adopted the OECD common incident framework — none found; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- China's CnAISDA (launched at Paris, Feb 2025) is adjacent to this scope; its 2026 activity was not researched beyond the IAPS account of its launch (see KQ5 source).

## KQ3. Which binding legal authorities now anchor APAC (Korea, Taiwan), and which institutions decide/verify/enforce?

### Takeaway
As of 2026-10-31 APAC has two binding national statutes: Korea's AI Basic Act (**in force 22 Jan 2026** — the region's first comprehensive AI law with penalties, extraterritorial reach and a compute threshold ≥10^26 FLOPs triggering risk-management duties) and Taiwan's AI Basic Act (**in force 14 Jan 2026** — a state-binding framework law that mandates an internationally *interoperable* risk taxonomy and delegates real rules to sectoral regulators within two years). These are the enforceable anchors that voluntary regional mechanisms currently lack.

### Cited Findings — Korea
- The AI Basic Act (Act on the Development of AI and Establishment of Trust) passed the National Assembly 26 Dec 2024, promulgated Jan 2025, **took effect 22 Jan 2026** with its Enforcement Decree — [MSIT press release, Dec 2024](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mId=4&mPid=2&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1071&searchOpt=ALL); [Cooley alert, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways); official English text — [Korea Law Translation Center](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/eng/engLsSc.do?menuId=2&query=FRAMEWORK%20ACT%20ON%20THE%20DEVELOPMENT%20OF%20ARTIFICIAL%20INTELLIGENCE%20AND%20THE%20CREATION%20OF%20A%20FOUNDATION%20FOR%20TRUST).
- Obligations: AI-content disclosure/labeling (generative and high-impact AI advance notice); **high-impact AI** (healthcare, energy, transport, hiring, biometrics): self-confirmation (MSIT assessment on request), "meaningful explanation", user-protection plan, human oversight, trust/safety documentation, fundamental-rights impact-assessment efforts; **high-performance AI** — systems trained with cumulative compute **≥10^26 FLOPs** (per MSIT Enforcement Decree legislative notice): risk-management and user-protection measures across the lifecycle, **with implementation outcomes reported to MSIT** — [Cooley alert, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways) (linking [MSIT legislative notice](https://www.msit.go.kr/eng/bbs/view.do?sCode=eng&mPid=2&mId=4&bbsSeqNo=42&nttSeqNo=1191)).
- Extraterritorial: applies to foreign AI affecting Korean users/markets; local representative required if prior-year revenue > KRW 1T, AI-service revenue > KRW 10B, or >1M average daily Korean users; the agent is legally responsible for government inquiries and **safety reports** — [Cooley alert, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways).
- Enforcement: MSIT corrective orders incl. service suspension for safety threats; administrative fines up to KRW 30M (~US$21k) for notification failures, missing local representative, violating orders or refusing inspections; **one-year grace period before fines** — [Cooley alert, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways); guidelines first issued Sept 2025 were revised alongside the Act's entry into force — [Kim & Chang (secondary)](https://www.kimchang.com/en/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=34018).
- Institutions created/charged: **National AI Committee** (chaired by the president), **AI Policy Center**, **AI Safety Research Institute** (evaluating AI risk, developing standards); MSIT finalizes enforcement decrees — [Cooley alert, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways).

### Cited Findings — Taiwan
- The Artificial Intelligence Basic Act was passed by the Legislative Yuan on 23 Dec 2025 ([Baker McKenzie (secondary)](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/taiwan-ai-basic-act)), **promulgated 14 Jan 2026 and effective the same day** (Article 20) — [official English text, NSTC Laws and Regulations Retrieving System, accessed 2026-10-31](https://law.nstc.gov.tw/EngLawContent.aspx?id=10099&lan=E).
- Central competent authority: the **National Science and Technology Council (NSTC)** (Art 2). The Executive Yuan must establish a **National AI Strategic Committee** (convened by the Premier; NSTC staff support; meets at least annually; **special meeting "in the event of a sudden emergency or major incident"**) to formulate National AI Development Guidelines (Art 6) — [official text](https://law.nstc.gov.tw/EngLawContent.aspx?id=10099&lan=E).
- High-risk applications may be designated by sectoral central authorities **in consultation with the Ministry of Digital Affairs (moda)**, with advisory notices/warnings; **moda must provide or recommend assessment and verification tools**, developed with input from industry/academia/civil society (Art 5) — [official text](https://law.nstc.gov.tw/EngLawContent.aspx?id=10099&lan=E).
- moda must, **with reference to international standards or norms, promote an AI risk taxonomy and assessment framework "interoperable with international frameworks"**; sectoral authorities must issue risk-based management regulations accordingly; Art 5(1) harms may be restricted or prohibited "in accordance with the law" (Art 16). High-risk AI liability, relief, compensation or insurance mechanisms must be clarified (Art 17, with an R&D exception except real-world testing). All conflicting/missing laws and measures must be enacted or amended **within two years of the effective date** (Art 18). Government AI use requires risk assessments and internal controls (Art 19). Public funding: the government must budget adequately for AI policy (Art 9) and may handle "subsidies, commissions, funding, investments, incentives and guidance" plus tax/financial incentives, with **annual performance reports publicly announced** (Art 10) — [official text](https://law.nstc.gov.tw/EngLawContent.aspx?id=10099&lan=E).
- Scholarly assessment: the Act is a 20-article framework that "binds the state rather than private deployers" — [AI Law Tracker (secondary)](https://ai-law-tracker.com/global/taiwan); analysis in [Nature Humanities & Social Sciences Communications (secondary), 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-07751-6).

### Inferences
- **Voluntary vs binding, stated sharply:** Korea = binding compliance regime with penalties (small fines, but suspension orders are the real teeth); Taiwan = binding on government organs only, with substantive private-sector rules deferred up to two years. ASEAN guides and AISI-network outputs = voluntary.
- Korea's **≥10^26 FLOPs compute threshold** is the first APAC example of tying legal duties to training compute — a concrete, verifiable trigger (operators self-report; MSIT verifies; refusal of inspection is finable). It demonstrates compute-based classification is legislatively feasible; whether it *works* depends on decrees and enforcement not yet observable (fines grace period to ~Jan 2027).
- Taiwan's Art 16 is a coalition-relevant hook: it **legally mandates interoperability with international frameworks** — the natural docking point for AISI-network methodologies or ASEAN guides, decided by moda.
- Neither act creates a cross-border *incident notification* regime: Korea obliges lifecycle risk-management reporting to MSIT (for high-performance AI) and safety-report handling via local representatives; Taiwan creates an emergency committee trigger. EU-style incident-notification law does not yet exist in either.

### Gaps
- Korea: no evidence yet of enforcement practice (Act in force nine months; grace period). Whether Korea's guidelines include a distinct "AI incident" definition/reporting duty was not verified.
- Taiwan: promulgation date appears as 13 Jan 2026 in one secondary source and 14 Jan 2026 in the official law record — I use the official record ([NSTC law page](https://law.nstc.gov.tw/EngLawContent.aspx?id=10099&lan=E)); treat 13 vs 14 Jan as a minor unresolved discrepancy.
- Japan's domestic AI legislation and Japan-AISI's institutional basis (hosting, mandate) were not verified in this pass.

## KQ4. What compute/investment contracting exists (Taiwan, regional development finance), and is any public finance conditioned on safeguards?

### Takeaway
Money is flowing without safety strings. Taiwan is executing a large **public sovereign-AI compute buildout** (NCHC Cloud Computing Center inaugurated Dec 2025 under the "New Ten Major AI Infrastructure Projects", 15 MW now, 23 MW by 2029, plus a planned 120 MW Shalun hub, with NVIDIA/AMD/Foxconn-affiliated partners) — framed as autonomy and resilience, **not conditioned on any model-safety or testing obligation**. The Asian Development Bank launched a 2026–2030 digital transformation strategy with a US$20B "Asia-Pacific Digital Highway", directing help toward connectivity, skills, cybersecurity/privacy, data governance and "responsible" AI use — strategy language, with **no evidence of AI-safety conditions in loan covenants**. "Conditional fresh public finance" for safeguards is an available but unused mechanism in APAC.

### Cited Findings — Taiwan
- NCHC's Cloud Computing Center (Southern Taiwan Science Park, Tainan) was inaugurated **12 Dec 2025**, built under the Executive Yuan's "New Ten Major AI Infrastructure Projects" and the "Greater Southern New Silicon Valley", established under the Forward-looking Infrastructure Development Program; installed power capacity 15 MW; NCHC computing capacity to expand to **23 MW by 2029**; Shalun Intelligent Innovation HUB (Tainan) planned to open in 2029 with up to **120 MW** — [NSTC Chip-based Industrial Innovation Office, 12 Dec 2025](https://cbi.nstc.gov.tw/en/7758ED8D9E3CA37A/5c52e259-429c-4b84-ac2e-6fadf09a0808).
- The **"Super Computing Alliance Taiwan"** was established the same day (NSTC-initiated, NCHC-organized), with the Central Weather Administration, **AMD, Ubilink.AI, WiAdvance, NVIDIA, and Visionbay.ai (Foxconn Technology Group)**, to co-build the compute ecosystem; the center houses the "Nano4 (晶創26)" supercomputer built with domestic manufacturers under the Taiwan Chip-based Industrial Innovation Program; President Lai: "computing power is national power," with a goal of Taiwan as a global AI-innovation hub by 2040 — [NSTC CBI Office, 12 Dec 2025](https://cbi.nstc.gov.tw/en/7758ED8D9E3CA37A/5c52e259-429c-4b84-ac2e-6fadf09a0808).
- NTT and Chunghwa Telecom are to introduce next-generation all-optical networking for the centers; the page stresses energy-efficient systems, earthquake resistance and telecom-grade backup — [NSTC CBI Office, 12 Dec 2025](https://cbi.nstc.gov.tw/en/7758ED8D9E3CA37A/5c52e259-429c-4b84-ac2e-6fadf09a0808).
- Policy framing: Taiwan's sovereign AI rests on "a self-sustaining domestic AI computing environment" and models reflecting Taiwan's linguistic/cultural context — [BowerGroupAsia (secondary)](https://bowergroupasia.com/taiwans-sovereign-ai-push-strategy-policy-programs-and-implications-for-businesses/); a Taiwan minister framed sovereignty as whether "computing infrastructure operates under domestic laws" and whether models reflect a country's perspective — [Taiwan News (secondary)](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/en/news/6410639).
- **No safety-testing, evaluation-reporting or incident-handling conditions were found attached to any of this compute or investment contracting** (all sources above; absence verified only against available text, 2026-10-31).

### Cited Findings — Regional development finance
- ADB approved a new digital transformation strategy, *Digital Transformation for Development: Advancing Inclusion, Security, and Innovation*, announced **18 June 2026**, guiding 2026–2030: expand connectivity, build skills, strengthen cybersecurity and privacy protections, improve data governance, and "use artificial intelligence responsibly"; identify countries' digital-readiness gaps and develop investments and reforms; mobilize financing with governments, companies, partners — [ADB news release, 18 June 2026](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-moves-help-asia-and-pacific-harness-ai-guard-against-risk); [Board Direction document](https://www.adb.org/documents/digital-transformation-development-board-direction).
- The strategy is linked to the **US$20 billion Asia-Pacific Digital Highway** initiative for digital infrastructure, connectivity and access — [ADB news release, 18 June 2026](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-moves-help-asia-and-pacific-harness-ai-guard-against-risk), referencing [ADB's $70B connectivity push](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-launches-70-billion-push-connect-asia-power-grids-digital-networks).
- An ADB regional technical-assistance program ("Advancing AI Readiness through Infrastructure, Solutions, and Enabling Skills") reportedly supports AI readiness across **12 developing economies** — [secondary coverage, Worldngayon, 2026](https://worldngayon.com/adb-ai-readiness-philippines-2026/) (ADB press release not re-verified; treat as lead).
- **No instance found of ADB financing conditioned on AI safety testing, evaluation disclosure, or incident-reporting obligations.** ADB loans are covenant-based and ADB has an accountability mechanism for its safeguards, so conditionality is structurally possible — [ADB news release and site structure, 18 June 2026](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-moves-help-asia-and-pacific-harness-ai-guard-against-risk).

### Inferences
- Decision-holders for conditionality: Taiwan — Executive Yuan/NSTC (Art 9–10 of the AI Basic Act authorize conditional subsidies, investments and tax/financial incentives with public annual performance reporting); Korea — MSIT/National AI Committee (public compute support is already mandated in the Act alongside the 10^26-FLOPs reporting duty, creating a natural conditionality point); ADB — Board of Directors and borrower governments negotiating loan covenants.
- Effect on real model risk: currently ~none from this finance — the buildouts govern **infrastructure** (energy, resilience, connectivity), and green/resilient data-centre rules do not constrain model behavior. Taiwan's hardware position likewise does not confer authority over foreign labs' training runs; jurisdiction reaches what is deployed/operated in Taiwan. Both would change only if contracts or subsidy instruments attached testing/reporting conditions.
- Who gains if conditions were attached: Taiwan gains a way to import safety capacity cheaply (tie NSTC compute access to moda's forthcoming interoperable risk framework); ADB borrowers gain technical assistance; standards-holding states (Singapore, Korea, Japan) gain demand for their evaluation outputs.

### Gaps
- Taiwan's NSTC FY2027 science budget (reported at ~NT$176.8B, +6.2%) rests on a single Chinese-language secondary source — [Mason AI Lab, 15 Jul 2026](https://masonailab.com/insights/taiwan-nstc-ai-budget-sovereign-ai-2026/) — **unverified**.
- US–Taiwan commercial contracting (e.g., TSMC-related deals, NVIDIA compute agreements) was not researched within this bounded scope; no conclusions about it.
- Whether ADB's readiness program includes governance/safety modules could not be verified from primary sources.

## KQ5. Emerging patterns and what APAC middle powers can do (Leverage Map + Coalition Builder)

### Takeaway
The pattern is a **three-speed region**: (1) binding national law in Korea and Taiwan (2026) with real but untested enforcement; (2) operational, APAC-led voluntary evaluation cooperation (AISI-network testing exercises, assurance pilot) that is producing shared evidence formats but no legal recognition; (3) mass public compute/investment finance (Taiwan, ADB, India) with **zero safety conditionality**. Meanwhile the summit series — hosted successively by Korea (2024), France (2025) and India (Feb 2026) — shows **decay of frontier-risk content**: the New Delhi Declaration omits frontier AI entirely, and the Delhi company commitments are weaker than Seoul's. The most promising middle-power play is to make the unused mechanisms real: adopt a common incident evidence format, write pooled procurement clauses, formalize testing recognition, and attach conditions to fresh public finance.

### Inferences — emerging patterns
1. **Voluntary-to-binding migration is happening nationally, not regionally** in APAC: Korea/Taiwan laws in force Jan 2026; ASEAN remains guidance-only; DEFA (signing Nov 2026) is the single regional binding-track instrument and its AI content is unverified.
2. **Evaluation cooperation is APAC's strongest export**: the network's testing track is co-led by Singapore with Japan/Korea as active partners and has shipped successive public methodologies (multilingual → agentic) through 2025–26. This is the region's comparative institutional advantage.
3. **Frontier-risk content is decaying at summit level** (Delhi vs Seoul) while national binding law quietly grows — coalition strategy should therefore route safety demands through national-law interoperability and procurement/finance channels rather than summit declarations.
4. **Finance precedes rules**: Taiwan, ADB and India are deploying very large public compute/infrastructure finance with no safeguard conditions; each has a known decision-holder who could attach them.
5. Discipline warnings (per tasking, and borne out above): Taiwan's semiconductor/compute position does **not** establish veto authority over model developers — its law reaches only what operates in Taiwan; energy-efficient/green data-centre requirements (Taiwan, ADB) are infrastructure rules, not model-safety rules; and broad geographic participation (100+ countries in Delhi; 10 network members; ASEAN consensus) does not itself constitute commitment or enforcement.

### Proposals (explicit, for the Coalition Builder; each names decide/verify/enforce, who gains, effect on model risk, and evidence needed)
- **P1 — Common incident evidence format & notification.** Adopt the OECD common reporting framework and incident definitions as the shared APAC format: Korea (MSIT/AI Safety Research Institute) could align its high-performance-AI reporting and Sept-2025 guidelines to it; Taiwan's moda is *legally mandated* (Art 16) to build an internationally interoperable risk taxonomy — it should reference the OECD definitions; ASEAN's WG-AI could add an incident-reporting annex to the Expanded Guide. Decide: national regulators + ADGMIN. Verify: national institutes (AISI Korea, SAISI, Japan AISI). Enforce: only where national law says so (Korea can; ASEAN cannot). Gains: smaller markets get incident learning at regional scale; labs get one format. Risk effect: faster detection of shared failure modes — but only through mandatory notification somewhere. Evidence needed: any APAC adoption event; EU AI Act Art. 73 practice (Aug 2026 onward) as the first large-scale incident-notification regime to mirror.
- **P2 — Pooled procurement clauses (ASEAN "AI model clauses").** Extend the ASEAN MCC precedent from data to AI: model contractual clauses for government AI procurement requiring vendors to (i) submit evaluation results in a common format (AI Verify / Global AI Assurance Pilot methods), (ii) notify incidents using the OECD taxonomy, (iii) accept post-market testing. Draft via WG-AI + GovTech Singapore + Korea's public-procurement authority; anchor implementation in DEFA's digital-trade machinery once signed (Nov 2026). Decide/verify: ASEAN member procurement agencies. Enforce: contractually (courts/arbitration) — the beauty of procurement is that **binding force comes from contract law, not treaty**. Gains: pooled demand raises vendor compliance costs of refusal across ten markets at once. Evidence needed: whether the DEFA text has any standards/conformity provisions usable as the hook (currently unverifiable — text not public).
- **P3 — Testing recognition.** Formalize what the joint exercises already produce: a mutual-acceptance understanding that an evaluation conducted by one network AISI (e.g., SAISI multilingual/agentic tests, AISI Korea high-performance-AI reviews) is accepted as compliance evidence by MSIT (Korea, which already requires reporting to MSIT), by moda's Taiwan risk framework, and as input to Japan's guidance. Decide: MSIT, moda, METI/CAO Japan. Verify: network institutes (peer review of methods). Enforce: via national filing requirements. Gains: labs face one test rather than many; small states gain influence over what gets tested (e.g., non-English harms). Evidence needed: any regulator crediting a foreign AISI evaluation to date — none found.
- **P4 — Conditional fresh public finance.** Attach safeguards to the money now flowing: Taiwan's NSTC subsidies/investments and National AI Strategic Committee could require Art-16-conformant risk assessments for access to NCHC compute (legal authority already exists in Art 9–10); Korea could link National AI Computing Center access to the Act's 10^26-FLOPs risk-management filings; ADB's Board and borrowers could write AI-governance-readiness covenants (incident reporting participation, evaluation disclosure) into Digital Highway loans under the 2026–2030 strategy. Gains: standards states monetize evaluation capacity; finance states import safety at near-zero cost. Risk effect: this is the mechanism that most directly reaches frontier-scale training, because it conditions scarce compute. Evidence needed: any existing covenant or subsidy decree text (none found as of 2026-10-31).
- **Coalition shape (who should team with whom):** an evaluation cluster (Singapore–Japan–Korea, network-tested since 2024) × a finance cluster (Taiwan, Korea, India — all deploying sovereign compute) × a standards-diffusion channel (ASEAN/DEFA). Singapore's upcoming hosting of the International Scientific Exchange and its co-lead of the network's testing track make it the natural convener; Korea's act is the template to interoperate with; Taiwan contributes compute and a legal interoperability clause but sits outside the AISI network — the proposal channels (P1–P4) are all ones it can join without needing network membership.
