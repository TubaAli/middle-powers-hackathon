How concentrated is each stage?

Concentration is the Herfindahl index (HHI, 0–10,000) over the country groups in the data; above 2,500 is usually called highly concentrated. "Others" and the producer group are treated as many small suppliers, so HHI here is a lower bound. A stage is marked fragile when its top supplier holds more than 60% (the threshold Mapping AI uses for funding dependence).

When are the laws open to change?

Openings in the next nine months where a jurisdiction is writing or revising rules that could carry the six conditions. Lanes for countries in your bloc are highlighted. Hollow markers are rough dates. Dates are openings, not delivery plans (source).

Earliest opening per condition

For each condition, the first date each jurisdiction can act on it. "Bloc's first chance" counts only members of your bloc, plus shared summits.

What this does

It asks one question: if this group of countries acted together, how much of the AI supply chain would it hold, and which safety conditions could that holding back?

Sources

    What this does not establish

    • Shares are rough. Most rows are the source author's own averages over several products; the grid-equipment row is the least certain. Each cell's note says how it was made.
    • Share is not power. A share only becomes leverage if members act together, the input is hard to replace, a body has the legal authority to attach a condition, and members accept the cost. None of that is modelled.
    • The labels are a scenario. "Strong / some / weak supply-chain basis" uses thresholds we chose (50% and 25% of the strongest relevant stage). They are shown so readers can disagree.
    • Replacement times are our guesses. They are not from the source and need checking.
    • Country groups are coarse. Singapore, India, France and others sit in "Others"; the EU's windows are shown under Germany and the Netherlands.
    • Withdrawal is exposure, not loss. The dependence view shows world shares, not who ships to whom: there is no bilateral trade data here, so it cannot say how much any one country would lose. Stockpiles, rerouting, smuggling and substitutes are not modelled.
    • Politics is missing. Coalition incentives, retaliation (e.g., China on gallium) and US pressure on allies are not modelled.
