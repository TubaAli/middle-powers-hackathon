## The inputs support complementary tests, not a leverage percentage

The notes report reading **all three linked pages in full**, including the third page’s HTML and embedded governance records. *Speed limits, not walls* supplies scarcity and substitution questions; its roughly 40% weighted-chain result is a scenario using judgmental weights, not measured bargaining power. *Six safety conditions* supplies concrete demands and decision windows. *Fairness Across Traditions* supplies a disaggregated governance checklist and federated-custody proposal; its legal judgments, cultural scores and simulations do not establish coalition consent or investor control. Together they motivate a chain: **holder → authority → instrument → timing → substitutes → incentive → enforceable demand**. ([Scarcity](https://gszep.com/en/blog/speed-limits-not-walls/#who-holds-leverage-from-mines-to-models); [six conditions](https://gszep.com/en/blog/six-safety-conditions-for-upcoming-laws/#when-the-decisions-are-made); [governance](https://tuba-sds.github.io/fairness-across-traditions/#gov))

Mapping AI contributes evidence-linked discovery and useful interaction patterns. Its inspected funding export contains **1,176 records, only 570 with amounts**, generated 3 May 2026. Its concentration calculation uses **counts of recorded relationships, not dollars**; its “kingmakers” ranking counts unique recipients in selected policy categories. These measures cannot establish national capital shares, budget dependence or withdrawal effects. The export mixes commercial and policy funding and lacks instrument, payment-status and legal-right fields. Its US-centered scope is not US exclusivity. Reuse the evidence-first approach; independently verify each proposed finance edge. ## Circular financing separates money, creditor rights and governance

Supplier investment, lab purchases and infrastructure borrowing are separate transactions. Anthropic’s exhibits document Amazon’s **$20 billion facility ceiling**, Google’s quarterly-draw notes, Broadcom’s secured notes, and Broadcom’s equipment lease to Anthropic-owned AI Infrastructure OpCo II. These establish overlapping supplier, creditor, lessor and shareholder relationships—not cash already received, a net-financing total, or fraud. Broadcom’s $24 billion/$32 billion maturity thresholds describe contract design, not funded balances. A draw schedule identifies something to inspect; **it does not establish a fresh discretionary veto** over a compliant borrower. ([Amazon](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit43-sx1.htm); [Google](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit45-sx1.htm); [Broadcom notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit44-sx1.htm); [lease](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/exhibit1011-sx1.htm))

Draft amendments dated 4 September, 1 October and 9 October were observable at retrieval; their filing dates do not establish when confidential drafts first became public. Phrase-search hits for Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs do **not** verify lead-underwriter roles. The filed current charter gives Class T holders **four of seven board seats from 11 February 2026**. The proposed post-IPO charter preserves the Long-Term Benefit Trust’s board-majority election right and gives Anthropic Founders, LLC’s single Class F share approximately **50.1% of general stockholder voting power**, subject to sunset provisions. Strategic instruments examined settle into non-voting stock; GIC’s Greenview vehicle appears in the investor-rights agreement, but no GIC safety-control right was identified. Existing preferred protective provisions must be distinguished from post-offering rights, not presumed permanent. ## Six demands need different obligors and verification

The following are **negotiating proposals**, not findings of existing contract rights. Their common incentive is reduced losses, clearer acceptance and reliable service; financiers can resist requirements that reduce utilization, and borrowers can seek substitutes. Coalition participation therefore requires testing willingness as well as authority. The six outcomes come from the linked conditions page. ([Conditions and caveats](https://gszep.com/en/blog/six-safety-conditions-for-upcoming-laws/#when-the-decisions-are-made))

| Demand | Minimum credible finance-to-safety link |
|---|---|
| Pre-release testing | Developer signs evaluator-access obligations; covered versions and release milestones defined; financing SPV alone is insufficient. |
| Incident reporting | Developer/operator accepts shared severity definitions, recipients, deadlines, evidence retention and an explicit remedy. |
| Protected weights | Developer/host allocate security and key-custody duties; independent assurance verifies compliance without unnecessarily disclosing weights. |
| Essential-user continuity | Licensed model access, reserved compute, staff/keys and tested failover; escrow alone cannot override export restrictions. |
| Known chip locations | Asset owner/operator supplies inventory and relocation records verified against physical equipment. |
| Local stop with due process | Operator tests scoped isolation/recovery under lawful authority, with appeal and continuity safeguards; loan acceleration is not an off-switch. |

Start with a shared verification schedule across willing procurement and financing counterparties, with separate remedies in each contract. Singapore’s October legislative explanation expressly places assured access beyond the Digital Infrastructure Bill’s scope, so a local resilience obligation cannot be presented as access to foreign model weights. ([Ministerial explanation](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/closing-speech-by-sms-tan-kiat-how-for-the-second-reading-of-the-digital-infrastructure-bill/))

## Build a rights layer, then run three discriminating tests

**Implementation proposal:** add a small, manually curated rights graph alongside the physical stack. Each record should identify holder, target, instrument, financial status, authority evidence, asset jurisdiction, decision window, substitutes, incentive, verifier and remedy. Separate observed rights from proposed conditions; unknown remains unknown. Show evidence-backed paths to each condition rather than dollar-weighted national scores. Preserve distinct rights within a transaction while deduplicating financial amounts by deal. The companion [implementation handoff](Finance%20layer%20handoff.md) specifies fields and proposed DESIGN corrections; no application changes are included.

**Test 1—credit authority:** extract Anthropic’s complete amendment/draw provisions and commitment schedule. Can willing named lenders meet the applicable threshold, and does a future decision genuinely allow negotiated safety reporting? This tests the most concrete newly identified contract channel. **Test 2—Canadian discretion:** obtain Cohere’s subscription/stage agreement, named signatory and next decision date. Does CAISI cooperation have an enforceable deliverable, and can replacement finance erase the bargaining opportunity? **Test 3—procurement feasibility:** inspect EuroHPC’s tender pack and contracting authorities. Can a common six-condition assurance annex lawfully fit before award, with developer flow-down and practical verification? These tests arise respectively from the linked credit agreement, Canadian release and EuroHPC call above.

**Limits:** the notes inspected the S-1 index and more than twenty exhibits in full or identified part—not the complete 14 MB prospectus. Ownership tables, underwriting section, financial statements and major cloud-purchase obligations remain incompletely examined. Several amounts and commitments are redacted; no cash reconciliation or comprehensive finance denominator exists. Temasek’s charter was inaccessible, private agreements remain unavailable, and the blogs’ bibliographies were not fully audited. No existing six-condition package or institutional consent was established.

## Conclusion

The useful demonstration is a traceable route from one institution’s real discretion to one verified safety deliverable. If the first three tests fail, the map should display precisely why—closed window, insufficient authority, replaceable capital or missing developer obligation. That makes it a negotiation instrument for middle powers rather than a visualization of presumed power.
