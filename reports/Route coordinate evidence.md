# Recovered evidence sharpens thirty-two safeguard routes

**Evidence cutoff: 31 October 2026. Target: Geneva AI Summit, 21–22 June 2027—233 days to its opening.** This report synthesises the supplied investigations and checks their saved data; it adds no new research. [Official summit dates](https://www.bakom.admin.ch/en/geneva-ai-summit-2027-international-summit-on-artificial-intelligence-in-geneva).

## Executive summary

**There is substantially more measurable evidence than the earlier access failures suggested.** India has 303 identifiable allocation records; Kenya's controlling statute and historical review counts are recovered; Japan has actual procurement awards, trial contracts and a documented release-approval role; Taiwan has a recoverable 2026 recipient inventory; and Anthropic's original and restated credit amounts and June utilisation are disclosed. Cross-country procurement and administrative datasets have also been downloaded and inspected. These discoveries establish real institutional mechanisms, historical activity and narrower populations for follow-up. **They do not establish a measured feasibility probability or consequential-safety-reach coordinate for any of the 32 proposed routes.** The principal missing link is now specific: connect an authorised, exact safeguard and its decision path to identifiable operational activity, then observe implementation and the relevant outcome. No estimated scores replace that missing evidence here.

The checked inventory contains **379 route fact records, of which 305 have numeric scalar `value` fields; 242 source records; and 193 grouped retrieval records**. These are not 379 independent observations, 305 independent measurements, 242 unique sources or 193 successful searches. Compound numerical values and three decimal-string money values are outside the 305 scalar count. Sources and evidence repeat across routes; retrieval records can contain multiple attempts and failures. The separate methodology file's 14 findings are not included in the 379 route facts. [Reproducibility and file inventory](#reproduce-the-findings-without-retrieving-new-data).

### Credit disclosure resolves amounts, not discretionary safety leverage

**Anthropic's aggregate facility size was never redacted in the retrieved agreement:** §1.01 states **$15 billion at the 4 September 2026 restatement**. The recovered S-1 describes the original **$2.5 billion, 14 May 2025 facility**, increased to $2.75 billion in September and $3.25 billion in November 2025. At **30 June 2026**, there were zero cash borrowings and **$78 million outstanding standby letters of credit** under that $3.25 billion facility. Thus **$3.172 billion / $3.25 billion = 97.6%** was unutilised by those disclosed uses. June utilisation cannot be divided by September's $15 billion commitment. Unused committed credit is not discretionary new lender capital. [Agreement §1.01 and recitals][fin-src-credit]; [S-1 MD&A pp.163–164, Note 9 pp.F-44–45][fin-src-prospectus].

The same S-1 has **blank September 30 liquidity/availability placeholders** at MD&A p.139, while Schedule 2.01 lender allocations remain redacted. These are observed limits of the public disclosure, unlike the solved 403/size-limit extraction failures. The contract's **strictly greater-than-50% Required Lenders** threshold is real, but weights, applicable exceptions, borrower agreement and a genuinely negotiable new-credit event are still needed. Its **160-day annual financial reporting cadence** is not safety reporting. [S-1 p.139 and exhibit omission legend][fin-src-prospectus]; [agreement §§5.04,9.08 and Schedule 2.01][fin-src-credit].

Canada likewise has two distinct instruments: the **$240 million partially conditionally repayable contribution** toward a $725 million project, and the **29 October 2026 equity announcement of up to $1.6 billion**. The recovered parliamentary response confirms the historical signed contribution; it predates the equity announcement. The **33.10% contribution/project-cost ratio** is a matched announced financing ratio, not October equity control or safety coverage. ## What still requires retrieval, and what requires a decision

**Access failure, non-disclosure and future evidence are different states.** Rwanda's form, Malaysia's annual report, Mexico's complete historical case table/manual, NESO originals and Chile's transmitted text remain access-limited. A failed URL does not show that their target data are absent. Taiwan's read award attachments lack resource quantities; Anthropic's read September fields are blank and lender allocations redacted. Q-935 contains explicit withholding by some departments and compilation limitations in another, so ISED's contribution cannot be treated as a complete government-wide relationship census. These are substantive disclosure limits, not a general finding that the needed facts are unobtainable. [Regional retrieval ledgers below](#reproduce-the-findings-without-retrieving-new-data); [Taiwan attachments][ap-s-final-tw-pdf2628]; [S-1][fin-src-prospectus]; [Q-935 full replies][fin-src-parliament-all].

**Future exact consent is not a public datum that necessarily exists yet.** A presently executed acceptance can be retrieved; a future negotiation outcome must occur before it can be observed. The investigations identify authority, historical choices and generic policy support, but no complete set of valid exact-proposal approvals. That does not mean refusal. Nor does every stakeholder require consent: an authorised regulator can act without a regulated entity's voluntary approval, while access beyond its powers can require a separate agreement. The institutional graph must distinguish legal vetoes, operational dependencies, optional advisers and internal handoffs.

Three scope decisions remain even after perfect retrieval. First, define x's event consistently: **enforceable operational safeguard by the deadline**, rather than mixing consultation submission, signed terms, laboratory demonstration and live operation. Approval coverage can be reported as a separate descriptive metric once the complete required set exists; it is not a probability. A forecast requires comparable historical attempts, current stage/age, failures and withdrawals, actual operational dates and held-out calibration. The existing dataset's handoff penalty is explicitly a modelling choice, not such calibration. [Current definition](../DESIGN.md); [measurement investigation](../research_notes/Route%20coordinate%20evidence/measurement.json).

Second, choose **reach or impact** for y. Reach needs a fixed eligible population, same-period activity units, verified binding scope, working evidence/remedy and overlap handling. A local pilot's 100% coverage is not worldwide 100%. Impact additionally needs an outcome-specific counterfactual and causal change in failure/harm, including severity, displacement, false refusals and costs. Privacy, grid reliability and catastrophic misuse are not interchangeable outcomes. Their common valuation is a substantive choice, not a missing webpage. Third, fix the deadline boundary/timezone and the reporting horizon: the summit is a checkpoint, not proof of operational readiness or a natural end of harm measurement. [Measurement protocol](../research_notes/Route%20coordinate%20evidence/measurement.md); [earlier validation design](../research_notes/Safeguard%20validation%20by%20Geneva/measurement.md).
