# Deeper evidence challenges apparent safeguard leaders

**Observed 31 October 2026; decision checkpoint 21–22 June 2027.** The deeper evidence makes Japan a defensible **next validation candidate**, but does not establish an India/Japan advantage in delivered safety. India's documented commercial-continuation path challenges the assumption that stopping subsidy stops a workload. Japan has an identifiable release process, yet supplier evidence, behavioural hold authority, independent review capacity and useful fallback remain unproved for the proposed safeguard. A common LUMI service condition could avoid repeated national policy changes; Brazil supplies a concrete failed-assurance case that deserves priority under a direct-rights objective. **Recommendation: first test Japan's authority, amendability and marginal cost; retain Brazil and matched LUMI as explicit competing validation priorities.** This is a value-of-information judgment, not policy adoption, a country ranking or a vote among models. The [audit](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/model-audit.md), [India evidence](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/compute.md), [Japan evidence](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/japan-asia.md) and [rights evidence](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/rights-enforcement.md) support those distinctions.

## The plotted advantage depends on preferences and untested mechanisms

**Audit result, not empirical ranking:** production is a two-dimensional editorial plot. Only the audit's extra assumption, **U = .5x + .5y**, makes Japan and Switzerland joint first, Singapore's leakage gate third and India fourth. Six routes are non-dominated, including India, Japan, Singapore, Switzerland, EuroHPC and the Anthropic lending route. India dominates the three routes labelled “middle-power” at baseline; Japan does not dominate the Japan–Korea–Singapore procurement profile, whose impact coordinate is slightly higher. Neither dominates EuroHPC or Anthropic. The selected groups—26 national, three middle-power and three broader routes—are unmatched, so their comparison cannot identify a coalition effect. ([Reproducible results](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/model-audit.json), `baseline`, `summary.groups`.)

Japan's feasibility lead over its procurement coalition is .340: .100 readiness, .090 timing and .150 coordination. The coalition gains only .015 on impact after its greater reach is offset partly by imputed remedy. This embeds judgments about a particular implementation design, not a measured cost of cooperation. With baseline axis weights but feasibility preference α=.25, India and EuroHPC instead tie first; India overtakes Japan below α≈.325581. Across the five equal-axis regimes at prior .5, Japan remains above India but loses Pareto status in three; India loses it in the rights/accountability proxy. Proxy weights cannot create missing international-spillover or frontier-specific-effect evidence. ([Audit](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/model-audit.md), pair decompositions and sensitivity sections.)

The audit contains **15 regime/prior combinations, 45 α evaluations, 5,760 one-at-a-time probes and three removal probes**; the 45 include the α=.5 cases, not 45 additional independent experiments. There are 23 missing factors among 192 entries across 14 routes. Changing their common prior from .5 to .25 or .75 yields 48 or 83 strict pair reversals at baseline weights. Under the documented independent sensitivity boxes, **zero dominance relationships are robust**. Japan and India each attain best rank 1 and worst rank 32 at opposite box extremes; this does not enumerate every intermediate rank or assign probabilities. Their narrower own-input-only rank ranges are 1–3 and 3–4. Eight of 24 jointly assessed readiness/remedy pairs are equal, with correlation .698668: shared maturity evidence merits checking, but universal duplication is false. No scores were updated. ([Audit executable](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/reproduce-model-audit.py); [results](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/model-audit.json), `structure`, `scenarios`, `ablations`.)

**Fact:** E2E describes IndiaAI services continuing at commercial prices after project expiry. That is ordinary operation at one provider, not observed safety evasion, but it separates the funding switch from the service switch. Policy audit/revocation provisions do not supply the missing signed safety-specific flow-down; quarterly subsidy settlement is different from allocation. The prior 303-project inventory remains useful without becoming consumed hours or paid support. **Inference:** test prospective public-support conditionality before promising a model-release veto. Provider terms, reservation costs, alternative funding and post-tuning behaviour matter more than another fleet total. ([E2E][compute-e2e], Billing/Platform Service; [policy][compute-policy], §§4.6–9; [prior report](Route%20coordinate%20evidence.md#india-allocations-are-visible-consumed-compute-and-payments-remain-different-questions).)

**Fact:** Gen-nai requires authorised production-change approval and another developer's review—not external independent certification. DS-920 includes independent testing among example methods, not a universally signed certificate requirement. Graffer and Rakuten's submissions identify inaccessible upstream information; government replies allow reasonable negotiated disclosure while retaining demanding expectations around upstream changes. **Inference:** Japan's advantage is a concrete place to test authority, not proof of deliverable independent assurance. Its strongest objection is additionality: fully implemented existing controls might already prevent the selected failure. ([Standard][asia-jp-standard], pp.8–9,36–40; [guideline][asia-jp-guideline], §§6.3.2–6.3.3; [responses][asia-jp-comments], pp.38,46–47,69.)

## Deeper retrieval changes the mechanism, not just confidence

**Fact:** LUMI's common terms name CSC, permit supplementary terms and apply common utilisation rules across allocation channels; content access remains purpose-limited and internal safety-amendment delegation is unrecovered. **Inference:** compare national award processing with a common-host implementation at equal safeguard strength. Belgium's allocation is nested within the national envelope; neither is protected consumption. International testing reports document useful debugging and multilingual contributions, but also judge/environment defects. Reuse can save work or propagate a shared error; neither outcome follows from consensus. ([LUMI terms][euro-lumi-terms], preamble, §§6,10–12; [2024 exercise][euro-joint-pilot], Finding 1; [2025 exercise][euro-joint-agents], methodological limitations.)

**Attributed findings:** Brazil's 11 February 2026 account reports persistent Grok failures and a verifiable-evidence demand in file 00261.000178/2026-27. Colombia's 8 July account describes a final administrative obligation for externally certified deletion with technical evidence. Katiba reports completed court-supervised deletion in Kenya. These are respectively a reported failed assurance/order, a corrective obligation, and a litigant's completion report—not independently verified persistent remedies. Grok is not the original football-club cohort; Colombia's biometric case is not demonstrated use of the proposed AI retest annex. **Value choice:** direct protection and redress deserve visibility alongside systemic risk, without automatically treating a rights instrument as effective. ([ANPD][rights-br-grok], opening/ANPD; [SIC][rights-co-final], technical section/conclusion; [Katiba][rights-ke-katiba], opening/judgment account.)

Other discoveries sharpen boundaries. BPOM's MORADA identifies an actual AI-assisted specification workflow; its price check is not an AI-safety hold. Thailand's recovered contract buys capacity-building, not participant release authority. SDAIA's named AI graduates are a separate Saudi programme and cannot upgrade CST. Malaysia's ADMP guidance and an original lawyer submission make the distinction between standard-form buyers and negotiated clauses practical. Finance still requires a genuinely discretionary event: Canada's speech suggests terms already agreed, while Anthropic's agreement contains instrument-specific amendment exceptions and disclosure carve-outs. Headquarters, unused credit and announced stages do not establish state-controlled safety rights. ([BPOM][sandbox-id-morada], SSI/APD; [Thai TOR][sandbox-th-tender], §§4.3–4.7; [SDAIA][sandbox-sa-sdaia], graduates/FAQ; [Malaysia annex][sandbox-my-clauses], pp.1–5; [speech][euro-canada-speech], agreement passages; [credit][euro-credit], §§2.21–2.23,5.04,9.08.)

## The lenses sharpen tests without outperforming the baseline by fiat

The plain baseline and implementation/frontier lenses choose Japan; rights chooses Brazil; international chooses matched Belgian/common LUMI. These are different explicit objectives, **not votes that establish truth**. Rights prioritises correction of an evidenced injury; international prioritises learning whether delegated shared administration adds distinct protected work. Implementation values executable, affordable delivery; frontier demands additional prevention of a specified serious failure and rejects calling a local success catastrophic-risk reduction. All have defensible objections to their own choice. ([Baseline](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/baseline.md); [implementation](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/lens-implementation.md); [frontier](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/lens-frontier.md); [rights](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/lens-rights.md); [international](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/lens-international.md).)

The baseline already supplies Japan's retirement rehearsal, confidential receipts, matched LUMI, Brazil's minority case, affordability, useful fallback, substitution and additionality. **The lenses introduce no new retrieval or newly verified external fact.** Their useful incremental detail is a two-supplier cost/exclusion test; a fully compliant ordinary-control comparator with adaptive tests and task-family uncertainty; an end-to-end contest/correction journey; and counterbalanced two-channel review with channel-shopping and non-recognition records. These are refinements, not proof of panel superiority. The implementation proposal's 90%-of-baseline utility floor and frontier's five-percentage-point loss limit are not equivalent; neither is adopted here as a universal threshold. No blind human quality judgment or measured effort comparison ran. The baseline remains the concise decision spine; [point coverage](../research_notes/Safeguard%20proposal%20stress%20tests/coverage.json) retains all 61 points, including contextual material omitted from this summary.

## Conclusion

The useful unit of leverage is a decision that changes an operation, not a national asset, programme label or impressive allocation. The next discriminating evidence is an authorised failed-test decision joined to a real configuration and meaningful activity, with repair, usable fallback and measured burden. If that evidence favours Brazil or a shared LUMI condition before Japan qualifies, the validation priority should change. If ordinary controls already work, implementing them is a success—and a reason to abandon an unnecessary new layer.
